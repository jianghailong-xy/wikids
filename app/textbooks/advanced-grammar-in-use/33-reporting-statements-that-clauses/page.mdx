When we report what someone said, the reported clause is very often a **that-clause** (see Unit 32). The clause itself is easy; what trips advanced learners up is everything that stands *between* the reporting verb and it — whether **that** can be dropped, whether an object is compulsory, optional or forbidden, and which **preposition** that object needs. This unit sorts the reporting verbs into those patterns.

<SayIt text="He said (that) he was enjoying his work." />
<br />
<SayIt text="The members of the Security Council warned that further action may be taken." />

## A — Leaving out *that* (and when you can't)

After the more common reporting verbs — **agree, mention, notice, promise, say** and **think** — we often leave **that** out, particularly in informal speech. However, it is **not usually left out**:

- after **less common** reporting verbs such as **complain, confide, deny, grumble, speculate, warn** (and after the common reporting verbs **answer, argue** and **reply**)
- in **formal writing**
- if the **that-clause doesn't immediately follow the verb**

<SayIt text="She agreed with her parents and brothers that it would be safer to buy a car than a motorbike." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Something intervenes between <em>agreed</em> and the clause, so <strong>that</strong> has to stay — <span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>She agreed with her parents and brothers it would be safer …</em>)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="The trap: distance forces *that*">
  The longer the gap between the reporting verb and the reported clause, the more the reader needs <strong>that</strong> as a signpost. With an adverbial, a prepositional phrase or a long object in the way, keeping <strong>that</strong> is not a stylistic nicety — it is what stops the reader parsing the next noun as the verb's object.
</Callout>

## B — A that-clause or an object + to-infinitive

Some reporting verbs followed by a **that-clause** have an alternative with an **object + to-infinitive** (often *to be*), although the alternatives are often rather **formal**. Compare:

<SayIt text="I felt that the results were satisfactory." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I felt the results to be satisfactory." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Same meaning — the second is distinctly more formal.)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-amber-200 bg-amber-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-amber-700">Also take object + to-infinitive</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-amber-900">acknowledge · assume · believe · consider · declare · expect · find · presume · report · think · understand</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-amber-800">Verbs outside this group — <em>argue, comment, stress, say, complain</em> — take the <strong>that-clause only</strong>: <span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>She stressed her stories to be aimed at children.</em></p>
</div>

## C — Verbs that must have an object before the that-clause

If we use a **that-clause** after an **active** form of some verbs, we **must** include an object between the verb and the that-clause. This object **can't** be a prepositional object (see D below):

<SayIt text="I notified the bank that I had changed my address." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>I notified that I …</em> / <span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>I notified to the bank that I …</em>)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-rose-200 bg-rose-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-rose-700">Object obligatory — and no preposition</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-rose-900">assure · convince · inform · notify · persuade · reassure · remind · tell</p>
</div>

However, after some other verbs an object before a that-clause is **not always necessary**:

<SayIt text="They promised (me) that they would come to the party." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">Object optional — still no preposition</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">advise · promise · show · teach · warn</p>
</div>

<Callout type="info" title="Why this decides Exercise 33.1">
  Whenever a sentence has <strong>no</strong> object after the reporting verb, a verb from the obligatory group (<em>tell, remind, persuade, inform, notify</em>) is simply ungrammatical: <em>Katarina told that she would be home</em> ✗ → <em>Katarina promised that she would be home</em> ✓. When an object <strong>is</strong> present, verbs from both groups usually work.
</Callout>

## D — Optional object — but put a preposition before it

After some verbs we use a **that-clause** with or without a **personal object** before the that-clause. However, if we **do** include an object, we put a **preposition** before it.

**With *to*:**

<SayIt text="She admitted (to me) that she was seriously ill." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I pointed out (to the driver) that he had parked across the entrance." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-sky-200 bg-sky-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-700">verb + to + object + that-clause</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-sky-900">admit · announce · complain · confess · explain · indicate · mention · point out · propose · recommend · report · say · suggest</p>
</div>

**With *with*:**

<SayIt text="We agreed (with Ella) that the information should go no further." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-violet-200 bg-violet-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-violet-700">verb + with + object + that-clause</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-violet-900">agree · argue · check · disagree · joke</p>
</div>

**With *of*** — after the verbs **ask, demand** and **require**:

<SayIt text="The club asks (of its members) that they pay their fees by 31st December." />
<br />
<SayIt text="The company demands (of its staff) that they should be at work by 8:30." />

This pattern is usually used in **formal contexts**. Less formally we can use a **to-infinitive clause** after **ask** and **require** — *The club asks its members to pay their fees by 31st December*. However, we **can't** use a to-infinitive clause after **demand**.

<Callout type="warning" title="demand takes no to-infinitive">
  <span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>The company demands its staff to be at work by 8:30.</em><br />
  ✓ <em>The company demands of its staff that they should be at work by 8:30.</em> &nbsp;·&nbsp; ✓ <em>The company demands that its staff should be at work by 8:30.</em>
</Callout>

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-slate-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-slate-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-slate-900">Pattern</div>
    <div className="bg-slate-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-slate-900">Example</div>
    <div className="border-t border-slate-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">verb + <strong>object</strong> + that (object required)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-slate-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>I notified <strong>the bank</strong> that …</em></div>
    <div className="border-t border-slate-100 bg-slate-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">verb + (object) + that (object optional, no preposition)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-slate-100 bg-slate-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>They promised <strong>(me)</strong> that …</em></div>
    <div className="border-t border-slate-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">verb + <strong>to</strong> + object + that</div>
    <div className="border-t border-slate-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>She admitted <strong>to me</strong> that …</em></div>
    <div className="border-t border-slate-100 bg-slate-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">verb + <strong>with</strong> + object + that</div>
    <div className="border-t border-slate-100 bg-slate-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>We agreed <strong>with Ella</strong> that …</em></div>
    <div className="border-t border-slate-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">verb + <strong>of</strong> + object + that (formal)</div>

  </div>

<Callout type="tip" title="Take it further">
  Take five reporting verbs you use most in your own writing and file each one into a frame: <em>bare object required</em>, <em>bare object optional</em>, <em>to</em>, <em>with</em>, or <em>of</em>. The verbs that cause real trouble are the near-synonyms that sit in different boxes — <em>tell</em> (bare object) against <em>say</em> (<em>to</em>), <em>inform</em> (bare object) against <em>explain</em> (<em>to</em>). Getting those four verbs right removes most reported-speech errors at C1.
</Callout>
