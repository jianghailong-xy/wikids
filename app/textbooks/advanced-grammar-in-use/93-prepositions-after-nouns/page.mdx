<Lesson textbook="advanced-grammar-in-use" slug="93-prepositions-after-nouns">

Many nouns, especially abstract ones, are completed by a preposition before the thing they relate to: *admiration **for** courage*, *a ban **on** smoking*, *the cost **of** living*. The trouble is that the preposition a noun chooses is rarely something you can reason out — it is fixed, and it is not always the one its related verb or adjective takes. This unit sorts the patterns into four groups: when a noun copies the preposition of its related word and when it doesn't (**A**), when a noun is followed by *of + -ing* or by a *to*-infinitive (**B**), the nouns that allow either with a similar — or a different — meaning (**C**), and the special behaviour of *intention* (**D**).

## A — Nouns and the prepositions of their related words

Many nouns are followed by the **same preposition** as their related adjective or verb. Compare:

<SayIt text="Are you satisfied with the way the business is being run?" />
<br />
<SayIt text="The shareholders have expressed satisfaction with the way the business is being run." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(*satisfied with* → *satisfaction with* — the preposition carries over.)</p>

A **few** nouns, however, take a **different** preposition from their related word:

<SayIt text="They became fond of each other at school." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Their fondness for each other grew, and many years later they married." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(*fond **of*** but *fondness **for***.)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="Adjective preposition ≠ noun preposition">
  A small set switches preposition when it turns from adjective to noun: <strong>fond of → fondness for</strong>, <strong>proud of → pride in</strong>, <strong>ashamed of → shame about / at</strong>. Don't assume the noun keeps the adjective's preposition — check these individually.
</Callout>

Some nouns take a preposition where their related **verb takes none** (a verb with a direct object becomes a noun + *of* / *for* / etc.):

<SayIt text="I respect Louis enormously." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I have enormous respect for Louis." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(The verb *respect* needs no preposition; the noun *respect* needs *for*.)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">Nouns that need a preposition their verb doesn't</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">admiration for · ban on · discussion about / on · improvement in · influence on · interview with · lack of · respect for</p>
</div>

Finally, note that a great many other nouns are commonly followed by **of-phrases** that show possession, a property, or what the noun classifies or relates to:

<SayIt text="He described the conductor as moving his arms like a windmill." />
<br />
<SayIt text="His description of the conductor was very funny." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(The verb *describe* takes a direct object; the noun *description* takes *of*.)</p>

## B — Noun + *of + -ing* vs noun + *to*-infinitive

Some nouns can be followed by ***of + -ing*** but **not** usually by a *to*-infinitive:

<SayIt text="He's got into the habit of biting his nails when he's nervous." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(*habit of biting* — not *habit to bite*.)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-sky-200 bg-sky-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-700">Take *of + -ing* (not a *to*-infinitive)</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-sky-900">cost · effect · fear · habit · likelihood · possibility · probability · problem · prospect · risk · sign</p>
</div>

Other nouns can be followed by a ***to*-infinitive** but **not** usually by *of + -ing*:

<SayIt text="His unhappy childhood explains his reluctance to talk about his parents." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(*reluctance to talk* — not *reluctance of talking*.)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-violet-200 bg-violet-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-violet-700">Take a *to*-infinitive (not *of + -ing*)</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-violet-900">ability · attempt · concern · decision · desire · determination · failure · inability · permission · proposal · reason · refusal · reluctance · (un)willingness</p>
</div>

<Callout type="info" title="A third option: other preposition + -ing">
  Some of these same nouns also combine with <em>another</em> preposition + <em>-ing</em>, with its own meaning: <em>an <strong>attempt at</strong> climbing the wall</em>, <em>a <strong>reason for</strong> leaving early</em>. So one noun can sit in more than one of these patterns — the structure you choose carries part of the meaning.
</Callout>

## C — Nouns that allow either form

Some nouns can be followed by **either** *of + -ing* **or** a *to*-infinitive with a **similar** meaning, usually after *the*:

<SayIt text="Do staff have the opportunity of taking unpaid leave?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or *the opportunity **to take*** unpaid leave — same meaning.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="The aim of providing clean drinking water has been achieved." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or *The aim **to provide*** clean drinking water …)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-teal-200 bg-teal-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-teal-700">Either form, similar meaning</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-teal-900">aim · ambition · idea · opportunity · option · plan</p>
</div>

But some nouns — notably **chance**, **sense** and **way** — have **more than one meaning**, and the form you choose depends on which meaning you intend:

<SayIt text="What's the chance of getting five heads when you toss a coin five times?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(*chance* = likelihood → *of + -ing*, not *chance to get*.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Will you get the chance to visit Miki in Japan?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(*chance* = opportunity → *to*-infinitive, not *of visiting*.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="He didn't have the sense to move away from the puddle as the bus went past." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(*sense* = good judgement → *to*-infinitive, not *sense of moving*.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="She had a sense of belonging within a few days of starting at her new school." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(*sense* = feeling → *of + -ing*, not *sense to belong*.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="I've got a new way to cook rice." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(*way* = method → either *to cook* **or** *of cooking*.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="She has a really funny way of speaking." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(*way* = manner → *of + -ing* only, not *way to speak*.)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="Let the meaning pick the form">
  <strong>chance</strong> = likelihood → <em>of + -ing</em>; = opportunity → <em>to</em>-infinitive.<br />
  <strong>sense</strong> = good judgement → <em>to</em>-infinitive; = feeling → <em>of + -ing</em>.<br />
  <strong>way</strong> = method → either; = manner → <em>of + -ing</em> only.
</Callout>

## D — *intention*: *of + -ing* after *no / every / the slightest*

After a small set of quantifying words — **no**, **every**, **the slightest**, **(not) any**, **with the** — the noun **intention** is followed by ***of + -ing***, **not** a *to*-infinitive:

<SayIt text="I have no intention of lending Dan any more money." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(not *no intention **to lend*** …)</p>

In **most other cases**, though, *intention* can take **either** form:

<SayIt text="He announced his intention to stand in the election." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or *his intention **of standing*** in the election — both natural.)</p>
