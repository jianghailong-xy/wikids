Most *wh*-questions look simple, but the advanced choices sit in the details: **who or whom**, **who or which**, **how or what**, and the traps around **whose**. This unit sorts out when each is right — including the formal patterns with a fronted preposition, the singular verb after a subject *who / what*, and the difference between *whose* (the possessive) and *who's* (= *who is*) that even fluent writers slip on.

## A — Who, whom and which for people

**Who** refers to people, and can work as **subject**, **object** or **complement**:

<SayIt text="Who owns that car?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<em>who</em> = subject of <em>owns</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Who did you meet?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<em>who</em> = object of <em>meet</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Who was her father?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<em>who</em> = complement of <em>was</em>)</p>

**Whom** is a formal alternative to *who* **as object**, and is used **directly after a preposition**:

<SayIt text="Whom did you meet?" />
<br />
<SayIt text="To whom were you talking?" />

**Which** picks out a person from within a **group** — for instance when we can see the people we are talking about:

<SayIt text="'Which is your brother?' 'The one next to Luka.'" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(looking at a photograph — identifying one of several)</p>

We can also use **which** instead of *who* to ask about **particular classes of people**:

<SayIt text="Which do you think earns more, a teacher or a police officer?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or <em>Who do you think ...?</em>)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="Whom is never a subject">
  Use <strong>whom</strong> only where <em>who</em> is the <strong>object</strong> or comes <strong>after a preposition</strong> — and only in formal style. It can <span className="text-rose-600">never</span> be the subject: say <em>Who translated the book?</em>, not <em>Whom translated the book?</em> After a fronted preposition, though, <em>whom</em> is obligatory: <em>To whom should I write?</em> (informally we split it off: <em>Who should I write to?</em>).
</Callout>

## B — Which before *one(s)* and *of*

Before **one(s)** and **of**, we normally use **which** rather than *who* or *what*, because these questions ask about a **choice** between things or people:

<SayIt text="I've decided to buy one of these jumpers. Which one do you think I should choose?" />
<br />
<SayIt text="Which of you would like to go first?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(rather than <em>Who of you ...?</em>)</p>

## C — Singular verb after a subject *who / what*

When **who** or **what** is the **subject**, the following verb is usually **singular**, even when we expect a plural answer:

<SayIt text="What is there to see in the town?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(expecting a list of things — <span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>What are there to see ...?</em>)</p>

The verb **can be plural**, however, in **echo questions** after a plural subject, or a subject made of two or more noun phrases joined by *and* — and when *who / what* is a **complement**, not the subject:

<SayIt text="'Mr Almeida and his family are here to see you.' 'Who are here?'" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(echoing a plural subject — or <em>Who's here?</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Who are those people over there?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<em>who</em> = complement, so the plural <em>are</em> agrees with <em>those people</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="What are the consequences of the decision?" />

<Callout type="info" title="Echo questions come back in Unit 27">
  The plural echo (<em>Who are here?</em>) is a special case; the full pattern for questions that check back — <em>She's done what?</em> — is covered in <strong>Unit 27</strong>.
</Callout>

## D — How or what?

Both *how* and *what* open questions, but they are rarely interchangeable. **How** tends to ask for a **manner, condition or overall impression**; **what** asks for a **description, detail, name or consequence**.

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-violet-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-violet-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-violet-900">How</div>
    <div className="bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">What</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>How was the journey?</em> — asking a general opinion</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>What was the journey like?</em> — asking a general opinion</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>How is your brother?</em> — asking about general health</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>What do you like about the job?</em> — asking for details</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>How do you like your coffee?</em> — food and drink preferences</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>What if your plan doesn't work?</em> — asking about consequences</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-400">&nbsp;</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>What's it called?</em> — asking about a name</div>
  </div>
</div>

Notice the pairing at the top: *How was the journey?* and *What was the journey like?* ask almost the same thing — but *what* needs **like**.

<Callout type="tip" title="Where how and what are both fine">
  In a couple of fixed patterns you can use <strong>either</strong>. To <strong>make a suggestion</strong>: <em>How / What about a swim?</em> And to <strong>ask someone to say more</strong>: '<em>He's a bit scary sometimes.</em>' '<em>How / What do you mean?</em>'
</Callout>

## E — Whose

We use **whose** to ask about the person who **owns** something or is **responsible** for it. It can stand **before a verb** (as a pronoun):

<SayIt text="Whose are these boots?" />

or **before a noun or noun phrase** (as a determiner), in direct or indirect questions:

<SayIt text="Whose boots are these?" />
<br />
<SayIt text="She asked me whose coat I was wearing." />

In **formal** contexts a preposition can come **before** *whose*; less formally it drops to the end:

<SayIt text="In whose desk was it found?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(less formally: <em>Whose desk was it found in?</em> — see Unit 55)</p>

But in a **question with no verb**, the preposition **must** come before *whose*:

<SayIt text="'We're meeting at nine.' 'In whose house?'" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>Whose house in?</em>)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="Whose ≠ who's">
  <strong>Whose</strong> is the possessive (<em>Whose car is this?</em>). <strong>Who's</strong> is only ever the contraction of <em>who is</em> / <em>who has</em> (<em>Who's going with you?</em> = <em>Who is going ...?</em>). They sound identical, so let the meaning decide: if you can expand it to <em>who is / who has</em>, write <em>who's</em>; otherwise it's <em>whose</em>.
</Callout>
