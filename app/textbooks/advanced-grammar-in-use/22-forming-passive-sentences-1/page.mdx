## A — 'Giving' and 'telling' verbs: two passives, or only one?

Verbs such as **give** take both a **direct object (DO)** and an **indirect object (IO)**, in two patterns: **V + IO + DO** or **V + DO + preposition + IO**. These verbs have **two corresponding passives**, because either object can become the subject.

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-emerald-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-emerald-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-emerald-900">active</div>
    <div className="bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">passive</div>
    <div className="border-t border-emerald-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">Alice gave <strong>us</strong> that vase. <span className="text-slate-400">(V + IO + DO)</span></div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><strong>We</strong> were given that vase (by Alice). ✓</div>
    <div className="border-t border-emerald-100 bg-emerald-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">Alice gave that vase <strong>to us</strong>. <span className="text-slate-400">(V + DO + prep + IO)</span></div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><strong>That vase</strong> was given (to) us (by Alice). ✓</div>
  </div>
</div>

<SayIt text="We were given that vase by Alice." />
<br />
<SayIt text="That vase was given to us by Alice." />

The passive form you choose depends on which is **more appropriate in a particular context** — usually you make the *known* or *topical* information the subject. If you specify an agent, it follows **by** at the end of the clause.

<div className="not-prose my-6 grid gap-3 sm:grid-cols-2">
  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">'giving' verbs (two passives)</p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">give, award, hand, lend, offer, send, throw</p>
  </div>
  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-violet-200 bg-violet-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-violet-700">'telling' verbs (two passives)</p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-violet-900">ask, read, teach</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout type="tip" title="Dropping 'to' in informal English">
  In informal contexts the <em>to</em> can be left out of the second pattern: <em>That vase was given <strong>(to)</strong> us by Alice.</em> Both <em>given to us</em> and <em>given us</em> are heard in speech.
</Callout>

Now the trap. **'Reporting' verbs** (explain, suggest, etc.) cannot be followed by **IO + DO** in the active — you must say *explain something **to** somebody*. So they have **only one** passive: the *thing* becomes the subject, never the person.

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-rose-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-rose-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-rose-900">active</div>
    <div className="bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">passive</div>
    <div className="border-t border-rose-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><s>He explained me the problem.</s> ✗</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><s>I was explained the problem.</s> ✗</div>
    <div className="border-t border-rose-100 bg-rose-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">He explained the problem <strong>to me</strong>. ✓</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><strong>The problem</strong> was explained to me. ✓</div>
  </div>
</div>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-rose-200 bg-rose-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-rose-700">'reporting' verbs (one passive only — the <em>thing</em>, not the person)</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-rose-900">announce, demonstrate, describe, explain, introduce, mention, propose, report, suggest</p>
</div>

<Callout type="warning" title="The classic slip">
  ✅ Some changes <strong>were suggested to</strong> me. &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ I was suggested some changes. <br />
  ✅ The problem <strong>was explained to</strong> us. &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ We were explained the problem. <br />
  ✅ The plan <strong>was described to</strong> them. &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ They were described the plan.
</Callout>

## B — Object + complement: one passive form

Verbs followed by **object + complement** in the active have **one** passive form. The complement (a noun or adjective that renames the object) simply stays put after the verb.

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-emerald-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-emerald-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-emerald-900">active — V + object + complement</div>
    <div className="bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">passive</div>
    <div className="border-t border-emerald-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">They elected <strong>her</strong> <span className="text-slate-500">president</span>.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><strong>She</strong> was elected <span className="text-slate-500">president</span>.</div>
  </div>
</div>

<SayIt text="She was elected president of the committee." />
<br />
<SayIt text="The road was declared unsafe after the flood." />

  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-amber-200 bg-amber-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-amber-700">giving a position</p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-amber-900">appoint, declare, elect, make, nominate, vote</p>
  </div>
  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-sky-200 bg-sky-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-700">'naming' verbs</p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-sky-900">call, name, title</p>
  </div>
</div>

## C — Object + bare infinitive → to-infinitive in the passive

Some verbs are followed by an **object + bare infinitive** (an infinitive *without* 'to') in the active. In the passive, that bare infinitive becomes a **to-infinitive**.

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-emerald-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-emerald-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-emerald-900">active — V + object + <em>bare</em> infinitive</div>
    <div className="bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">passive — <em>to</em>-infinitive</div>
    <div className="border-t border-emerald-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">They have made him <strong>return</strong> the money.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">He has been made <strong>to return</strong> the money.</div>
  </div>
</div>

<SayIt text="He was made to return the money." />
<br />
<SayIt text="The suspect was seen to leave the building just after midnight." />

<Callout type="info" title="Which verbs do this?">
  The verbs that switch to a <em>to</em>-infinitive in the passive are <strong>feel, hear, help, make, observe, see</strong>. (<em>help</em> already allows <em>object + to-infinitive</em> in the active anyway.) Note that <strong>let</strong> is the exception — it has no passive of this kind; we use <em>be allowed to</em> instead. More on perception and <em>-ing</em> forms in <strong>Unit 23A</strong>.
</Callout>

## D — Transitive two- and three-word verbs

Many phrasal and prepositional verbs are transitive and so *can* be made passive — but not all, and some only in certain senses.

**Some have passive forms** — keep the particle(s) attached to the verb:

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-emerald-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-emerald-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-emerald-900">active</div>
    <div className="bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">passive</div>
    <div className="border-t border-emerald-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">Ella <strong>looked after</strong> him.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">He <strong>was looked after</strong> (by Ella).</div>
  </div>
</div>

<SayIt text="The repairs were carried out overnight." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Also: <em>carry out</em> (= put into practice), <em>disapprove of</em>, <em>hold over</em> (= delay), <em>talk down to</em> (= patronise).</p>

**Some are not used in the passive** — typically intransitive or idiomatic combinations:

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-rose-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-rose-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-rose-900">active</div>
    <div className="bg-slate-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-slate-700">no passive</div>
    <div className="border-t border-rose-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">We <strong>came up against</strong> a problem.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-slate-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-500"><s>A problem was come up against.</s> ✗</div>
  </div>
</div>

<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Also no passive: <em>brush up on</em> (= revise), <em>cast (your mind) back</em> (= try to remember), <em>get (something) down</em> (= write), <em>take after</em> (= resemble).</p>

**Some can be used in the passive only with certain senses:**

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-amber-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-amber-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-amber-900">active</div>
    <div className="bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">passive?</div>
    <div className="border-t border-amber-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">They <strong>put out</strong> the fire.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">The fire <strong>was put out</strong>. ✓</div>
    <div className="border-t border-amber-100 bg-amber-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">I <strong>put out</strong> a hand to steady myself.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-500"><s>A hand was put out to steady myself.</s> ✗</div>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout type="info" title="Sense decides">
  The same verb can be passive in one meaning but not another: <em>call (someone) up</em> (passive when it means 'order to join the army' or 'telephone'), <em>call (someone) back</em>, <em>let in</em> (allow into a place / let rain in), <em>let out</em> (allow to leave / produce a sound). Always check which sense you mean before reaching for the passive.
</Callout>
