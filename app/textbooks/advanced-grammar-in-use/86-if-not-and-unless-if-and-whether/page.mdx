## A — *Unless* = 'except if'

**Unless** is used in conditional sentences with the meaning **'except if'**. It introduces the one circumstance that would stop the main clause from being true.

<SayIt text="You can't travel on this train unless you have a reservation." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= except if you have a reservation — that's the only thing that lets you on.)</p>

When we talk about the future, the verb after **unless** stays in a **present tense**, just as it does after *if*:

<SayIt text="Unless it rains, I'll pick you up at six." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Not <em>Unless it will rain ...</em>)</p>

<Callout type="tip" title="The 'except if' test">
  If you can replace <em>unless</em> with <em>except if</em> and the meaning survives, you're using it correctly: <em>I'll be there at six <strong>except if</strong> it rains</em>. That little paraphrase is also the quickest way to feel whether <em>unless</em> really fits — see section B for the times it doesn't.
</Callout>

## B — When *unless* and *if ... not* part company

In **real** conditional sentences, you can often use **either** *unless* **or** *if ... not* with much the same meaning:

<SayIt text="Unless the theatre is able to raise £100,000, it will have to close." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or <em>If the theatre <strong>isn't</strong> able to raise £100,000 ...</em> — both imply 'it will close <em>only if</em> it can't raise the money'.)</p>

But there are three situations where you must use **if ... not** and **cannot** use *unless*:

**1. When the main clause says the if-clause event would be *unexpected*:**

<SayIt text="I'll be amazed if Christie doesn't win." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Not <em>... amazed unless Christie wins.</em> The surprise marks the event as unexpected.)</p>

**2. Usually in questions:**

<SayIt text="If you don't pass the test, what will you do?" />

**3. When the 'only if' implication does *not* apply:**

<SayIt text="If it wasn't the best performance of Hamlet I've seen, it was certainly the strangest." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(There's no 'only if' here — so <em>unless</em> would be wrong.)</p>

With **unreal** conditionals we usually prefer **if ... not**, not *unless*:

<SayIt text="If I weren't so tired, I'd give you a hand." />

**However**, *unless* **can** appear in an unreal conditional when the **main clause is negative**:

<SayIt text="She wouldn't have gone to university unless her parents had insisted." />

<Callout type="warning" title="Three places unless can't go">
  Swap to <strong>if ... not</strong> when the sentence is:<br />
  • <strong>a surprise</strong> — <em>I'll be amazed <strong>if</strong> she <strong>doesn't</strong> win</em> (not <em>unless she wins</em>)<br />
  • <strong>a question</strong> — <em>What will you do <strong>if</strong> you <strong>don't</strong> get in?</em><br />
  • <strong>an unreal conditional with an affirmative main clause</strong> — <em>She'd be perfect for the job <strong>if</strong> she <strong>weren't</strong> so disorganised</em> (not <em>unless she was so disorganised</em>).
</Callout>

## C — *if* and *whether* for two possibilities

We can use **if** or **whether** to report that two possibilities have been talked or thought about, or that someone isn't sure about something:

<SayIt text="They couldn't decide if / whether it was worth resitting the exam." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Do you know if / whether Ben's at home?" />

But **whether** (not *if*) can usually be followed **immediately** by **or not**:

<SayIt text="I didn't know whether or not Aya was coming." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Also fine: <em>... whether Aya was coming or not</em> / <em>... if Aya was coming or not</em>. But <strong>not</strong> <em>... if or not Aya was coming.</em>)</p>

<Callout type="info" title="Where 'or not' can sit">
  <em>or not</em> right after the conjunction → only <strong>whether</strong>: <em>whether <strong>or not</strong> she comes</em>.<br />
  <em>or not</em> pushed to the end → <strong>either</strong> works: <em>whether / if she comes <strong>or not</strong></em>.
</Callout>

## D — When only *whether* will do

Use **whether** rather than *if* —

**• after a preposition** (informally *if* is sometimes heard) and **before a to-infinitive**:

<SayIt text="We argued about whether butter or margarine would be better for us." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(after the preposition <em>about</em>; informally <em>... about if ...</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="I couldn't decide whether to buy apples or bananas." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(before the to-infinitive <em>to buy</em> — never <em>if to buy</em>.)</p>

**• in the pattern noun / adjective + *as to whether*** (meaning 'about' or 'concerning'):

<SayIt text="There was some disagreement as to whether he was eligible to play for France." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 grid gap-3 sm:grid-cols-2">
  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-violet-200 bg-violet-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-violet-700">nouns before <em>as to whether</em></p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-violet-900">conflict, confusion, debate, discussion, doubt, question, speculation, uncertainty, disagreement</p>
  </div>
  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">adjectives before <em>as to whether</em></p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">concerned, indifferent, uncertain, undecided, unsure</p>
  </div>
</div>

And we **prefer** **whether** rather than *if* —

**• after the verbs *advise, choose, consider, depend on, discuss, talk about,* and *think about*:**

<SayIt text="You should consider whether the car you are interested in is good value." />

**• in a clause acting as a subject or complement:**

<SayIt text="Whether the minister will quit over the issue remains to be seen." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(subject of <em>remains</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="The first issue is whether he knew he was committing a crime." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(complement after <em>is</em>)</p>

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick decision: if or whether?">
  Reach for <strong>whether</strong> when the clause comes <em>before</em> something — a <em>to-</em>infinitive (<em>whether to go</em>), <em>or not</em> (<em>whether or not</em>), or the main verb itself (<em>Whether he comes is ...</em>) — and after <em>as to</em> / verbs like <em>consider, discuss, depend on</em>. In plain reported questions where none of that applies, <strong>if</strong> and <strong>whether</strong> are interchangeable.
</Callout>
