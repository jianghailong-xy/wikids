## A — Reporting with a plain passive

We often use a passive to report what people say, think, etc. — particularly when it isn't important to mention **who** is being reported. The reporter disappears, and the message takes centre stage.

<SayIt text="People in the area have been told that they should stay indoors." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Everyone was asked to bring some food to the party." />

<Callout type="info" title="Why reach for the passive here">
  The passive lets you report a claim, an order or a finding without naming a source — useful when the source is an unspecified group (<em>people</em>, <em>the authorities</em>), is obvious, or is deliberately left vague. This is the engine behind the impersonal <em>it is said that …</em> patterns in the rest of the unit.
</Callout>

## B — It + passive verb + that-clause

Another common way of reporting what is said by an **unspecified group of people** is **it + passive verb + that-clause**. The dummy *it* fills the subject slot, which lets us push the important information to the **end** of the sentence (see Unit 24C on end-weight):

<SayIt text="It is reported that the damage is extensive." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Compare: <em>The damage is extensive, according to government sources.</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="It was decided that the meeting should be cancelled." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Or, with a to-infinitive: <em>It was decided to cancel the meeting.</em>)</p>

These reporting verbs take **it + passive + that-clause**:

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">it is / was + past participle + that …</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">allege, announce, assume, believe, calculate, claim, consider, demonstrate, discover, establish, estimate, expect, feel, find, know, mention, recommend, reveal, say, show, suggest, suppose, think, understand</p>
  <p className="mt-3 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">… and these can also be followed by a to-infinitive clause</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">agree, decide, hope, intend, plan, propose</p>
</div>

<SayIt text="It is hoped that a settlement will be reached soon." />
<br />
<SayIt text="It has been agreed to hold new negotiations next month." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<em>agree</em> is in the second group, so the to-infinitive frame works too.)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="The trap: verbs that need a personal object">
  Many reporting verbs are <strong>not</strong> used in the <em>it + passive + that-clause</em> pattern — they need a <strong>personal object</strong> before the that-clause:
  <div className="mt-2 rounded-lg bg-white/70 px-3 py-2 text-sm font-semibold text-rose-900">encourage, persuade, reassure, remind, tell, warn</div>
  <p className="mt-2">✅ We <strong>have been informed</strong> that we have to leave. &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ <s>It has been informed us that …</s></p>
  <p className="mt-1">These verbs work only with an object: <em>They have informed <strong>me</strong> that …</em>, <em>They warned <strong>us</strong> that …</em></p>
</Callout>

## C — Subject + passive verb + to-infinitive

An alternative to *it + passive + that-clause* is **subject + passive verb + to-infinitive** — used when we want the **subject** (not dummy *it*) to be the topic of the sentence. Compare:

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-violet-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-violet-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-violet-900">it + passive + that-clause</div>
    <div className="bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">subject + passive + to-infinitive</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">It is reported <em>that the damage is extensive.</em></div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>The damage</em> is reported <strong>to be</strong> extensive.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">It is believed <em>that the gang has left.</em></div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>The gang</em> is believed <strong>to have</strong> left.</div>
  </div>
</div>

Most of the verbs in the **first box in B** work in this pattern too — **except** for these six:

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-rose-200 bg-rose-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-rose-700">NOT used in subject + passive + to-infinitive</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-rose-900">announce, decide, mention, propose, recommend, suggest</p>
</div>

<Callout type="tip" title="tell in this pattern means 'order', not 'say'">
  We can only use <strong>tell</strong> in the subject + to-infinitive pattern when it means <em>order</em>:
  <p className="mt-2">✅ <strong>I was told</strong> (= ordered) <strong>to go</strong> with them to the railway station.</p>
  <p className="mt-1">❌ <s>The accident was told (= said) to have happened just after midnight.</s> → use <em>was said to have happened</em>.</p>
</Callout>

## D — It + passive verb + wh-clause

With some verbs we can also use **it + passive verb + wh-clause** to report information that has been **given or found out**:

<SayIt text="It has now been revealed who was responsible for the accident." />
<br />
<SayIt text="The decision to build the bridge was taken before it was established whether it was actually needed." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-sky-200 bg-sky-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-700">it is / was + past participle + who / what / whether / how …</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-sky-900">discover, establish, explain, find, know, reveal, show, understand</p>
</div>

## E — There + passive verb + to be / to have been

When a that-clause begins with **there …**, we can make a corresponding passive form **there + passive verb + to be / to have been**. Compare:

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-amber-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-1 text-sm sm:grid-cols-2">
    <div className="bg-amber-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-amber-900">it + passive + that there …</div>
    <div className="bg-orange-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-orange-900">there + passive + to be …</div>
    <div className="border-t border-amber-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">It is thought <em>(that)</em> <strong>there are</strong> too many obstacles to peace.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-orange-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><strong>There are</strong> thought <strong>to be</strong> too many obstacles to peace.</div>
  </div>
</div>

We can use the **same verbs** in this *there*-pattern as in the subject + passive + to-infinitive pattern (see C).

<SayIt text="There are believed to be survivors trapped in the building." />
<br />
<SayIt text="There is said to have been a serious disagreement at the meeting." />

<Callout type="tip" title="Take it further">
  Pick three reporting verbs you use a lot (<em>say, believe, expect</em>?) and write each claim twice — once as <em>It is … that …</em> and once as <em>X is … to …</em>. Then check: is your verb on the <strong>barred-from-pattern-C</strong> list (announce, decide, mention, propose, recommend, suggest)? If so, only the first version is possible. Switching deliberately between the two — and knowing when you can't — is what makes impersonal, objective writing sound natural.
