Unit 67 introduced the basic split: **gradable** adjectives (can be stronger or weaker — *very cold*, *a bit tired*) versus **non-gradable** adjectives (already at an extreme — *absolutely freezing*, *completely exhausted*). This unit covers adjectives that live on **both** sides of the line: the same word can be gradable in one sense and non-gradable in another. Mastering this double life is what separates advanced from intermediate use.

## A — Different senses: gradable vs non-gradable

Some adjectives have **completely different meanings** depending on whether they're used gradably or non-gradably. Compare:

**common**

<SayIt text="Smith is a very common name." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(common = frequently found — gradable; you can say <em>very common</em>, <em>fairly common</em>.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="We have a lot of common interests." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(common = shared — non-gradable; <span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>very common interests</em> to mean shared.)</p>

**old**

<SayIt text="The house is very old." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(old = existed many years — gradable.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="I met my old politics professor the other day." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(old = former — non-gradable; <span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>my very old politics professor</em> to mean former.)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="Watch the sense, not just the word">
  The same adjective can be gradable in one sentence and non-gradable in the very next. What decides is the <strong>meaning</strong>, not the form. Ask yourself: does it describe a quality with degrees (gradable), or does it assign something to a category (non-gradable)?
</Callout>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-amber-200 bg-amber-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-amber-700">Adjectives that work this way</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-amber-900">civil · clean · critical · electric · empty · false · late · odd · original · particular · straight</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-amber-800">Examples: <em>a very civil conversation</em> (= polite; gradable) vs <em>civil rights</em> (= relating to citizens; non-gradable); <em>very clean hands</em> (= not dirty; gradable) vs <em>clean energy</em> (= not polluting; non-gradable); <em>a very electric performance</em> (= exciting; gradable) vs <em>an electric guitar</em> (= powered by electricity; non-gradable).</p>
</div>

## B — Qualitative vs classifying adjectives

Some adjectives have **similar** meanings in both uses, but there's a systematic difference: when **gradable**, they describe a **quality** a person or thing has (qualitative adjectives, so they can take an adverb); when **non-gradable**, they describe the **category or type** something belongs to (classifying adjectives). Compare:

**foreign**

<SayIt text="I don't know where he came from, but he sounded slightly foreign." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(foreign = not from this country — a quality you can have more or less of; gradable.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="She is now advising on the government's foreign policy." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(foreign = concerning other countries — a category of policy; non-gradable.)</p>

**public**

<SayIt text="They had a very public argument." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(public = seen / heard by a lot of people — a quality; gradable.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="He was forced to resign by public pressure." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(public = from many people in the community — a type of pressure; non-gradable.)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-sky-200 bg-sky-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-700">Also behave this way</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-sky-900">academic · adult · average · diplomatic · genuine · guilty · human · individual · innocent · mobile · private · professional · scientific · technical · true · wild</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-sky-800">When these are gradable they answer "how much?"; when non-gradable they answer "what kind?" — <em>a very academic child</em> (studious; gradable) vs <em>the academic year</em> (relating to education; non-gradable); <em>a very private person</em> (reserved; gradable) vs <em>private property</em> (belonging to an individual; non-gradable).</p>
</div>

## C — good / lovely / nice + and + adjective

In spoken English, we can use **good and ...**, **lovely and ...**, and **nice and ...** followed by another gradable adjective to **emphasise** the second adjective. The pattern means something like "pleasantly" or "satisfyingly" + the adjective.

<Callout type="info" title="Not all combinations work">
  These are fixed collocations — native speakers use them with specific adjectives only. The lists below show what's natural and what isn't.
</Callout>

**good and ...**

<SayIt text="If you're all feeling good and relaxed after the break, let's get on with the meeting." />

Common with: **good and ready**, **good and proper**, **good and relaxed**, **good and strong**.

<p className="text-sm text-rose-600 mt-1">✗ Not usually: good and beautiful, good and rich, good and tall.</p>

**lovely and ...**

<SayIt text="It's lovely and warm in here. Freezing outside, though." />

Common with: **lovely and dry**, **lovely and soft**, **lovely and sunny**, **lovely and warm**.

<p className="text-sm text-rose-600 mt-1">✗ Not usually: lovely and decent, lovely and empty, lovely and short.</p>

**nice and ...**

<SayIt text="'Shall we get some strawberries?' 'Yes, they look nice and fresh.'" />

Common with: **nice and bright**, **nice and clean**, **nice and cold**, **nice and comfortable**, **nice and early**, **nice and fresh**, **nice and quiet**, **nice and simple**, **nice and soft**, **nice and tidy**, **nice and warm**.

<p className="text-sm text-rose-600 mt-1">✗ Not usually: nice and interesting, nice and handsome, nice and exciting.</p>

<Callout type="tip" title="The grammar behind it">
  The <em>good / lovely / nice + and + adjective</em> pattern is a fixed spoken formula. The <em>and</em> doesn't join two independent ideas — it's part of an intensifier. Think of <em>nice and warm</em> as one unit meaning "pleasantly warm."
</Callout>

## D — Comparative adjectives linked with *and*

We can link **comparative adjectives** with **and** to talk about an **increasing degree** of the quality. We also use **more and more + adjective** in a similar way:

<SayIt text="As she got more and more excited, her voice got higher and higher and louder and louder." />

<SayIt text="The taxi driver just drove faster and faster and faster until I told him to stop, and I got out." />

<Callout type="info" title="Two patterns, same meaning">
  Both <em>higher and higher</em> and <em>more and more excited</em> express a continuous increase. Choose based on whether the adjective has a synthetic comparative (<em>-er</em>) or takes <em>more</em>:<br />
  • shorter adjectives → <em>colder and colder, worse and worse, faster and faster</em><br />
  • longer adjectives → <em>more and more expensive, more and more complicated</em>
</Callout>

  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-violet-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-violet-900">Gradable sense</div>

    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">describes a quality with degrees (<em>very common, very old, very public</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">assigns to a category or has a fixed meaning (<em>common interests, old professor, public pressure</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">qualitative: answers "how much?" (<em>slightly foreign, very private</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">classifying: answers "what kind?" (<em>foreign policy, private property</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>good and ready, lovely and warm, nice and fresh</em> — spoken intensifiers</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>higher and higher, more and more excited</em> — continuous increase</div>
  </div>
</div>
