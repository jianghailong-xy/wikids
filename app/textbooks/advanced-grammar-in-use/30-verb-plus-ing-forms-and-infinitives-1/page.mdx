When one verb is followed by another, English forces a choice of shape: an **-ing** form (*I enjoy **cooking***), a **to-**infinitive (*I want **to cook***), or a **bare infinitive** (*I made him **cook***). This unit gathers five patterns where that choice is governed by the first verb and, often, by an object or a preposition sitting between them — and where a small change of form quietly changes the meaning.

## A — Object + -ing or possessive + -ing

Some verbs can be followed either by an **object + -ing** or by a **possessive + -ing** with a similar meaning. The **possessive + -ing** form is usually felt to be rather **formal**:

<SayIt text="I resented Tom winning the prize." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(more formally: <em>I resented Tom's winning the prize.</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Mia recalled him buying the book." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(more formally: <em>Mia recalled his buying the book.</em>)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">Verbs that work this way</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">'(dis)liking' verbs — detest · (dis)approve of · (dis)like · hate · love · object to</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">'thinking' verbs — forget · imagine · remember · think of</p>
</div>

<Callout type="warning" title="A possessive only for people">
  We use a possessive form (<em>Tom's, his, their</em>) here only to talk about a <strong>person or group of people</strong> — not an animal or a thing:<br /><br />
  ✅ I remember <strong>the horse</strong> winning the race. &nbsp; ❌ <span className="text-rose-600">… the horse's winning …</span>
</Callout>

## B — to + -ing, where *to* is a preposition

With a handful of verbs, the *to* that follows is a **preposition**, not part of an infinitive — so the verb after it must be an **-ing** form, never a bare infinitive:

<SayIt text="She confessed to stealing the money." />
<br />
<SayIt text="You don't object to working late tonight, do you?" />

Because this *to* is a preposition, the very same verbs can also be followed by **to + a noun phrase**:

<SayIt text="She confessed to the crime." />
<br />
<SayIt text="You don't object to the work, do you?" />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">Also take <em>to + -ing</em></p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">adapt · adjust · admit · look forward · own up · resort</p>
</div>

<Callout type="tip" title="The two-step test">
  If you can drop in a noun and it still reads (<em>look forward to the holiday</em> → <em>look forward to going</em>), the <em>to</em> is a preposition and the verb takes <strong>-ing</strong>. Contrast <em>hope to go</em>, where <em>hope to the holiday</em> is impossible — there <em>to</em> is part of the infinitive.
</Callout>

## C — Other prepositions + -ing

Many verbs lock onto a **different preposition**, and after any preposition a following verb takes the **-ing** form. Often an **object** can sit between the preposition and the -ing form:

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-sky-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-[auto_1fr] text-sm">
    <div className="bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">by + -ing</div>
    <div className="bg-sky-50 px-4 py-2 text-slate-700">begin, close, end, finish (off / up), open, start (off / out)</div>
    <div className="col-span-2 border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>Can you <strong>begin by</strong> cleaning the floors, and then do the windows?</em></div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">on (+ obj) + -ing</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-50 px-4 py-2 text-slate-700">concentrate, count, depend, focus, insist, rely</div>
    <div className="col-span-2 border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>Clare <strong>insisted on</strong> (Jack) wearing a suit to the party.</em></div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">of (+ obj) + -ing</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-50 px-4 py-2 text-slate-700">approve, hear, know, speak, talk, tell</div>
    <div className="col-span-2 border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>Have you ever <strong>heard of</strong> (anyone) getting arrested for gossiping before?</em></div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">obj + from + -ing</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-50 px-4 py-2 text-slate-700">deter, discourage, keep, prevent, prohibit, stop</div>
    <div className="col-span-2 border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>The noise from next door <strong>prevented me from</strong> sleeping.</em></div>
  </div>
</div>

## D — Perception verbs: -ing or bare infinitive

Some verbs of perception — **feel, hear, notice, observe, overhear, see, watch** — can be followed by an object and then either by an **-ing** form or a **bare infinitive**. Both are correct, but the meaning shifts slightly. The **-ing** form frames an action **in progress** (and often only partly witnessed); the **bare infinitive** frames a **complete** action, witnessed from start to finish:

<SayIt text="I saw them playing football from my window." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<strong>-ing</strong> — an action in progress; I caught them in the middle of it.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="I saw him smash the bottle." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<strong>bare infinitive</strong> — one complete action, seen from beginning to end.)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-violet-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-violet-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-violet-900">object + -ing</div>
    <div className="bg-amber-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-amber-900">object + bare infinitive</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">action <strong>in progress</strong>; watched, but perhaps not start to finish<br /><em>I watched them building the new car park.</em></div>
    <div className="border-t border-amber-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">a <strong>complete</strong> action, watched the whole way through<br /><em>I watched him climb through the window, and then I called the police.</em></div>
  </div>
</div>

## E — dare, help; and have, let, make

After **dare** and **help** we can use **either** a bare infinitive **or** a to-infinitive — the two are interchangeable:

<SayIt text="I was angry with him, but I didn't dare (to) say anything." />
<br />
<SayIt text="We hope the campaign will help (to) raise awareness of the problem." />

But when **dare** has an **object**, only the **to-**infinitive is possible:

<SayIt text="I dared him to cross the river." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>I dared him cross …</em>) — compare <em>I helped them (to) pack</em>, where help still allows either.</p>

After **have**, **let** and **make**, use an **object + bare infinitive** — never a to-infinitive:

<SayIt text="His exam results might make him work harder." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>… might make him to work …</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="I had Irena clean up her bedroom before I let her go out to play." />

<Callout type="info" title="Quick reference — five patterns">
  <strong>A</strong> object / possessive + -ing → <em>I resented Tom / Tom's winning</em><br />
  <strong>B</strong> to (= preposition) + -ing → <em>confessed to stealing</em><br />
  <strong>C</strong> other preposition + -ing → <em>begin by, insist on, hear of, prevent … from</em><br />
  <strong>D</strong> perception verb + object + -ing / bare infinitive → <em>saw them playing / saw him smash</em><br />
  <strong>E</strong> dare, help + (to) do; have / let / make + object + bare infinitive
</Callout>
