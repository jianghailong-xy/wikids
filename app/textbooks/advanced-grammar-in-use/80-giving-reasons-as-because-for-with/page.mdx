There are far more ways to give a reason than just *because*. Some introduce a whole clause (*as, since, seeing that, for, in that*); some take only a noun phrase (*because of, due to, owing to*); and two — *for* and *with* — slip a reason in almost invisibly. The choices carry register with them: *seeing as* is chatty, *since* and *for* are formal, *inasmuch as* is positively literary. This unit sorts out which connective takes which kind of clause, and where each one is allowed to sit in the sentence.

## A — Reason clauses: as, because, seeing that, since

We can begin a clause with **as, because, seeing that, seeing as,** or **since** to give a reason for a particular situation:

<SayIt text="As it was getting late, I decided I should go home." />
<br />
<SayIt text="We must be near the beach, because I can hear the waves." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Since he was going to be away on his birthday, we celebrated before he left." />
<br />
<SayIt text="We could go and visit Natalia, seeing that we have to drive past her house anyway." />

Notice that the reason clause can come **first** (then a comma) or **second** — and that, unlike in some languages, it is common and perfectly acceptable for **because** to open a sentence:

<SayIt text="Because everything looked different, I had no idea where to go." />

<Callout type="info" title="because vs so — reason vs result">
  To give reasons in speech we most often use <strong>because</strong>. <strong>So</strong> expresses a similar idea from the other direction — it introduces the <em>result</em>, not the reason. Compare:<br /><br />
  <em>Because my mother's ill, I won't be able to come.</em> — <strong>because</strong> introduces the reason.<br />
  <em>My mother's ill, so I won't be able to come.</em> — <strong>so</strong> introduces the result.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="since = because is formal">
  When it means 'because', <strong>since</strong> is rather formal: it is uncommon in conversation but frequent in academic writing.<br /><br />
  Informal: <em>I had to go outside <strong>because</strong> I was feeling awful.</em> (<em>since</em> would sound odd here)<br />
  Academic: <em>The results of this analysis can be easily compared to future observations <strong>since</strong> satellite coverage will remain continuous.</em> (more natural than <em>because</em> in this formal context)
</Callout>

At the other end of the register scale, **seeing that** is informal, and some speakers also use **seeing as** in casual speech:

<SayIt text="Joel just had to apologise, seeing that he knew he'd made a mistake." />

## B — Formal and literary: for, in that, inasmuch as

In **formal or literary** written English we can also introduce a reason in a clause beginning **for, in that,** or — less commonly still — **inasmuch as**.

**For** is a formal alternative to *because*. Crucially, a *for*-clause **cannot open** the sentence — it must follow the main clause:

<SayIt text="We must begin planning now, for the future may bring unexpected changes." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(not <em>For the future may bring unexpected changes, we must begin planning now.</em>)</p>

**In that** and **inasmuch as** don't simply give a reason — they introduce a clause that **clarifies or qualifies** what has just been said by adding detail. Either can stand at the front or in the middle:

<SayIt text="The film is unusual in that it features only four actors." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or <em>In that it features only four actors, the film is unusual.</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Clara and I have an easy life, inasmuch as neither of us has to work too hard but we earn quite a lot of money." />

## C — because of, due to, owing to + noun phrase

The prepositions **because of, due to,** and **owing to** introduce a reason as a **noun (phrase)**, not a clause:

<SayIt text="We were delayed because of an accident." />
<br />
<SayIt text="She was unable to run owing to / due to a leg injury." />
<br />
<SayIt text="We have less money to spend owing to / due to budget cuts." />

<Callout type="warning" title="because + clause, but because OF + noun">
  Keep the two apart: <strong>because</strong> is followed by a full clause (subject + verb), while <strong>because of</strong> is followed by a noun phrase.<br /><br />
  ✅ <em>We were delayed <strong>because there was</strong> an accident.</em><br />
  ✅ <em>We were delayed <strong>because of</strong> an accident.</em><br />
  ❌ <em>We were delayed because of there was an accident.</em>
</Callout>

There are two restrictions on **owing to** that don't apply to **due to**. First, in current English we usually **avoid *owing to* directly after a form of *be*** — use *due to* there:

<SayIt text="The company's success is due to the new director." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(not <em>... is owing to the new director.</em>)</p>

But *owing to* **is** allowed after *be* once a **degree adverb** — *entirely, largely, mainly, partly* — comes in between:

<SayIt text="The low election turnout was partly due to / owing to the bad weather." />

Both also fit the cleft pattern *it was ... that ...*, which throws the spotlight onto the reason:

<SayIt text="It was owing to his encouragement that she applied for the job." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or <em>It was due to his encouragement that she applied for the job.</em>)</p>

## D — for and with + noun phrase

Finally, **for** and **with** followed by a noun phrase can give a reason too (compare the *for*-clause in B above — here *for* takes a noun, not a clause).

**For** typically pairs with a comparative or with *all the* + adjective, meaning *'as a result of'*:

<SayIt text="She was looking all the better for her stay in hospital." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= as a result of her stay in hospital)</p>

**With** introduces an attendant circumstance — often a noun followed by an *-ing* form, a participle, an adjective or a phrase — meaning *'as a result of there being'*:

<SayIt text="With so many people ill, the meeting was cancelled." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= as a result of there being so many people ill)</p>
