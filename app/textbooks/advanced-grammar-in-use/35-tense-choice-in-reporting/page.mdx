By now you know how to backshift a report (Units 32–34): *'I'm leaving'* becomes *She said she was leaving*. But backshift is not automatic. Two things decide the tense you actually choose: **when the situation was true** relative to the moment you report it, and **whether the reporting verb itself is present or past**. This unit is about reading those two signals so that a report says exactly what you mean — no more, and no less.

## A — Verb tense in the *reported* clause

When the situation described in the *reported* clause was in the **past** at the time you report it, use a **past tense** (past simple, past continuous, etc.):

<SayIt text="'I don't want anything to eat.' → Yusuf said that he didn't want anything to eat." />
<br />
<SayIt text="'I'm leaving!' → Lamar announced that she was leaving." />

When the situation was **already in the past** when it was first spoken about, we often use the **past perfect**:

<SayIt text="'We have found the missing girl.' → Police said that they had found the missing girl." />

However, if it is already **clear that one event happened before another**, the past perfect may not be necessary and the **past simple** is enough:

<SayIt text="'I sent out the invitations well before the wedding.' → She reassured me that she sent out the invitations well before the wedding. (or … had sent …)" />

<Callout type="info" title="The rule of thumb">
  A <strong>past</strong> tense in the reported clause reports a situation seen as finished. The <strong>past perfect</strong> reaches one step further back — to something already past when it was mentioned — but you only need it when the order of events isn't otherwise obvious. Once a time marker such as <em>two weeks ago</em> or <em>before the wedding</em> makes the sequence clear, the plain past simple will do.
</Callout>

## B — Reported clause: still true, permanent or uncertain

When the situation is a **permanent or habitual** one, or **still exists / is still relevant** as you report it, you can use a **present tense (or present perfect)** — *provided you also use a present tense in the reporting clause*:

<SayIt text="Dr Weir thinks that he spends about five minutes on a typical appointment with a patient." />
<br />
<SayIt text="US scientists claim that they have developed a new vaccine against malaria." />

<Callout type="tip" title="Why the present perfect here?">
  <em>…have developed a new vaccine…</em> uses the present perfect to focus on the <strong>result</strong> of the action — the vaccine now exists — rather than on the act of developing it.
</Callout>

But when the reporting verb is **past**, you have a genuine choice: a **present** tense keeps the situation live, while a **past** tense is neutral. Both are grammatical:

<SayIt text="She argued that Carl is / was the best person for the job." />
<br />
<SayIt text="They noted that the rate of inflation has / had slowed down." />

Choosing the **present** (or present perfect) after a past reporting verb **emphasises that the situation still exists or is still relevant** now.

<Callout type="warning" title="A present tense can quietly vouch for the truth">
  If you are <strong>not sure</strong> that what you are reporting is true, or the situation may no longer hold, prefer the <strong>past</strong>. Compare:
  <br /><br />
  <em>Yasemin told me that she <strong>has</strong> two houses.</em> — hints you take it to be true.<br />
  <em>Yasemin told me that she <strong>had</strong> two houses.</em> — hints it may not be true, or that it was once so but isn't now.
</Callout>

## C — Verb tense in the *reporting* clause

To report something said or thought in the past, the reporting verb is often **past**:

<SayIt text="Just before her wedding, she revealed that she had been married before." />

But to report **current news, opinions, laws and hearsay**, you can put the reporting verb in the **present**. Where either is possible, a **present** reporting verb stresses that what was said is **true or still relevant** now:

<SayIt text="She says that she'll have to close the shop unless business improves. (or … said …)" />

We especially prefer a **present** reporting verb in three cases:

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-sky-200 bg-sky-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-700">Present reporting verb preferred</p>
  <ul className="mt-2 space-y-2 text-sm text-sky-900">
    <li>▸ Reporting what you've been <strong>told or heard</strong> but can't vouch for: <em>I <strong>hear</strong> you're unhappy with your job.</em></li>
    <li>▸ Reporting what an <strong>authority</strong> says: <em>The law <strong>says</strong> that no one under 16 can buy a lottery ticket.</em></li>
    <li>▸ Reporting what <strong>many people</strong> say: <em>Every teacher I've spoken to <strong>tells</strong> me that standards of spelling are in decline.</em></li>
  </ul>
</div>

  </div>
