An adjective rarely stands alone. After a linking verb — **appear, be, become, get, seem** (see Unit 21) — we very often want to extend the adjective: *free **to leave***, *busy **doing it***, *worried **that** she'd fall*, *not certain **why** he wants it*. The problem for an advanced learner is not knowing the patterns exist but knowing **which adjective takes which** — *keen to go* but not *keen going*, *busy doing* but not *busy to do*, *confident that...* but not *confident to...*. This unit sorts the adjectives into those patterns, and then looks at the parallel **It + be + adjective** construction.

## A — Four patterns after an adjective

When an adjective follows a linking verb we can complete it with a **to-infinitive**, an **-ing** form, a **that-clause** or a **wh-clause**. Each pattern goes with its own set of adjectives.

<SayIt text="You're free to leave at any time you want." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(i — <em>free</em> + to-infinitive.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="He was busy doing his homework." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(ii — <em>busy</em> + -ing.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="He became worried that she might fall down." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(iii — <em>worried</em> + that-clause; the <em>that</em> can be left out.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="I'm not certain why he wants to borrow the money." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(iv — <em>certain</em> + wh-clause; we can add <em>of / about</em>: <em>not certain about why...</em>)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="The classic mix-ups">
  <strong>busy</strong> takes only <strong>-ing</strong>, never a to-infinitive: <em>busy doing</em>, <span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>busy to do</em>.<br /><br />
  <strong>confident, certain, positive, sure</strong> take a <strong>that-clause</strong>, not a to-infinitive: <em>confident (that) he wouldn't get lost</em>, <span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>confident not to get lost</em>.<br /><br />
  The <strong>wh-clause</strong> adjectives are nearly all <strong>negative or doubting</strong> in sense (<em>not sure, uncertain, doubtful, unaware</em>) — uncertainty is what licenses the <em>why / whether / what</em> clause.
</Callout>

## B — Adjectives that allow two patterns

Some adjectives accept **more than one** completion, often with no real change of meaning.

**to-infinitive *or* that-clause** — all the *that-clause* adjectives in (iii) **except** *aware, confident* and *positive*:

<SayIt text="She was afraid to say anything." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I was afraid that I would be late." />

**to-infinitive *or* -ing** — the small group **crazy, foolish, mad, stupid**:

<SayIt text="He'd be stupid to leave now." />
<br />
<SayIt text="He'd be stupid giving up the job." />

**-ing *or* that-clause** — after the verb **feel**, the adjectives **awful, awkward, bad, good, guilty, terrible**:

<SayIt text="She felt awful leaving him with all the clearing up." />
<br />
<SayIt text="She felt awful that she was late." />

<Callout type="tip" title="Read the adjective, then the slot">
  Before you commit to a form, ask which family the adjective belongs to. <em>stupid / crazy / mad / foolish</em> are the flexible four — to-infinitive <strong>or</strong> -ing. <em>feel + awful / guilty / awkward</em> takes -ing <strong>or</strong> a that-clause, but never a bare to-infinitive (<span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>felt guilty to shop</em>).
</Callout>

## C — It + linking verb + adjective

We can often move the adjective into an **It + linking verb + adjective** frame instead of putting the real subject first (see also Units 96–97):

<SayIt text="The fireworks were amazing to watch." />
<br />
<SayIt text="It was amazing to watch the fireworks." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Same meaning — <em>It</em> stands in, the real subject moves after the verb.)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-sky-200 bg-sky-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-700">Adjectives in this frame</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-sky-900">amazing · annoying · awkward · easy · good · interesting · lovely · simple · terrific · wonderful</p>
</div>

In **informal** speech an **-ing** form can replace the to-infinitive, and a parallel pattern works with a **wh-clause**:

<SayIt text="It is easy understanding her." />
<br />
<SayIt text="It is not clear why he did it." />
<br />
<SayIt text="It was odd that she left so suddenly." />

After adjectives that **judge a person's behaviour**, we often slip **of + subject** between the adjective and the to-infinitive:

<SayIt text="It was rude of them to criticise her." />
<br />
<SayIt text="They were rude to criticise her." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-violet-200 bg-violet-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-violet-700">Take "of + person" — they judge character</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-violet-900">brave · generous · kind · mean · rude · thoughtful · unprofessional · unreasonable</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-violet-800">Use <strong>of</strong>, not <em>for</em>: <em>It was kind <strong>of</strong> you</em>, <span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>kind for you</em>. The "experience" adjectives above (<em>easy, awkward, wonderful, simple</em>) describe the task, not a person, so they take <strong>no</strong> of-phrase.</p>
</div>

## D — It made me + adjective (reacting to a situation)

To say how somebody **reacts** to something, use **It + make** with an adjective followed by a **to-infinitive, an -ing form, or a that-clause**:

<SayIt text="It made me angry to discover that so much money was wasted." />
<br />
<SayIt text="It made me angry discovering that so much money was wasted." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I was angry to discover that so much money was wasted." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(All three are natural — the to-infinitive and -ing are interchangeable here.)</p>

</div>
