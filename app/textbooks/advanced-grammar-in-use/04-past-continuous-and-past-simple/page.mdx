Both the **past continuous** (*was / were + -ing*) and the **past simple** (*I did*) put us firmly in finished past time. The choice between them is not about *when* something happened but about *how* you frame it: as an activity stretched out and **in progress**, or as a single, bounded event that is simply **done**. This unit drills the four places where that framing matters — overlapping activities, repeated actions, narrative, and things you meant to do but never did.

## A — Two things going on over the same past period

When we talk about two events or activities that went on over the **same period** of past time, we often use the past continuous or the past simple for **both**:

<SayIt text="Mia was reading to the children while Ben was washing up." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or … Mia <em>read</em> to the children while Ben <em>washed up</em>.)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-2xl border-2 border-indigo-200 bg-gradient-to-br from-indigo-50 to-violet-50 p-5 shadow-sm">
  <p className="text-center text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-indigo-700">the same past period</p>
  <div className="mt-3 space-y-2 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-700">
    <div className="flex items-center gap-2">
      <span className="w-32 text-right text-indigo-800">was reading</span>
      <span className="flex-1 rounded-full bg-indigo-200 px-3 py-1 text-center text-indigo-900">━━━━━━━━━━</span>
    </div>
    <div className="flex items-center gap-2">
      <span className="w-32 text-right text-violet-800">was washing up</span>
      <span className="flex-1 rounded-full bg-violet-200 px-3 py-1 text-center text-violet-900">━━━━━━━━━━</span>
    </div>
  </div>
  <p className="mt-3 text-center text-sm text-slate-600">past <span className="mx-4 text-indigo-400">───────────────</span> now</p>
</div>

Using the past continuous **emphasises that the activity was in progress** during the past period of time (*'was reading'* while *'Ben was washing up'*). Compare:

<SayIt text="When I was learning to drive I was living with my parents." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1"><strong>was learning</strong> emphasises that the activity was in progress ('I had lessons during this time').</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="When I learned to drive I was living with my parents." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1"><strong>learned</strong> emphasises completion ('I passed my test during this time').</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="A sequence of completed events takes the past simple">
  When you talk about two or more <strong>completed</strong> events that followed one another, use the <strong>past simple</strong> for both — <span className="text-rose-600">not</span> the past continuous: <em>She <strong>got</strong> up when the alarm clock <strong>went</strong> off.</em> (First the alarm, then she got up — one finished thing after another.)
</Callout>

## B — Repeated past actions

We usually use the **past simple** rather than the past continuous to talk about **repeated** past actions:

<SayIt text="We went to Spain three times last year." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Did you drive past her house every day?" />

However, we **can** use the past continuous — particularly in spoken English — when we want to emphasise that the repeated actions went on for a **limited and temporary period** of time:

<SayIt text="When Kata was in hospital, we were visiting her twice a day." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or … we <em>visited</em> her twice a day.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="To lose weight before the race, I wasn't eating any biscuits for weeks." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or … I <em>didn't eat</em> any biscuits for weeks.)</p>

…or to talk about something that happened **surprisingly often**:

<SayIt text="Last week I was having to drive home every night to get it all done." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or … I <em>had</em> to drive home every night.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="When the builders were here I was making them cups of tea all the time." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or … I <em>made</em> them cups of tea all the time.)</p>

<Callout type="tip" title="Both are correct — the continuous just adds flavour">
  In all of these, the past simple is perfectly fine. Choosing the continuous signals that the repetition was <strong>temporary</strong> (just for that stretch of time) or <strong>strikingly frequent</strong>. Reserve it for that emphasis; the default for plain repeated actions stays the past simple.
</Callout>

## C — Narrative: the event and the situation around it

We often use the **past simple** in a narrative (a report or a story) to talk about a **single completed event**, and the **past continuous** to describe the **situation that existed at the time**. The event might have **interrupted** the situation, or the situation was simply in progress around it:

<SayIt text="Erika dropped her bag while she was getting into her car." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(The situation — getting into the car — was in progress; <em>dropped</em> is the single event inside it.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="She was shaking with anger as she left the hotel." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(The continuous paints the background state; <em>left</em> is the completed event.)</p>

## D — Things we intended to do, but didn't

We can use **either** the past continuous **or** the past simple (or the past perfect), even with a single completed event, to talk about something we **intended to do but didn't**:

<SayIt text="We were meaning to call in and see you, but Marc wasn't feeling well." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or We <em>meant</em> to call in …)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-amber-200 bg-amber-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-amber-700">Verbs that work this way</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-amber-900">consider + -ing · expect · hope to · intend · plan to / on + -ing · think about / of + -ing · want to</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-amber-800">e.g. <em>I <strong>was hoping</strong> to finish today</em> · <em>We <strong>were planning</strong> on leaving early</em> · <em>She <strong>was wanting</strong> to ask you a favour</em>.</p>
</div>

These verbs (with the exception of **mean** and **expect**), together with **wonder about**, can also appear in the **present or past continuous** to report what we **might do in the future**. The **past** continuous is **less definite** than the present continuous:

<SayIt text="I was thinking of going to China next year, but it depends how much money I've got." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(less definite than <em>I'm thinking of going …</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="We were wondering about inviting Eva over tomorrow." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(less definite than <em>We're wondering about …</em>)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-violet-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-violet-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-violet-900">Past continuous (was / were doing)</div>
    <div className="bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">Past simple (did)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">an activity <strong>in progress</strong> through a past period (<em>was reading, was living</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">a <strong>completed</strong> event, or completion stressed (<em>learned, passed</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">repeated actions over a <strong>temporary</strong> period / surprisingly often (<em>were visiting, was making</em>)</div>

  </div>
