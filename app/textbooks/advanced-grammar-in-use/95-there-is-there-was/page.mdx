## A — Introducing something new: *there + be*

When we introduce a new person or thing — to say that it **exists**, **happens**, or **is found in a particular place** — we start the clause with **there + be**. The grammatical subject is the empty *there*; the "real" subject comes after the verb.

<SayIt text="There was a loud bang from upstairs." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Not <em>A loud bang was from upstairs.</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="There's nothing to eat." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Not <em>Nothing is to eat.</em>)</p>

In questions we simply invert *be* and *there*:

<SayIt text="Is there anybody in here?" />

We also use *there* with **auxiliary and modal verbs + be**, with **verb + to be**, and with a few other verbs of existence:

<div className="not-prose my-6 space-y-3">
  <div className="rounded-xl border border-slate-200 bg-white p-4 shadow-sm">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-violet-700">auxiliary / modal + be</p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm text-slate-700">has been, can be, must be … — <em>There <strong>must be</strong> some way of contacting her.</em></p>
  </div>
  <div className="rounded-xl border border-slate-200 bg-white p-4 shadow-sm">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-700">verb + to be</p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm text-slate-700">used to be, is supposed to be, tends to be, appears/seems to be — <em>There <strong>appears to be</strong> a major disagreement between the two presidents.</em></p>
  </div>
  <div className="rounded-xl border border-slate-200 bg-white p-4 shadow-sm">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">other verbs of existence</p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm text-slate-700">arise, emerge, exist, remain — <em>During the 1990s there <strong>arose</strong> a demand for organic food.</em></p>
  </div>
</div>

Because *there + be* exists to **introduce** a topic, the noun that follows usually carries an **indefinite or non-specific** meaning. So it normally takes **a / an**, **zero article**, **any(one)**, **some(thing)**, **no(body)** — rather than **the**, **this**, **my**, **your**, or a **name**, which already point to something definite.

<SayIt text="There's nobody here." />
<br />
<SayIt text="There was something strange about her." />
<br />
<SayIt text="The cat was in the kitchen." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(More usual than <em>There was the cat in the kitchen</em> — but compare the indefinite <em>There was a cat in the kitchen.</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Jan is waiting for me outside." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(More usual than <em>There is Jan waiting for me outside.</em> — a name is already specific.)</p>

<Callout type="tip" title="When the + there is fine">
  We <em>do</em> use <strong>there + be + the / that</strong> to signal a deliberate <strong>change of topic</strong> — "and now, in addition, this point": <em>… And then there is <strong>the</strong> question of who is going to pay.</em> Here the definite article is doing work, not breaking the rule.
</Callout>

## B — Agreement: *is there* or *are there*?

The verb *be* agrees with the noun that **comes after it**, not with *there*.

<SayIt text="There is a very good reason for my decision." />
<br />
<SayIt text="There were too many people trying to get into the football stadium." />

<Callout type="warning" title="Informal there's">
  In casual <strong>speech</strong> you'll hear <em>there's</em> before a plural — <em>"Anything to eat?" "Well, <strong>there's</strong> some apples on the table."</em> Native speakers do it constantly, but in <strong>careful or written</strong> English use <em>there are</em> before a plural noun.
</Callout>

When the real subject is a **list** of nouns, the verb agrees with the **first** item: singular/uncountable → singular verb; plural → plural verb.

<SayIt text="When I opened the fridge there was only a bottle of milk, some eggs, and butter." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(First item <em>a bottle of milk</em> = singular → <em>was</em>.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="When I opened the fridge there were only some eggs, a bottle of milk, and butter." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(First item <em>some eggs</em> = plural → <em>were</em>.)</p>

## C — *there + be* followed by a clause

*There + be* often introduces a noun that is then expanded by a **that-**, **wh-**, **to-infinitive**, or **-ing** clause.

<div className="not-prose my-6 space-y-2 text-sm">
  <p className="rounded-lg bg-slate-50 px-4 py-2 text-slate-700"><span className="font-semibold text-violet-700">that-</span> &nbsp; Is there a chance <em>(that)</em> Kim could arrive this afternoon?</p>
  <p className="rounded-lg bg-slate-50 px-4 py-2 text-slate-700"><span className="font-semibold text-violet-700">wh-</span> &nbsp; There is no reason <em>(why)</em> I can't see you tomorrow.</p>
  <p className="rounded-lg bg-slate-50 px-4 py-2 text-slate-700"><span className="font-semibold text-violet-700">relative</span> &nbsp; There is a small stream <em>which / that</em> runs at the bottom of the garden.</p>
  <p className="rounded-lg bg-slate-50 px-4 py-2 text-slate-700"><span className="font-semibold text-violet-700">to-infinitive</span> &nbsp; There was an attempt <em>to resolve</em> the dispute at the factory.</p>
  <p className="rounded-lg bg-slate-50 px-4 py-2 text-slate-700"><span className="font-semibold text-violet-700">-ing</span> &nbsp; There was a taxi <em>waiting</em> outside the hotel.</p>
</div>

<Callout type="info" title="Leaving out the relative pronoun">
  Keep the pronoun when it is the <strong>subject</strong> of the following verb, but you may drop it when it is the <strong>object</strong> (see Unit 53).<br />
  ✅ There is a stream <strong>that</strong> runs … <span className="text-slate-400">(subject — keep it)</span><br />
  ✅ There's a cake <strong>(that)</strong> I made … <span className="text-slate-400">(object — drop it freely)</span>
</Callout>

## D — *There being …* (formal reason clauses)

In **formal** English a clause beginning **there being** can introduce the **reason** for something — a compact alternative to *Because there was …*.

<SayIt text="There being no evidence against him, he was released." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= Because there was no evidence against him.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="There being no reports of adverse reactions, the drug is to be sold more widely." />

<Callout type="tip" title="How to build it">
  Take the <em>there was / there were</em> clause, replace the finite verb with <strong>being</strong>, and attach it to the main clause: <em>There was no time</em> → <em><strong>There being</strong> no time, we left.</em>
</Callout>
