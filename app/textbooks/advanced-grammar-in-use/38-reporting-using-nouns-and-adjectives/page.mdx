<Lesson textbook="advanced-grammar-in-use" slug="38-reporting-using-nouns-and-adjectives">

When we report what people say or think, we don't have to start with a reporting **verb** (*she said…, he claimed…*). We can also pack the report into a **noun** (*her claim that…, the question of whether…*) or an **adjective** (*she was adamant that…, they were critical of…*). This unit is about those two patterns — and the small but reliable rules that govern what comes after each noun or adjective.

<Callout type="info" title="One idea to hold on to">
  Reporting with a noun or adjective rarely reproduces the speaker's exact words. We use our own words instead, or we report <em>that</em> something was said without saying <em>what</em> — <em>the jury reached a conclusion</em> tells you a conclusion exists, not what it was.
</Callout>

## A — Reporting using nouns

We sometimes report other people's words and thoughts with a **noun** in the reporting clause, followed by a reported clause beginning with **that**, a **to-infinitive**, or a **wh-word**. Most of these nouns are related to reporting verbs (*acknowledgement → acknowledge*, *statement → state*).

**Noun + that-clause**

<SayIt text="The claim is often made that smoking causes heart disease." />
<br />
<SayIt text="The jury came to the conclusion that the woman was guilty." />

<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Also: <em>acknowledgement, advice, allegation, announcement, answer, argument, comment, decision, explanation, forecast, guarantee, indication, observation, promise, recommendation, reply, speculation, statement, suggestion, threat, warning</em>.</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't drop the 'that'">
  After these nouns we don't usually leave out <em>that</em> the way we can after a reporting verb. ✅ <em>the claim <strong>that</strong> smoking causes heart disease</em> — ❌ <em>the claim smoking causes heart disease</em>. (See Unit 33 on omitting <em>that</em> after verbs.)
</Callout>

**Noun + to-infinitive clause**

<SayIt text="I accepted Luisa's invitation to visit her in Rome." />
<br />
<SayIt text="He gave me every encouragement to take up painting again." />

<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Also: <em>decision, instruction, order, promise, recommendation, refusal, threat, warning</em>.</p>

Some of these nouns can take **either** a to-infinitive **or** a that-clause:

<SayIt text="They carried out their threat to dismiss workers on strike." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">or … <em>their threat <strong>that they would</strong> dismiss workers on strike.</em></p>

**Noun + wh-clause**

<SayIt text="Juan raised the question of when the money would be collected." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Our previous meeting looked at the issue of how to increase income." />

<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Also: <em>explanation, discussion, problem</em>.</p>

<Callout type="tip" title="The 'of' before a wh-word">
  We usually put <strong>of</strong> after the noun when a wh-word follows: <em>the question <strong>of</strong> when…</em>, <em>the issue <strong>of</strong> how…</em>, <em>the problem <strong>of</strong> who…</em>. The <em>of</em> isn't optional here.
</Callout>

## B — *as to* + a wh-clause

After many of the nouns in A we can use **as to** + a **wh-clause**, or **as to** + a **wh-word** + a **to-infinitive**, to introduce the subject of a question or topic discussed or thought about. An alternative preposition can usually replace **as to**:

<SayIt text="She asked my advice as to what subject she should study at university." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">or … <em>advice <strong>on</strong> what subject…</em></p>
<br />
<SayIt text="There was some discussion as to whether the price included tax or not." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">or … <em>discussion <strong>of</strong> whether…</em></p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Before we left we gave them strict instructions as to how to cook it." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">or … <em>instructions <strong>about</strong> how to cook it.</em></p>

<Callout type="info" title="When to reach for 'as to'">
  <em>as to</em> is most natural before <em>whether</em> and other wh-words, especially after nouns of discussion and uncertainty — <em>argument, speculation, doubt, question, discussion, instruction, advice</em>. It often sounds a touch formal; <em>of / on / about</em> are lighter everyday alternatives. (See also Unit 34 on wh-clauses.)
</Callout>

## C — Reporting using adjectives

Some adjectives report a speaker's **feelings or opinion** and are followed by a **that-clause**:

<SayIt text="The builders are certain that they'll be finished by the end of next week." />

<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Also: <em>adamant, agreed, angry, annoyed, grateful, insistent, sure</em>.</p>

Adjectives expressing **uncertainty** are usually followed by a **wh-clause**:

<SayIt text="Scientists aren't sure where the remains of the satellite will land." />

<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Also: <em>doubtful</em> (usually + <em>whether</em>), <em>uncertain, not certain, unsure</em>.</p>

Some adjectives are usually followed by a **preposition + noun phrase**:

<SayIt text="Today's newspapers are very critical of the President's decision to appoint Mr Walters." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 grid gap-3 sm:grid-cols-3">
  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-amber-200 bg-amber-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-amber-700">+ about</p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-amber-900">apologetic, complimentary, insulting, tactful</p>
  </div>
  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-rose-200 bg-rose-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-rose-700">+ of</p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-rose-900">critical, dismissive, scornful</p>
  </div>
  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-sky-200 bg-sky-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-700">+ to / towards</p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-sky-900">abusive, sympathetic</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout type="warning" title="The preposition is fixed">
  The preposition belongs to the adjective and can't be swapped freely: ✅ <em>dismissive <strong>of</strong></em> / ❌ <em>dismissive about</em>; ✅ <em>complimentary <strong>about</strong></em> / ❌ <em>complimentary of</em>; ✅ <em>abusive <strong>towards</strong></em> / ❌ <em>abusive of</em>. Learn each adjective together with its preposition.
</Callout>
