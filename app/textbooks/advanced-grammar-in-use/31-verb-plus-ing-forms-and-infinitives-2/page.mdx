Unit 30 sorted out *which* verbs take an *-ing* form and which take a *to*-infinitive. This unit deals with the next layer of difficulty: **what comes between the verb and the infinitive**. Some verbs insist on an object first (*warn someone to ...*); others refuse one (*decide to ...*). Some demand a preposition (*arrange **for** ..., depend **on** ...*). And the infinitive itself can be made negative (*not to ...*) or pushed back into the past (*to have done*). Getting these patterns right is largely a matter of knowing which verb belongs to which family.

## A — Object before the to-infinitive, or no object?

After some verbs we **must** include an object before a *to*-infinitive in active sentences:

<SayIt text="The police warned everyone to stay inside with their windows closed." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<span className="text-rose-600">not</span> The police warned to stay inside …)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">Verb + object + to-infinitive</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">advise · allow · believe · cause · command · enable · encourage · entitle · force · invite · order · persuade · remind · show · teach · tell</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-emerald-800">These verbs need someone to aim the infinitive at: <em>tell <strong>her</strong> to wait</em>, <em>persuade <strong>him</strong> to stay</em>, <em>remind <strong>me</strong> to call</em>.</p>
</div>

After other verbs, however, we **can't** put an object before the *to*-infinitive:

<SayIt text="We've decided to leave early." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<span className="text-rose-600">not</span> We've decided us to leave early.)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-sky-200 bg-sky-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-700">Verb + to-infinitive (no object)</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-sky-900">agree · consent · fail · hope · manage · offer · pretend · refuse · start · threaten · volunteer</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-sky-800">The subject does the infinitive themselves: <em>I agreed <strong>to help</strong></em>, <em>she managed <strong>to finish</strong></em>, <em>they offered <strong>to pay</strong></em>.</p>
</div>

<Callout type="tip" title="Same verb, both lists?">
  A handful of verbs (such as <strong>ask, expect, want, would like</strong>) can go either way — <em>I want to leave</em> or <em>I want you to leave</em>. But many cannot: <em>decide, hope, manage, offer</em> take no object, while <em>persuade, remind, force, entitle</em> require one. When in doubt, check whether you can rephrase the action onto a second person.
</Callout>

## B — Preposition + object + to-infinitive

After some verbs we have to put a preposition — usually **for** — immediately after the verb, before an **object + to-infinitive** (see also Unit 29):

<SayIt text="They arranged for Rania to stay in London." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<span className="text-rose-600">not</span> They arranged Rania to stay …)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="They applied for the court appearance to be postponed." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-amber-200 bg-amber-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-amber-700">Verb + for + object + to-infinitive</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-amber-900">advertise · apply · arrange · campaign · long · plan · wait</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-amber-800">After <strong>apply</strong> and <strong>campaign</strong> the to-infinitive is usually passive: <em>they applied for the case <strong>to be</strong> reviewed</em>.</p>
</div>

Other verbs are followed by **different** prepositions before object + to-infinitive:

<SayIt text="I shouted at the man to open the door." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1"><strong>at</strong> + object → go on [= criticise continually], keep on [= mention repeatedly], scream, shout, yell</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="We're depending on you to find a solution soon." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1"><strong>on</strong> + object → call [= officially ask someone to do something], count, depend, prevail, rely</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="He closed the door and signalled to the pilot to take off." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1"><strong>to</strong> + object → appeal, gesture, motion, signal</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't drop the preposition">
  These verbs cannot reach their object directly: <em>rely <span className="text-rose-600">on</span> Greta</em>, <em>wait <span className="text-rose-600">for</span> her</em>, <em>appeal <span className="text-rose-600">to</span> workers</em>, <em>prevail <span className="text-rose-600">on</span> the strikers</em>. Leaving the preposition out (<em>rely Greta to …</em>) is one of the most common slips at this level.
</Callout>

## C — Negative infinitives, and infinitives about the past

A number of other to-infinitive and *-ing* forms can follow verbs.

**Negative to-infinitive and negative -ing form.** Put **not** *before* the *to* or the *-ing*:

<SayIt text="We decided not to go to Paris after all." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Some of my friends have considered not going to college because of the cost." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Compare the affirmatives: <em>We decided to go …</em> / <em>I haven't considered going …</em>)</p>

**Verb + to have + past participle.** Use this to talk about an *earlier* event:

<SayIt text="The accident seems to have happened at around 1 pm yesterday." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Simmons is alleged to have assaulted a police officer." />

<Callout type="info" title="Two jobs for to have + past participle">
  This form is used to <strong>give an opinion</strong> about a past event (after verbs like <em>seem</em> and <em>appear</em>), or to <strong>report</strong> what is or was said (after passive verbs like <em>is / was alleged, believed, said, thought</em>): <em>The Etruscans <strong>are believed to have arrived</strong> in Italy in the 8th century BC.</em>
</Callout>

**Verb + having + past participle.** With a small set of verbs, *verb + -ing* and *verb + having + past participle* mean much the same thing:

<SayIt text="I now regret buying the car." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I now regret having bought the car." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Both natural — same meaning.)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-violet-200 bg-violet-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-violet-700">-ing ⇄ having + past participle with</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-violet-900">admit · deny · forget · recall · regret · remember</p>
</div>
