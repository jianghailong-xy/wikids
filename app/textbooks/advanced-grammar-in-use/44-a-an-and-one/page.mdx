The choice between **a** and **an** is decided by *sound*, not by spelling — and the choice between **a / an** and **one** turns on whether you simply mean "some unspecified thing" or you want to put real weight on the number *one*. This unit sharpens both decisions, including the cases where either word will do.

## A — *a* or *an*? Listen, don't look

We use **a** before a noun or noun phrase that begins with a **consonant sound** — even when it is *spelt* with a vowel letter:

<SayIt text="a university" />
<SayIt text="a European passport" />
<SayIt text="a one-parent family" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<em>university</em> and <em>European</em> start /j.../, <em>one</em> starts /w.../ — all consonant sounds.)</p>

We use **an** before a noun that begins with a **vowel sound**, including a word with a silent letter *h*:

<SayIt text="an orange" /> &nbsp; <SayIt text="an Italian" /> &nbsp; <SayIt text="an umbrella" /> &nbsp; <SayIt text="an hour" /> &nbsp; <SayIt text="an honour" />

We also use **an** before abbreviations said as **individual letters** when the first letter's *name* begins with a vowel sound — that is, the letters **A, E, F, H, I, L, M, N, O, R, S** and **X**:

<SayIt text="an MP" /> &nbsp; <SayIt text="an FBI agent" /> &nbsp; <SayIt text="an IOU" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(M = /em/, F = /ef/, I = /aɪ/ — each letter <em>name</em> opens with a vowel sound.)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="Said as letters vs said as a word">
  The same string of capitals takes <strong>a</strong> or <strong>an</strong> depending on how you pronounce it.<br /><br />
  Said as letters: <em>an MP</em>, <em>an FBI agent</em>. But said as a word: <em><strong>a</strong> NATO general</em> (/neɪtəʊ/), <em><strong>a</strong> FIFA official</em> (/fiːfə/) — yet <em><strong>an</strong> OPEC meeting</em> (/əʊpek/), because that word still opens with a vowel sound.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="The h words">
  Note that we say <strong>a history</strong> (book) but <strong>a</strong> <em>or</em> <strong>an historical</strong> (novel). With <em>historical, horrific, hotel</em> and a few others where the first syllable is unstressed, the older <em>an</em> survives alongside the now-more-common <em>a</em>.
</Callout>

## B — *a / an*, not *one*, for an unspecified thing

We use **a / an** (not *one*) to talk about a **particular but unspecified** person, thing or event:

<SayIt text="I really need a cup of coffee." />
<br />
<SayIt text="You never see a police officer in this part of town, do you?" />

We also use **a / an**, not *one*, in many **number and quantity expressions**:

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">a / an in measurements</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-emerald-900"><strong>three times a year</strong> · <strong>half an hour</strong> · <strong>a quarter of an hour</strong> · <strong>a day or so</strong> (= "about a day")</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-emerald-900"><strong>50 cents a litre</strong> (= each) · <strong>a week or two</strong> · <strong>a few</strong> · <strong>a little</strong> · <strong>a huge number of …</strong></p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-emerald-800">We <em>can</em> also say "50 cents <strong>for one</strong> litre" or "<strong>one</strong> week or two weeks" — but in the bare pattern it is <em>a / an</em>.</p>
</div>

And we use **a** rather than *one* in the pattern **a … of …** with possessives:

<SayIt text="She's a colleague of mine." />
<br />
<SayIt text="That's a friend of Gabriel's." />

## C — *one* when the number really matters

Before a singular countable noun, **one** and **a / an** can both refer to a single thing — but **one** adds a little more emphasis to the length of time, quantity or amount:

<SayIt text="We'll be in Australia for one year." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or "… for a year." — <em>one</em> stresses the span slightly more.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="He weighs one hundred and twenty kilos! Would you believe it!" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<em>one</em> presses on the figure more than <em>a</em> would.)</p>

We use **one** rather than *a / an* when we want to stress that we mean **only one** thing or person, not two or more:

<SayIt text="Can I have one sandwich, or two?" />
<br />
<SayIt text="Are you staying only one night?" />
<br />
<SayIt text="I just took one look at her and she started crying." />

We use **one**, not *a / an*, in the pattern **one … other / another**:

<SayIt text="Close one eye, and then the other." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Bees carry pollen from one plant to another." />

And we use **one** in phrases such as **one day, one evening, one spring** to mean a *particular but unspecified* day, evening or season:

<SayIt text="Hope to see you again one day." />
<br />
<SayIt text="One evening, while he was working late at the office …" />

  </div>
</div>
