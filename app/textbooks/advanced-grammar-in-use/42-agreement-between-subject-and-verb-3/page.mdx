A verb usually agrees with its subject in number: a singular subject takes a singular verb, a plural one a plural verb. The difficulty comes with subjects that *look* one way but *behave* the other — a plural-looking word that is grammatically singular, a singular-looking word made of many parts, or a measurement that is really a single quantity. This third unit on agreement gathers the awkward cases: **plural-only nouns**, **plural forms taken as singular**, **nouns ending in -s that are singular**, and the agreement of **measurements, percentages and fractions**.

## A — Plural nouns

Some nouns are **usually plural** and take a **plural verb**. They have no ordinary singular form:

<SayIt text="The company's earnings have increased for the last five years." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">Also usually plural — take a plural verb</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">belongings · clothes · congratulations · goods · outskirts · overheads · particulars (= information) · premises (= building) · riches · savings · stairs · surroundings · thanks</p>
</div>

<Callout type="warning" title="Three nouns that bend the rule">
  <strong>whereabouts</strong> can be used with <strong>either</strong> a singular or a plural verb: <em>His whereabouts <span className="font-semibold">are / is</span> unknown.</em><br /><br />
  <strong>police</strong> and <strong>people</strong> always take a <strong>plural</strong> verb, and <strong>staff</strong> usually does:<br />
  <em>Police <span className="font-semibold">believe</span> that Thomas is in Brazil.</em> · <em>Staff <span className="font-semibold">say</span> the new system has raised stress levels.</em>
</Callout>

## B — Plural nouns used with singular verbs

Although the words **data** and **media** (= newspapers, television, etc.) are plural (their singulars are **datum** and **medium**), they are commonly used with a **singular** verb. However, in formal contexts such as academic writing a **plural** verb is preferred.

<SayIt text="All the data is available for public inspection." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or, more formally, <em>All the data <strong>are</strong> available …</em>)</p>

Other similar Latin/Greek plurals such as **criteria** and **phenomena** (singulars **criterion** and **phenomenon**) are **always** used with a **plural** verb:

<SayIt text="I agree that the criteria are not of equal importance." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>… the criteria <strong>is</strong> not …</em>)</p>

<Callout type="info" title="Keep the singular / plural pairs straight">
  one <strong>criterion</strong> → several <strong>criteria</strong> · one <strong>phenomenon</strong> → several <strong>phenomena</strong> · one <strong>datum</strong> → much <strong>data</strong> · one <strong>medium</strong> → the <strong>media</strong>. With <em>criteria</em> and <em>phenomena</em> the plural verb is fixed; with <em>data</em> and <em>media</em> the singular verb is now normal in everyday use.
</Callout>

## C — Plural nouns ending in -s

Some **uncountable** nouns always end in **-s** and *look* as if they are plural, but when they are the subject they take a **singular** verb:

<SayIt text="The news from the Middle East seems very encouraging." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-sky-200 bg-sky-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-700">Also -s but singular</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-sky-900">means (= 'method' or 'money') · economics · linguistics · mathematics · phonetics · physics · politics · statistics · athletics · gymnastics · diabetes · measles · rabies</p>
</div>

However, some of these words can also be used in a more **general** sense — and then they are plural. Compare the academic subject (singular) with the everyday meaning (plural):

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-violet-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-violet-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-violet-900">Academic subject — singular verb</div>
    <div className="bg-amber-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-amber-900">General use — plural verb</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><strong>Politics</strong> <em>is</em> popular at this university.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-amber-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">Her <strong>politics</strong> <em>are</em> bordering on the fascist. <span className="text-slate-400">(= political beliefs)</span></div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><strong>Statistics</strong> <em>was</em> always my worst subject.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-amber-100 bg-amber-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><strong>Statistics</strong> <em>are</em> able to prove anything you want. <span className="text-slate-400">(= numerical information)</span></div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><strong>Economics</strong> <em>has</em> only recently been recognised as a science.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-amber-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">The <strong>economics</strong> behind their policies <em>are</em> unreasonable. <span className="text-slate-400">(= the financial system)</span></div>
  </div>
</div>

## D — Agreement with measurements, percentages, etc.

With a phrase referring to a **measurement, amount or quantity** we usually prefer a **singular** verb, even when the subject is plural in form:

<SayIt text="Only three metres separates the runners in first and second place." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(rather than <em>… three metres <strong>separate</strong> …</em>)</p>

And a **singular verb must be used** when the complement is a singular noun phrase (e.g. *a long time*):

<SayIt text="Three hours seems a long time to take on the homework." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>Three hours <strong>seem</strong> …</em>)</p>

When the first noun in a **complex subject** is a **percentage or a fraction**, the verb agrees with the **noun nearest to it**. Contrast that with a subject whose head is an ordinary noun, where the verb agrees with that **main noun**:

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-rose-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-1 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-rose-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-rose-900">The verb agrees with the <em>nearest</em> noun after a % or fraction</div>
    <div className="border-t border-rose-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">About 50% / half of the <strong>houses</strong> <em>need</em> major repairs. <span className="text-slate-400">(closest noun = houses → plural)</span></div>
    <div className="border-t border-rose-100 bg-rose-50/40 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">An inflation <strong>rate</strong> of only 2% <em>makes</em> a difference to exports. <span className="text-slate-400">(head noun = rate → singular)</span></div>
  </div>
</div>

Where we use a singular noun that can be thought of as **either** a whole unit **or** a collection of individuals, we can use **either** a singular or a plural verb:

<SayIt text="Some 80% of the electorate is expected to vote." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or <em>… <strong>are</strong> expected to vote.</em> — both are fine)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-slate-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-emerald-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-emerald-900">Plural verb</div>
    <div className="bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">Singular verb</div>
    <div className="border-t border-emerald-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">plural-only nouns (<em>earnings, outskirts, premises, belongings</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">an -s subject that is really uncountable (<em>news, economics, measles</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-emerald-100 bg-emerald-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>criteria, phenomena</em>; <em>police, people</em></div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">a measurement / amount (<em>three metres, three hours</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-emerald-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">after a % / fraction + <strong>plural</strong> noun (<em>half of the houses</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">after a % / fraction + <strong>singular</strong> noun (<em>30% of the space</em>)</div>
  </div>
</div>
