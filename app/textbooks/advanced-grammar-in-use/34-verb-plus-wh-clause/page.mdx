## A — Verb + wh-clause

Many verbs can be followed by a clause that begins with a **wh-word** — *how, what, when, where, which, who,* or *why*. The whole wh-clause behaves as the object of the verb, reporting a question or an area of uncertainty rather than a plain fact.

<SayIt text="That might explain why he's unhappy." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I couldn't decide which train to catch." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Let's consider how we can solve the problem." />

Many of these verbs can **also** be followed by a *that*-clause (Unit 33):

<SayIt text="I decided that I ought to leave." />

And after most of them a **wh-word (except *why*) + to-infinitive** is possible:

<SayIt text="Did you find out where to go?" />

<Callout type="warning" title="The trap: a subject blocks the to-infinitive">
  As soon as the wh-clause has its <strong>own subject</strong>, drop the to-infinitive and use a full finite clause. <br />
  ✅ I can't imagine <strong>what he likes</strong> about jazz. &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ I can't imagine what to like about jazz. <br />
  The to-infinitive form means roughly <em>"what one should do"</em>; once you name <em>who</em>, you need a tensed verb.
</Callout>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-2xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-5 shadow-sm">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">Verbs that take a wh-clause directly (no object)</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm font-semibold leading-relaxed text-emerald-900">arrange · calculate · check · choose · debate · decide · determine · discover · discuss · establish · explain · find out · forget · guess · imagine · know · learn · notice · plan · realise · remember · say · see · talk about · think (about) · understand · wonder</p>
</div>

## B — Verbs that need an object before the wh-clause

Some verbs must have an **object** between the verb and the wh-clause — they tell, remind or warn *somebody* something:

<SayIt text="She reminded me what I had to do." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I told Linda how to get to my house." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-2xl border-2 border-rose-200 bg-rose-50 p-5 shadow-sm">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-rose-700">Always need an object first</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm font-semibold leading-relaxed text-rose-900">advise · inform · instruct · remind · teach · tell · warn</p>
</div>

The verbs **ask** and **show** often take an object before a wh-clause, but not always:

<SayIt text="I asked (him) how I could get to the station, and he told me." />

These verbs can also be followed by **object + wh-word + to-infinitive**:

<SayIt text="She taught me how to play chess." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I showed her where to put her coat." />

<Callout type="tip" title="Two patterns, one meaning">
  With <em>teach / show / tell / remind</em> the object comes first, then the wh-word: <em>show <strong>her</strong> where to sit</em>, <em>tell <strong>me</strong> what happened</em>. Leave out the object and the sentence breaks — ❌ <s>I told what happened.</s>
</Callout>

## C — *the way* instead of *how*

We can often use **the way** in place of **how** when we refer to either a **route** or a **method**:

<SayIt text="Go back the way you came." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= the way <em>that</em> / <em>by which</em> you came; or informally <em>Go back how you came.</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Have you noticed the way he spins the ball?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= the way <em>that</em> / <em>in which</em> he spins it; or <em>... noticed how he spins ...?</em>)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="Never 'the way how'">
  Use <strong>the way</strong> or <strong>how</strong> — not both together. <br />
  ✅ Go back <strong>the way</strong> you came. &nbsp; ✅ Go back <strong>how</strong> you came. &nbsp; ❌ Go back the way how you came.
</Callout>

## D — *whether*: presenting a choice

We can use **whether** as the wh-word when we want to show possible **choices**. In a finite clause, **whether** is close in meaning to *if* (Unit 86):

<SayIt text="He couldn't remember whether / if he had turned the computer off." />

Some verbs take **whether + to-infinitive** to talk about a choice between two or more possibilities. Note that *if* is **never** used before a to-infinitive:

<SayIt text="You have 14 days to decide whether to keep it or not." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Not <em>... to decide <s>if to keep</s> it or not.</em>)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 grid gap-3 sm:grid-cols-2">
  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-sky-200 bg-sky-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-700">✅ Take <em>whether + to-infinitive</em></p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-sky-900">choose · consider · debate · decide · determine · discuss · know <span className="font-normal text-sky-700">(in questions &amp; negatives)</span> · think about · wonder</p>
  </div>
  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-amber-200 bg-amber-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-amber-700">❌ Do <em>not</em> take <em>whether + to-infinitive</em></p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-amber-900">ask · conclude · explain · imagine · realise · speculate · think</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout type="info" title="Why these two groups split">
  The verbs on the left frame an <em>open decision</em> — you are weighing options — so <em>whether to ...</em> fits. The verbs on the right report <em>talking or thinking</em> without that decision frame, so they need a finite clause: ✅ He <strong>explained whether the plan would work</strong>, not ❌ <s>explained whether to work the plan</s>.
</Callout>

## E — *whether*-clause vs *that*-clause

The choice between **whether** and **that** changes the meaning. **whether** leaves the outcome open; **that** presents it as a fact (Unit 33):

<SayIt text="I didn't know whether the shop was shut." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= I had no idea — it may have been shut <em>or not</em>.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="I didn't know that the shop was shut." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Suggests the shop <em>was</em> shut — that's the fact I was unaware of.)</p>

## F — *as to* before a wh-clause (formal writing)

In rather formal contexts, particularly in writing, we can use **as to** with a meaning similar to *about* or *concerning* before a wh-clause. It is most common before **whether**:

<SayIt text="Opinion was divided as to whether the findings from the study were representative of the population as a whole." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Or, less formally, <em>... divided whether ...</em>)</p>

<Callout type="tip" title="Register check">
  <em>as to whether</em> belongs to reports, essays and formal speech (<em>questions remain <strong>as to whether</strong> ...</em>, <em>there is doubt <strong>as to who</strong> ...</em>). In conversation, drop it: <em>I'm not sure <strong>whether</strong> we should go.</em>
</Callout>
