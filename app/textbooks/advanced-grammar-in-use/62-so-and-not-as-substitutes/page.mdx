Rather than repeat a whole phrase or clause that has just been said, English lets a single word stand in for it. The two workhorses are **so** and **not** — *I think so*, *I hope not*, *So it is*. This unit sorts out which verbs take **so**, which reject it, how the negative behaves, and two short-answer patterns that look identical but mean opposite things.

## A — So in place of an adjective, adverb, or a whole clause

We can use **so** instead of repeating an **adjective**, an **adverb**, or a **whole clause**:

<SayIt text="The workers were angry, and they had every right to be so." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(so = angry — it stands in for the adjective)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Joe took the work seriously, and Petra perhaps even more so." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(so = took the work seriously)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Usman's giving us a lift. At least, I presume so." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(so = that he's giving us a lift — it replaces the whole clause)</p>

<Callout type="info" title="What 'so' is doing here">
  Each <em>so</em> is a substitute — a pro-form — that saves you repeating words already in play. Read it as "the thing we've just been talking about". It points <strong>backwards</strong> to something already said, never forwards.
</Callout>

## B — So after verbs of thinking and speaking — and the verbs that refuse it

We often use **so** instead of a clause after verbs concerned with **thinking** and **speaking**:

<SayIt text="'Is Lewis going back to Scotland to see his parents this summer?' 'I've no idea, but I'd imagine so. He goes most years.'" />
<br />
<SayIt text="I found it ridiculous, and said so." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I found it ridiculous, and told them so." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(said / told them <em>so</em> = that I found it ridiculous)</p>

<Callout type="tip" title="Tell needs an object">
  After <strong>tell</strong> we must include an indirect object: <em>I told <strong>them</strong> so</em> — not <em>I told so</em>. <strong>Say</strong> takes no object: <em>I said so</em>.
</Callout>

These verbs take **so**:

<div className="not-prose my-4 rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">✓ verb + so</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">imagine, believe, expect, guess, hope, presume, suppose, suspect, think, assume · appear / seem (after <em>it</em>) · be afraid (expressing regret) · say, tell (+ object)</p>
</div>

But we **don't** use **so** after certain other verbs. Instead we use **it** or a full **that-clause**:

<SayIt text="'Will Stefan know how to mend it?' 'I doubt it. / I doubt (that) he will.'" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(not <s>I doubt so.</s>)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-4 rounded-xl border-2 border-rose-200 bg-rose-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-rose-700">✗ never + so → use <em>it</em> / a that-clause</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-rose-900">accept, admit, agree, be certain, be sure, hear, know, promise, suggest</p>
</div>

<Callout type="warning" title="One informal exception">
  In informal English, particularly in an argument, you may hear <em>I <strong>know</strong> so</em> — "I just know it's true." Treat it as colloquial; in neutral or written English, <em>know</em> still rejects <em>so</em> (<em>I know he will</em>, not <s>I know so</s>).
</Callout>

## C — Negatives: not, or not … so

In negative sentences we use **not**, or **not … so**:

<SayIt text="'Is the Socialist Party offering anything new in its statement?' 'It would appear not.'" />
<br />
<SayIt text="They want to buy the house, although they didn't say so directly." />

Which pattern you choose depends on the verb:

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-violet-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-[1fr_1.4fr] text-sm">
    <div className="bg-violet-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-violet-900">verb</div>
    <div className="bg-violet-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-violet-900">negative reply</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-slate-700">appear, seem, suppose</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><strong>either</strong> form — <em>I don't suppose so.</em> / <em>I suppose not.</em></div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/50 px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-slate-700">believe, expect, imagine, think</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">prefer <strong>not … so</strong> — <em>I don't think so.</em> (<em>I think not</em> = formal)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-slate-700">be afraid, assume, guess, hope, presume, suspect</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><strong>not</strong> only — <em>I guess not.</em> (not <s>I don't guess so</s>)</div>
  </div>
</div>

<SayIt text="'I don't suppose there'll be any seats left.' 'No, I don't suppose so.' (or '… I suppose not.')" />
<br />
<SayIt text="'Will we need to show our passports?' 'I don't think so.' (rather than 'I think not.')" />
<br />
<SayIt text="'You'd better do it yourself. Eva won't help.' 'No, I guess not.'" />

**Say** is special — compare **not (to)** with **not … so**:

<div className="not-prose my-6 space-y-3">
  <div className="rounded-xl border border-slate-200 bg-white p-4 shadow-sm">
    <p className="text-sm text-slate-600">'Do we have to do all ten questions?' '<strong>The teacher said not.</strong>' <span className="text-slate-400">= the teacher said we didn't have to</span></p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm text-slate-600">'… <strong>The teacher said not to.</strong>' <span className="text-slate-400">= the teacher said we shouldn't</span></p>
  </div>
  <div className="rounded-xl border border-slate-200 bg-white p-4 shadow-sm">
    <p className="text-sm text-slate-600">'Do we have to do all ten questions?' '<strong>The teacher didn't say so.</strong>' <span className="text-slate-400">= the teacher didn't say we should — but perhaps we should</span></p>
  </div>
</div>

## D — "So + pronoun + auxiliary": noticing something now

We can use **so** in a short answer — instead of *Yes, …* — to say that **we can now see something is true, now that we've been told**, often when we're **surprised**. The pattern is **so + pronoun + auxiliary verb** (*be, can, have*, etc.):

<SayIt text="'Lisa and Sara are here.' 'So they are!' (= I can now see that too — I hadn't realised)" />

However, if we **already knew**, we use *Yes, …*, not *So …*. Compare:

<SayIt text="'Your bike's been moved.' 'So it has! / Yes, it has. I wonder who did it.'" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= I didn't know before you told me — discovery)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="'Your bike's been moved.' 'Yes, it has. Philip borrowed it this morning.'" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= I knew before you told me — so <s>So it has</s> is impossible here)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="The test for 'So + pronoun + auxiliary'">
  Use it <strong>only</strong> when the news is new to you and you can now confirm it for yourself. The moment you reveal you knew all along (<em>Philip borrowed it</em>), you must switch to <em>Yes, …</em>.
</Callout>

## E — "So + I + reporting verb": I already knew

We can use **so** in a similar short answer with the **reporting verbs** *appear* (after *it*), *believe, gather, hear, say, seem, tell* (e.g. *So she tells me*), *understand*. But here the pattern carries the **opposite** implication — **"I knew before you told me"**:

<SayIt text="'I found that lecture really boring.' 'So I gather — I saw you sleeping!' (= I already knew that)" />
<br />
<SayIt text="'My car won't start again.' 'So I hear.'" />
<br />
<SayIt text="'Tom and Mia are getting married.' 'So she tells me.'" />

<Callout type="info" title="D and E look alike — they mean opposites">
  Both start with <strong>So …</strong>, but the word after <em>So</em> decides the meaning:
  <br />• <strong>So + pronoun + auxiliary</strong> (<em>So they are, So it has</em>) = I've <strong>just</strong> noticed / been told — it's news.
  <br />• <strong>So + I + reporting verb</strong> (<em>So I hear, So I gather</em>) = I'd <strong>already</strong> heard — it's old news.
</Callout>
