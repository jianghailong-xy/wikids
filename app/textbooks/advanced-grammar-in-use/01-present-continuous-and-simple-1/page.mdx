You already know the broad division: the **present continuous** (*am / is / are + -ing*) for what is in progress, temporary or changing, and the **present simple** (*I do / he does*) for what is permanent, habitual or generally true. This unit is about the fine print — the verbs that cross the line. Many so-called *state verbs* can in fact take the continuous, and when they do, the meaning shifts in a precise, learnable way.

## A — State verbs in the continuous for emphasis

We can use the present continuous with some state verbs — for example **attract, like, look, love, sound** — to emphasise that a situation is **temporary** or true **for a period of time around the present**, rather than permanently. Compare:

<SayIt text="Ella stays with us quite often. The children like having her here." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(A general, repeated arrangement — present simple.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Ella's with us at the moment. The children are loving having her here." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Just now, this visit — the continuous stresses that it is temporary.)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="Some state verbs stay in the simple">
  A handful of state verbs we <strong>rarely</strong> use in the present continuous at all, even for a temporary feeling — including <strong>believe, consist of, doubt</strong> and <strong>own</strong>. Say <em>I believe you</em>, <em>It consists of three parts</em>, <em>I doubt it</em>, <em>She owns the flat</em> — not the <em>-ing</em> forms.
</Callout>

## B — State meaning vs action meaning

Some verbs have **different meanings** when they describe a *state* and when they describe an *action*. With their **state** meaning they usually take simple, not continuous, forms. With their **action** meaning they may take either simple or continuous forms, depending on context. Compare:

<SayIt text="The app doesn't appear to work on my phone." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<em>appear</em> = seem — a state, so present simple.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Carley Robb is currently appearing in a musical on Broadway." />
<br />
<SayIt text="She often appears in musicals." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<em>appear</em> = take part, perform — an action, so simple or continuous.)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-amber-200 bg-amber-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-amber-700">Also behave this way</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-amber-900">cost · expect · feel · fit · have · imagine · measure · think · weigh</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-amber-800">Each splits into a fixed-state sense (simple) and a doing-it-right-now sense (often continuous): <em>The bag weighs 2 kg</em> vs <em>The vet is weighing the cat</em>; <em>This shirt measures 40 cm</em> vs <em>I'm measuring the room</em>.</p>
</div>

## C — Mental state verbs: recently started or not yet sure

With some verbs describing **mental states** — **find, realise, regret, think, understand** — we can use the present continuous to emphasise that we have **recently started** to think something, or that we are **not yet sure** about it. Compare:

<SayIt text="I regret that the company will have to be sold." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= The decision is made and I'm sorry about it — a settled state.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="I'm regretting my decision to give her the job." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= I'm increasingly aware that it was the wrong decision — a feeling that is developing.)</p>

<Callout type="info" title="Two verbs that break the pattern">
  When it means <em>think carefully about</em>, <strong>consider</strong> is used <strong>only</strong> in the present continuous: <em>He's considering taking early retirement</em> — <span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>He considers taking early retirement</em>.<br /><br />
  By contrast, some verbs of preference and belief — <strong>agree, believe, conclude, know, prefer</strong> — are <strong>rarely</strong> used in the continuous: <em>I believe you now</em> — <span className="text-rose-600">not</span> <em>I'm believing you now</em>.
</Callout>

## D — Performatives

We use the **present simple** with verbs that **perform the very action they describe** — *performatives*. Saying the word *is* doing the thing:

<SayIt text="I suggest you park outside the city and get the bus to the centre." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I request that you read the terms and conditions carefully before signing." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-sky-200 bg-sky-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-700">Common performative verbs</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-sky-900">acknowledge · admit · advise · apologise · beg · confess · congratulate · declare · deny · forbid · guarantee · name · order · permit · predict · promise · refuse · remind · request · thank · warn</p>
</div>

Some verbs used as performatives — **apologise, deny, guarantee, promise, suggest** — take the present simple in affirmative sentences, but have a similar meaning with **either** the simple **or** the continuous in **negative** sentences:

<SayIt text="I don't deny taking the books, but Miguel said it would be okay." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I'm not denying taking the books, but Miguel said it would be okay." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Both are natural — negative performative.)</p>

We also often combine performatives with **modals** to make what we say more tentative or polite:

<SayIt text="We would advise you to arrive two hours before the flight leaves." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I must beg you to keep this a secret." />

  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-violet-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-violet-900">Present continuous</div>
    <div className="bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">Present simple</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">a state verb stressing it's temporary / around now (<em>are loving</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">a steady, permanent state (<em>like, believe, own</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">the action sense of a split verb (<em>is appearing, is weighing</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">the state sense of a split verb (<em>appears = seems</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">a mental state just begun or unsure (<em>I'm regretting</em>, <em>He's considering</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">a performative (<em>I apologise, I promise, I warn you</em>)</div>
  </div>
</div>
