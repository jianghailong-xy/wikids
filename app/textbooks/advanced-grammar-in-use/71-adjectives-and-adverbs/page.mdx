Most adverbs of manner are simply an adjective **+ -ly** (*sudden → suddenly*, *happy → happily*). But the system is full of exceptions that catch advanced learners: adjectives that *already* end in **-ly**, **-ed** participles that have no adverb at all, words that exist in **two forms** (*quick / quickly*), and pairs where the **-ly** version means something completely different (*hard / hardly*, *late / lately*). This unit is about choosing correctly among them.

## A — Adverbs of manner from adjectives (and when you can't make one)

Many adverbs of manner — saying *how* something is done — are formed from an **adjective + -ly**. But three groups resist this:

**1. Adjectives already ending in -ly.** When an adjective already ends in **-ly** (*cowardly, elderly, friendly, kindly, lively, lonely, lovely*) we don't add another **-ly**. Instead we use a prepositional phrase with **fashion, manner** or **way**:

<SayIt text="He smiled at me in a friendly way." />
<br />
<SayIt text="She waved her hands around in a lively fashion." />

**2. Most -ed participle adjectives.** Most participle adjectives ending in **-ed** have no adverb form, so we use a prepositional phrase with a related noun (or a preposition + noun) instead:

<SayIt text="They rose to greet me in a subdued manner." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(not <span className="text-rose-600">subduedly</span>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="She looked at me in amazement." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(not <span className="text-rose-600">amazedly</span>)</p>

**3. …but some -ed participles *do* have an -ly adverb.** Compare:

<SayIt text="The storm was unexpected." />
<br />
<SayIt text="The weather turned unexpectedly stormy." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">-ed participles that DO take -ly</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">agitatedly · allegedly · deservedly · determinedly · disappointedly · excitedly · hurriedly · pointedly · repeatedly · reportedly · reputedly · supposedly · worriedly</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-emerald-800">Outside this short list, assume a participle has <strong>no</strong> adverb and reach for <em>in a … manner / way</em> or <em>in / with + noun</em> instead.</p>
</div>

<Callout type="warning" title="The trap">
  You can't simply bolt <strong>-ly</strong> onto any adjective. <span className="text-rose-600">friendlily</span>, <span className="text-rose-600">cowardlily</span>, <span className="text-rose-600">subduedly</span> and <span className="text-rose-600">amazedly</span> are all wrong — these adjectives need a prepositional phrase to do an adverb's job.
</Callout>

## B — Adverbs with two forms (*quick / quickly*)

Some adverbs have **two forms**, one ending in **-ly** and one not. We can often use **either** without changing the meaning — though the **-ly** form is the one that is always grammatically correct and is preferred in formal English:

<SayIt text="She ran quick towards the door." />
<br />
<SayIt text="She ran quickly towards the door." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(both heard in speech; <em>quickly</em> is the safe, formal choice)</p>

But the **-ly** form **must** be used when the adverb comes **immediately before the verb**:

<SayIt text="She quickly ran towards the door." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(not <span className="text-rose-600">She quick ran towards the door.</span>)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-amber-200 bg-amber-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-amber-700">Two-form adverbs (same meaning)</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-amber-900">cheap(ly) · clean(ly) · clear(ly) · fine(ly) · loud(ly) · slow(ly) · thin(ly)</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-amber-800">In careful writing prefer the <strong>-ly</strong> form; before the verb it is obligatory: <em>He <strong>loudly</strong> applauded</em> — not <span className="text-rose-600">He loud applauded</span>.</p>
</div>

## C — Adverbs with *different* meanings with or without -ly

This is the dangerous group: the two forms exist, but they **do not** mean the same thing.

<SayIt text="She gave her time free." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= for no money)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="She gave her time freely." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= willingly)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="I arrived late for the concert." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= not on time)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="I haven't seen Amy lately." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= recently)</p>

Compare these other pairs — the bare form tends to be literal (distance, height, position), while the **-ly** form is figurative or an intensifier:

  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">without -ly (often literal)</div>
    <div className="bg-violet-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-violet-900">with -ly (figurative / intensifier)</div>

    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">He wandered <strong>deep</strong> into the forest. <span className="text-slate-400">(= a long way)</span></div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">He felt <strong>deeply</strong> hurt by her criticisms. <span className="text-slate-400">(= very)</span></div>

    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">You can fly <strong>direct</strong>. <span className="text-slate-400">(= without stopping)</span></div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">I'll be with you <strong>directly</strong>. <span className="text-slate-400">(= very soon; or = straight: directly ahead)</span></div>

    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">A choir member was singing <strong>flat</strong>. <span className="text-slate-400">(= out of tune)</span></div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">I <strong>flatly</strong> refused to lend him money. <span className="text-slate-400">(= absolutely)</span></div>

    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">He kicked the ball <strong>high</strong> over the goal. <span className="text-slate-400">(= to a great height)</span></div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">Everyone thinks <strong>highly</strong> of her teaching. <span className="text-slate-400">(= it is very good)</span></div>

    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">He's <strong>just</strong> arrived. <span className="text-slate-400">(= a moment ago); just like her mother (= exactly)</span></div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">You can be <strong>justly</strong> proud of this. <span className="text-slate-400">(= rightly; justifiably)</span></div>

    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">Which cheese do you like <strong>most</strong>? <span className="text-slate-400">(superlative)</span></div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">Her novels are <strong>mostly</strong> out of print. <span className="text-slate-400">(= most of them); we mostly go to France (= usually)</span></div>

    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">They cut <strong>short</strong> their holiday. <span className="text-slate-400">(= ended it early)</span></div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">The speaker will be arriving <strong>shortly</strong>. <span className="text-slate-400">(= soon)</span></div>

    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 bg-sky-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">The door was <strong>wide</strong> open. <span className="text-slate-400">(= completely)</span></div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">The book is <strong>widely</strong> available. <span className="text-slate-400">(= in many places)</span></div>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick check before you write -ly">
  Ask: <em>am I being literal or figurative?</em> <strong>deep</strong> in the forest but <strong>deeply</strong> moved; the ball goes <strong>high</strong> but you think <strong>highly</strong> of someone; you go <strong>direct</strong> (no stops) but I'll come <strong>directly</strong> (soon). The bare form usually keeps the concrete meaning.
</Callout>
