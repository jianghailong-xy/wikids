## A — Negative questions: persuade, suggest, criticise

A negative yes/no- or wh-question is usually made with an auxiliary verb (**have, did, would**, etc.) **+ -n't**. We reach for it far more often to **suggest, persuade or criticise** than to ask a genuine yes-or-no question.

<SayIt text="Wouldn't it be better to stay at home?" />
<br />
<SayIt text="Won't you go out for a meal with us?" />

In **formal** contexts, or when we want to give the negative special emphasis — to sound angry, very surprised, or to push hard — we put the full word **not after the subject**. This happens mainly in **yes/no-** questions rather than wh-questions:

<SayIt text="Did she not realise that she'd broken it?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(less emphatically: <em>Didn't she realise that …?</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Can you not get there a bit earlier?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(less emphatically: <em>Can't you get there …?</em>)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="Where does 'not' go?">
  The contracted <strong>-n't</strong> attaches to the auxiliary; the full word <strong>not</strong> goes <em>after the subject</em>. <br />
  ✅ <strong>Didn't</strong> she realise? &nbsp;·&nbsp; ✅ Did she <strong>not</strong> realise? &nbsp;·&nbsp; ❌ Did <strong>not</strong> she realise?
</Callout>

## B — Negative words other than -n't

We sometimes build the negative with **never, no, nobody, nothing, nowhere**. These feel more emphatic and rather more formal:

<SayIt text="Why do you never help?" />
<br />
<SayIt text="Have you nowhere to go?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or <em>Do you have nowhere to go?</em>)</p>

Less emphatically, or more informally, we use **-n't** together with **ever, any, anybody, anything, anywhere**:

<SayIt text="Why don't you ever help?" />
<br />
<SayIt text="Haven't you got anywhere to go?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or <em>Don't you have anywhere to go?</em>)</p>

<Callout type="tip" title="The emphatic / everyday pair">
  Each emphatic negative word has an everyday <strong>-n't … any-</strong> partner that means the same thing: <br />
  <em>never</em> ↔ <em>don't … ever</em> &nbsp;·&nbsp; <em>nothing</em> ↔ <em>don't … anything</em> &nbsp;·&nbsp; <em>nowhere</em> ↔ <em>don't … anywhere</em> &nbsp;·&nbsp; <em>nobody</em> ↔ <em>isn't … anybody</em>
</Callout>

## C — Suggestions with 'Why not …?' — and criticism with 'Why didn't …?'

To make a **suggestion** we use **Why not + verb** or **Why don't …?** — but **never** *Why do not …?* / *Why does not …?*

<SayIt text="Why not decorate the house yourself?" />
<br />
<SayIt text="Why don't you decorate the house yourself?" />

**Why didn't …?** is *not* a suggestion. Depending on intonation and context it can **criticise** — it signals that we think an action was wrong:

<SayIt text="Why didn't you tell me that in the first place?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= I'm annoyed that you didn't.)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't say 'Why do not you …?'">
  ❌ Why <strong>do not</strong> you phone her? &nbsp;&nbsp; ✅ <strong>Why not</strong> phone her? &nbsp;/&nbsp; ✅ <strong>Why don't</strong> you phone her?
</Callout>

## D — Negative questions as exclamations

Negative question forms, usually said with a **falling intonation**, work as **exclamations giving an opinion**. They aren't really questions — no answer is expected, and in writing they often take an exclamation mark:

<SayIt text="Haven't you grown!" />
<br />
<SayIt text="Doesn't she look lovely!" />
<br />
<SayIt text="Didn't it snow a lot!" />

<Callout type="info" title="Question on the page, exclamation in the voice">
  A genuine question rises at the end (<em>Hasn't she grown?</em> — checking). The exclamation <em>falls</em> (<em>Hasn't she grown!</em> — admiring). Same words; the intonation decides whether you want an answer.
</Callout>

## E — Echo questions

We use an **echo question** when we haven't understood what was said, or to check that we heard correctly — often because it surprised us. We can echo the **whole sentence**, usually with a **rising intonation**:

  participants={[
    { name: "Sam", emoji: "🗣️", side: "left", tone: "slate" },
    { name: "You", emoji: "😮", side: "right", tone: "brand" },
  ]}
  turns={[
    { speaker: "Sam", text: "Tala's lost her job." },
    { speaker: "You", text: "Tala's lost her job?" },
    { speaker: "Sam", text: "We paid £3,000 for the painting.", say: "We paid three thousand pounds for the painting." },
    { speaker: "You", text: "You paid how much?", say: "You paid how much?" },
    { speaker: "Sam", text: "I think she's having a sleep." },
    { speaker: "You", text: "She's doing what?" },
  ]}
/>

Or we stress just the **wh-word**, or a phrase with **how**. The wh-word can stay in its normal place (echoing the word order) or move to the front:

<SayIt text="'Leon's arriving at 6:30.' 'When's Leon arriving?'" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or, echoing the word order: <em>'Leon's arriving when?'</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="'We paid £3,000 for the painting.' 'How much did you pay?'" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or: <em>'You paid how much?'</em>)</p>

We can use **what** — or **do what** — to focus on the **verb**, or on the part of the sentence that begins with the verb:

<SayIt text="'We paid £3,000 for the painting.' 'You what?'" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or <em>'You did what?'</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="'I think she's having a sleep.' 'She's what?'" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or <em>'She's doing what?'</em>)</p>

## F — Questions with that-clauses

A wh-question can point forward to a following **that-clause**, especially after verbs such as **expect, hope, reckon, say, suggest, suppose** and **think**. In these questions we can **leave out** *that*:

<div className="not-prose my-6 grid grid-cols-2 gap-2 sm:grid-cols-5">
  {["think", "expect", "suppose", "reckon", "hope", "say", "suggest", "believe", "imagine", "guess"].map((w) => (
    <span key={w} className="rounded-lg border-2 border-violet-200 bg-violet-50 px-3 py-2 text-center text-sm font-semibold text-violet-800">{w}</span>
  ))}
</div>

<SayIt text="When do you think (that) you'll finish the job?" />

**However**, when the wh-word is the **subject, object or complement** of the verb in the that-clause, we do **not** use *that*:

<SayIt text="What did you think was in the box?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(not <em>What did you think that was in the box?</em>)</p>

<Callout type="tip" title="The 'that' test">
  Look at the that-clause without the wh-word. If it still has its own subject, <em>that</em> is optional — <em>When do you think (that) <strong>you'll</strong> finish?</em> But if the wh-word <em>is</em> the missing subject, object or complement, the clause is now incomplete — <em>What did you think <strong>___</strong> was in the box?</em> — so you must drop <em>that</em>.
</Callout>
