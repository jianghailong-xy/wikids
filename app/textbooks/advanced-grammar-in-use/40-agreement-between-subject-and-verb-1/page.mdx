The rule sounds trivial — a singular subject takes a singular verb, a plural subject a plural verb. In practice the trouble is finding the subject. Once a noun phrase grows long, once a clause does the subject's work, once the subject slides *after* the verb, or once a collective or an -s title is involved, the agreement stops being automatic. This unit works through exactly those cases.

## A — The verb agrees with the main noun of the subject

If a sentence has a **singular subject**, it takes a **singular verb**; a **plural subject** takes a **plural verb**. The verb *agrees with* the subject. Compare:

<SayIt text="She lives in China." />
<br />
<SayIt text="More people live in Asia than in any other continent." />

When the subject is **complex**, the verb must agree with the **main noun** inside it — not with whatever noun happens to sit closest to the verb:

<SayIt text="Many leading members of the opposition party have criticised the delay." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Main noun = <em>members</em>, plural → <em>have</em>. Not <em>party has</em>.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="The only excuse that he gave for his actions was that he was tired." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Main noun = <em>excuse</em>, singular → <em>was</em>. Not <em>actions were</em>.)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't be pulled towards the nearest noun">
  The words directly before the verb (<em>the opposition party</em>, <em>his actions</em>) are a trap. Strip a complex subject down to its <strong>head noun</strong> and make the verb agree with <em>that</em>: <em>the <u>excuse</u> … was</em>, <em>the <u>members</u> … have</em>.
</Callout>

The verb also has to agree with the subject when the subject **follows** the verb — for example after a fronted element (see Units 99 &amp; 100):

<SayIt text="Displayed on the board were the exam results." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Subject = <em>the exam results</em>, plural → <em>were</em>. Compare: <em>The exam results were displayed …</em>)</p>

## B — Clauses and what-clauses as subject

If the subject is a **clause**, we usually use a **singular** verb:

<SayIt text="To keep these young people in prison is inhuman." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Having overall responsibility for the course means that I have a lot of meetings." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Whoever took them remains a mystery." />

However, if the subject is a **what-clause** (see Unit 98B), we use a **singular** verb when the following main noun is singular, and **either a singular or a plural** verb when the following main noun is plural (a plural verb is preferred in more formal contexts):

<SayIt text="What worries us is the poor selection process." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Following main noun <em>process</em> is singular → <em>is</em>.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="What is needed are additional resources." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Following main noun <em>resources</em> is plural → <em>are</em> in a formal style; more colloquially <em>… is …</em>.)</p>

## C — Collective nouns: one unit or many individuals?

Some nouns with a **singular form** that refer to a group — *collective nouns* — can take **either** a singular or a plural verb:

<SayIt text="The council has (or have) postponed a decision on the new road." />

Use a **singular** verb when the focus is on the institution or organisation **as a single whole**, and a **plural** verb when the focus is on the **collection of individuals** within it. Often either works with very little difference in meaning — though in formal contexts (such as academic writing) a singular verb is more common.

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">Collective nouns that take either verb</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">army · association · audience · class · club · college · commission · committee · community · company · crew · crowd · department · electorate · enemy · family · federation · generation · government · group · institute · jury · opposition · orchestra · population · press · public · school · team · university</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-emerald-800">…and specific organisations: <em>the Bank of England, the BBC, IBM, Sony, the United Nations</em>.</p>
</div>

In some contexts, though, the meaning **forces** one form or the other:

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-violet-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-1 text-sm sm:grid-cols-2">
    <div className="bg-violet-100 px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-violet-900">The committee usually <strong>raise</strong> their hands to vote 'Yes'.<br /><span className="text-xs font-normal text-violet-700">(not <em>… raises its hands …</em>)</span></div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-white px-4 py-3 text-slate-700 sm:border-l sm:border-t-0">Raising a hand is something the <strong>individuals</strong> do, not the committee as a whole — so a plural verb.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-sky-50 px-4 py-3 font-semibold text-sky-900">The school <strong>is</strong> to close next year.<br /><span className="text-xs font-normal text-sky-700">(not <em>The school are to close …</em>)</span></div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-white px-4 py-3 text-slate-700 sm:border-l sm:border-t-0">Closing happens to the <strong>building or institution</strong>, not to the people in it — so a singular verb.</div>
  </div>
</div>

## D — Names and titles ending in -s that name a single thing

When a name or title **ending in -s refers to a single unit**, we use a **singular** verb. This covers countries; newspapers; titles of books, films and games; and quoted plural words or phrases:

<SayIt text="At this time of the year the Netherlands is one hour ahead of the UK." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(A single country → singular.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="The Machine Gunners was one of Robert Westall's most successful books." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(A single book title → singular, even though it names 'gunners'.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="'Daps' is the word used in the south-west of the country for sports shoes." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(A quoted word treated as one item → singular.)</p>

<Callout type="info" title="At a glance">
  <strong>Singular verb</strong> for: a clause subject (<em>To keep … is</em>, <em>Whoever took them remains</em>); a complex subject with a singular head noun (<em>the excuse … was</em>); a collective seen as one body (<em>the school is to close</em>); a name/title ending in -s that names one thing (<em>the Netherlands is</em>).<br /><br />
  <strong>Plural verb</strong> for: a complex subject with a plural head noun (<em>the members … have</em>); a collective seen as individuals (<em>the committee raise their hands</em>); a genuine plural subject, even when it sits after the verb (<em>… were the exam results</em>).
</Callout>
