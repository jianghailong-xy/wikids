## A — Adverbs of place and direction: fronting and inversion

Adverbs of **place** and **direction** — often prepositional phrases — usually sit in **end position**. We can move them to **front position** to emphasise the location, or to throw the spotlight onto whatever now lands at the end of the clause. This fronting belongs mainly to **formal, descriptive writing and reports**. Compare:

<SayIt text="The money was eventually found under the floorboards." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(End position — the neutral, everyday order.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="The police searched the house. Under the floorboards they found a body." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Front position — the new, dramatic information, <em>a body</em>, is held back to the end.)</p>

When a place adverbial goes to the front, we often put the **subject after the verb** (see also Unit 99A):

<SayIt text="Next to the bookshelf was a fireplace." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Less formally: <em>Next to the bookshelf there was a fireplace.</em> — but not <em>Next to the bookshelf a fireplace was ...</em>)</p>

This subject-after-verb order also happens with **intransitive verbs of position or movement to a position** — but **not** when the subject is a pronoun:

<SayIt text="Beyond the houses lay open fields." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Beyond the houses they lay." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Pronoun subject <em>they</em> → no inversion. Not <em>Beyond the houses lay they</em>.)</p>

<Callout type="info" title="Verbs that trigger this inversion">
  Inversion after a fronted place/direction adverbial typically comes with <strong>be</strong> and with verbs of being-in-a-position or moving-into-one: <em>hang, live, sit, stand</em> · <em>come, fly, go, march, roll, run, swim, walk</em>. A literary variant keeps the normal order — <em>Beyond the houses open fields lay</em>.
</Callout>

However, we usually **keep the normal subject–verb order** (no inversion) when we are describing an **action**, when one of those intransitive verbs is followed by an **adverb of manner**, with **other intransitive verbs**, or with **transitive verbs**:

<SayIt text="Through the window the boy swam powerfully." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Manner adverb <em>powerfully</em> → no inversion. Rather than <em>... swam the boy powerfully</em>.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Outside the church the choir sang." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<em>sing</em> is an action verb here → no inversion. Rather than <em>... sang the choir</em>.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="In the garden Nik built a play house for the children." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Transitive verb with an object → no inversion. Not <em>In the garden built Nik ...</em>)</p>

## B — Adverbs of time that force the auxiliary in front of the subject

When certain **negative or restrictive time adverbials** are fronted, the subject must come **after an auxiliary verb** (or after the main verb **be**) — see also Unit 100:

<SayIt text="At no time would he admit that his team played badly." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Not <em>At no time he would admit ...</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Not once was she at home when I phoned." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Main verb <em>be</em> jumps in front of the subject. Not <em>Not once she was ...</em>)</p>

If the main verb is **not** *be* and there is no other auxiliary, we bring in **do / does / did** — though here the inversion is **optional**:

<SayIt text="Only later did she realise how much damage had been caused." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Or, without inversion: <em>Only later she realised how much damage had been caused.</em>)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="These adverbials pull the auxiliary forward">
  Negative time adverbials that trigger this inversion include <strong>at no time, hardly ever, not once, only later, rarely,</strong> and <strong>seldom</strong>. With these in front position, an auxiliary (or <em>be</em>) <em>must</em> come before the subject — leaving the normal order sounds wrong.
</Callout>

**first, next, now** and **then** can also be fronted — and here a comma makes all the difference. With **no comma**, the verb comes before the subject; **with a comma** (an intonation break in speech), the normal subject–verb order returns:

<SayIt text="At first there was silence. Then came a voice that I knew." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(No comma after <em>Then</em> → inversion. Not <em>Then a voice came ...</em>)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="At first there was silence. Then, a voice came that I knew." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Comma after <em>Then</em> → no inversion. Not <em>Then, came a voice ...</em>)</p>

## C — Definite time and frequency: end or front, but never the middle, and never inverted

Adverbs of time that mark a **definite point or period** in time, or a **definite frequency**, normally go in **end position**, or in **front position for emphasis** — but **not in mid position**. Crucially, when they are fronted there is **no inversion** of subject and verb:

<SayIt text="I went to Paris yesterday." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Or: <em>Yesterday I went to Paris.</em> — subject before verb, as normal.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="We meet for lunch once a week." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Or: <em>Once a week we meet for lunch.</em> — still no inversion.)</p>

The adverbs **daily, hourly, monthly, weekly, annually, quarterly** (= four times a year), and the like, go in **end position only**:

<SayIt text="I pay my subscription annually." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Not <em>Annually I pay ...</em>; not <em>I annually pay ...</em>)</p>

<Callout type="tip" title="Three quick contrasts to hold onto">
  <strong>Place fronted →</strong> inversion is possible (<em>Around her neck hung a necklace</em>). <br />
  <strong>Negative time adverbial fronted →</strong> auxiliary inversion is required (<em>Rarely had we seen such a storm</em>). <br />
  <strong>Definite time / frequency fronted →</strong> never inverted (<em>Every Monday she goes swimming</em>), and <em>-ly</em> words like <em>daily, weekly</em> can't be fronted at all.
</Callout>
