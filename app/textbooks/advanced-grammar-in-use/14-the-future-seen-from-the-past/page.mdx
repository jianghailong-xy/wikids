English has a whole toolkit for talking about the future — *will*, *be going to*, the present continuous, *will have done*, *be to + infinitive*, *be about to*. This unit is about what happens to all of them when the vantage point moves: when you stand at a moment **in the past** and look forward to something that was, at that time, still ahead. The rule is elegantly simple — take the future form you'd use now and shift it one step into the past — but the choices it forces (did the thing actually happen? is it *still* ahead for me, the speaker?) are exactly the kind of fine control this book is built to train. These forms turn up constantly in reporting (see Units 32–36).

## A — Use the *past* tenses of the future forms

To talk about an activity or event that was in the future at a particular point in the past, we use the **past tenses of the verb forms we would normally use to talk about the future**. In other words, every "from now" form has a matching "from the past" form:

    <div className="bg-sky-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-sky-900">The future from now …</div>
    <div className="bg-violet-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-violet-900">The future from the past …</div>

    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">I think I<strong>'ll stay</strong> at home this summer.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/40 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">Eleni decided that she <strong>would stay</strong> at home for the summer.</div>

    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">I<strong>'m not going to say</strong> anything about the exams today.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/40 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">I <strong>wasn't going to say</strong> anything about the exams, but the students asked me to.</div>

    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><strong>Will</strong> you <strong>be going</strong> alone, or is Louise going with you?</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/40 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">At the time, I thought I <strong>would be going</strong> alone, but then Jan said he wanted to come.</div>

    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">The exam <strong>will have finished</strong> by three o'clock.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/40 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">The exam was so easy that most people <strong>would have finished</strong> after 30 minutes.</div>

    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">There <strong>is to be</strong> a meeting of ministers this evening.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/40 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">It was announced that there <strong>was to be</strong> a meeting of ministers that evening.</div>

    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">When the school closes, all the children <strong>are to be moved</strong> to one nearby.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/40 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">Mrs Novak heard that she <strong>was to be moved</strong> to a post in a nearby school.</div>

    <div className="border-t border-sky-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">As the bell <strong>is about to go</strong>, pack your books away.</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/40 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">The bell <strong>was about to go</strong> when all the children started to pack their books away.</div>
  </div>
</div>

So the mapping is mechanical: *will → would*, *am/is/are going to → was/were going to*, *will be doing → would be doing*, *will have done → would have done*, *is to → was to*, *is about to → was about to*.

<Callout type="info" title="When the future is still ahead, either form works">
  If the future seen from the past is <strong>still in the future for the speaker</strong>, then <strong>either</strong> form is possible:<br /><br />
  It was announced this morning that there <strong>is</strong> / <strong>was</strong> to be a statement this evening.<br /><br />
  The statement hasn't happened yet, so you can report it with the present <em>is to be</em> or with the back-shifted <em>was to be</em>.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="The form can leave it open whether it actually happened">
  Sometimes the past form tells us nothing about whether the event came off — only context does. Compare these two, which are <em>identical</em> in grammar:<br /><br />
  • I didn't phone to give him the news because we <strong>were seeing</strong> each other later. He was very upset when I told him. <span className="text-emerald-700">(= we <u>did</u> see each other)</span><br />
  • We <strong>were seeing</strong> each other later that day, but I had to phone and cancel. <span className="text-rose-600">(= we <u>didn't</u> see each other)</span>
</Callout>

## B — *was / were to (+ have + past participle)*

There is one pair of forms worth isolating, because the choice between them encodes **whether the planned event actually happened**:

- **was / were to + infinitive** — for things that were in the future then and **actually happened**.
- **was / were to have + past participle** — for things that were **expected, but didn't happen**.

<SayIt text="At the time she was probably the best actor in the theatre company, but in fact some of her colleagues were to become much better known." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<em>were to become</em> — and they did; this is a destined future fact, seen from then.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="He was to find out years later that the car he had bought was stolen." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<em>was to find out</em> — it happened, just much later.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="The boat, which was to have taken them to the island, failed to arrive." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<em>was to have taken</em> — the plan, which then fell through.)</p>

<Callout type="tip" title="In everyday English, prefer 'be supposed to'">
  <em>was / were to have + past participle</em> is quite formal. In less formal contexts it is more natural to use <strong>be supposed to</strong>:<br /><br />
  I <strong>was supposed to help</strong>, but I was ill. <span className="text-slate-500">(more natural than <em>I was to have helped …</em>)</span>
</Callout>
