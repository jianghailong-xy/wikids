## A — Focusing with it-clauses

We can use an **it-clause** with **be** to focus attention on the information that immediately follows **it + (be)**. A sentence built this way is a kind of **cleft sentence** ("cleft" = split in two). The clause that comes *after* the it-clause — usually a **that-clause** — carries the information that is already known or treated as less important:

<SayIt text="'Lea bought the car from Olav.' 'No, it was Olav that bought the car from Lea.'" />
<br />
<SayIt text="I don't mind her criticising me, but it's how she does it that I object to." />
<br />
<SayIt text="It was to show how much I cared for her that I bought her the necklace." />

So the spotlight falls on the chunk between **it + be** and **that** — a noun (*Olav*), a phrase (*how she does it*), or even a whole purpose clause (*to show how much I cared*).

We sometimes use **which** or **who** instead of **that**, and **when** and **where** are possible too — but usually only in informal English:

<SayIt text="Karl was always there to help her, and it was to him that she now turned for support." />
<br />
<SayIt text="It was only last Sunday that I was playing tennis with Ida." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Informal: ... only last Sunday <em>when</em> I was playing tennis with her.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="It was in Warsaw that the film was made." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Informal: ... in Warsaw <em>where</em> the film was made.)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="The classic trap: not how, not why">
  Even when the focused phrase is about manner or reason, the second clause is still introduced by <strong>that</strong> — never <em>how</em> or <em>why</em>:<br />
  ✅ It was <strong>by improving distribution that</strong> he made the firm profitable.<br />
  ❌ It was by improving distribution <s>how</s> he made the firm profitable.<br />
  ✅ It was <strong>to apologise that</strong> I phoned. &nbsp; ❌ It was to apologise <s>why</s> I phoned.
</Callout>

## B — Focusing with what-clauses

We can also use a **what-clause** followed by **be** to focus attention on certain information — another form of cleft sentence, and one that is especially common in **conversation**. The information we want to highlight sits **outside** the what-clause. Compare the neutral sentence with the cleft:

<div className="not-prose my-6 grid gap-3 sm:grid-cols-2">
  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-slate-200 bg-slate-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-slate-500">neutral</p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-800">We gave them some home-made cake.</p>
  </div>
  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-violet-200 bg-violet-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-violet-600">focused</p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-violet-900"><strong>What we gave them</strong> was some home-made cake.</p>
  </div>
</div>

We do this when we want to **introduce a new topic**, **give a reason, instruction or explanation**, or **correct** something that has been said or done. The information in focus is shown in italics:

<SayIt text="What I'd like you to work on is the revision exercise on the website." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Isa arrived two hours late: what had happened was that his bicycle chain had broken." />
<br />
<SayIt text="'Will this small bookcase do?' 'No, what I was looking for was something much bigger and stronger.'" />

The what-clause can often go either at the **beginning** or the **end** of the sentence:

<SayIt text="What upset me most was his rudeness." />
<br />
<SayIt text="His rudeness was what upset me most." />

To focus attention on an **action** performed by someone, we use a special pattern: **what + subject + do + be + (to-)infinitive**. Note that we *cannot* use an it-clause to do this:

<SayIt text="Luis lost his job and was short of money, so what he did was (to) sell his house and move in with his brother." />

<Callout type="tip" title="what + do + be: focusing on an action">
  The verb <em>do</em> stands in for the action, and the real verb follows <strong>be</strong> as a bare or <em>to-</em>infinitive:<br />
  <em>What he <strong>did</strong> was <strong>(to) sell</strong> the house.</em> &nbsp;·&nbsp; <em>What we should <strong>do</strong> is <strong>(to) wait</strong>.</em><br />
  ✅ What I did was get my brother to record it. &nbsp; ❌ What I did was <s>getting</s> my brother to record it.<br />
  ❌ It was sell the house <s>that he did</s>. &nbsp;→&nbsp; use the <em>what</em>-pattern instead.
</Callout>

## C — Focusing with other wh-clauses: prefer a noun + clause

The what-pattern in **B** also works in principle with other **wh-clauses** (*how, where, who, when, why* …). But rather than start a sentence with a bare wh-clause (*Why I left… , Where you play…*), we normally prefer a **noun related to the wh-word**, followed by a **that-** or **wh-clause**:

<div className="not-prose my-6 grid gap-2 sm:grid-cols-2 lg:grid-cols-3">
  {[
    ["why", "the reason"],
    ["where", "the place"],
    ["who", "the person / somebody"],
    ["when", "the time"],
    ["how", "the way"],
    ["what kind of", "the sort of thing"],
  ].map(([w, n]) => (
    <div key={w} className="flex items-center gap-2 rounded-lg border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 px-3 py-2 text-sm">
      <span className="font-mono font-bold text-emerald-700">{w}</span>
      <span className="text-emerald-400">→</span>
      <span className="font-semibold text-emerald-900">{n}</span>
    </div>
  ))}
</div>

<SayIt text="The only reason (why / that) I left the party early was that I was feeling unwell." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Rather than: <em>Why I left the party early was</em> that I was feeling unwell.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="The place (where / that) you should play football is the playground, not the classroom." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Somebody (who / that) I enjoy reading is Peter Carey." />
<br />
<SayIt text="The time (when / that) I work best is early morning." />

<Callout type="info" title="Three ways to put one part in the spotlight">
  English gives you three cleft templates. Pick the one that frames your focus most naturally:
  <ul className="mt-2 list-disc space-y-1 pl-5">
    <li><strong>it-cleft</strong> — <em>It was <u>Olav</u> that bought the car.</em> (focus a noun, phrase or purpose)</li>
    <li><strong>what-cleft</strong> — <em>What I need is <u>a holiday</u>.</em> (focus information; great for actions with <em>what … do</em>)</li>
    <li><strong>noun + clause</strong> — <em>The reason I called was <u>to apologise</u>.</em> (the smooth alternative to a bare <em>why/where/how</em> opening)</li>
  </ul>
</Callout>
