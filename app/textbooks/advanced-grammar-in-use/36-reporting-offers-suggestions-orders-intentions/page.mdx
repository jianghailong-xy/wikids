When we report **offers, suggestions, orders, intentions, promises, requests** and the like, the meaning is rarely the hard part — the pattern is. Each reporting verb is fussy about what can follow it: a bare *to*-infinitive, an object **and** a *to*-infinitive, a *that*-clause, an *-ing* clause — or only some of these. This unit sorts the common verbs into those patterns and shows where learners slip (★ *suggest to do*, ★ *offer that...*).

## A — Verb + (object) + to-infinitive clause

Many reporting verbs are followed by a **to-infinitive clause**. Some take the infinitive directly; others need an **object** in between — usually the person the offer or order is aimed at, who then performs the reported action.

**With a *to*-infinitive (no object):**

<SayIt text="They propose to build the theatre next to the town hall." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Reporting: 'The theatre will be built next to the town hall.' &nbsp;—&nbsp; <em>not</em> They propose <em>them</em> to build...</p>

Also: **agree, demand, guarantee, offer, promise, swear, threaten, volunteer**.

**With an object + a *to*-infinitive:**

<SayIt text="She encouraged Fran to take the job." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Reporting: 'You should take the job, Fran.'</p>

Also: **advise, ask, call on, command, instruct, invite, order, persuade, recommend, remind, request, urge, warn, tell**.

<Callout type="info" title="What the object is doing">
  The object normally names the person the offer / suggestion / order is <em>made to</em> — i.e. the one who carries out the action in the reported clause. <em>She encouraged <strong>Fran</strong> to take the job</em> → Fran is the one who would take it.
</Callout>

A few verbs — **ask** is the classic — work **with or without** an object, and the object changes who wants what:

<SayIt text="We asked to leave our bags outside the exam room." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= this is something <strong>we</strong> wanted)</p>
<SayIt text="They asked us to leave our bags outside the exam room." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(= this is something <strong>they</strong> wanted)</p>

## B — Verb + that-clause *or* verb + to-infinitive clause

After some verbs you can swap the *to*-infinitive for a **that-clause** with no real change in meaning:

<SayIt text="He promised to arrive on time." />
<SayIt text="He promised that he would arrive on time." />

Also usable both ways: **agree, demand, expect, guarantee, hope, propose, request, vow**.

The *that*-clause buys you one thing the infinitive can't: the promiser (or hoper, etc.) and the person referred to in the reported clause can be **different people**.

<SayIt text="He promised that he wouldn't be late." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">'He...' and '...he...' may point to different people — impossible to show with <em>promised to...</em>, where the subject is fixed.</p>

## C — Verb + that-clause (*not* verb + to-infinitive clause)

After **insist, order, say** and **suggest** you can use a **that-clause** but **not** a *to*-infinitive clause:

<SayIt text="There were cheers when he suggested that we went home early." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">★ <em>not</em> ...he suggested <em>to go</em> home early.</p>

**advise** and **order** are flexible: they take **either** an *object + to*-infinitive (see A) **or** a *that*-clause — but never a bare *to*-infinitive on their own:

<SayIt text="I advised her to accept." />
<SayIt text="I advised that she should accept." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">★ <em>not</em> I advised <em>to accept</em>.</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="The number-one trap: suggest">
  <strong>suggest</strong> never takes a <em>to</em>-infinitive — not <em>suggest to do</em>, not <em>suggest somebody to do</em>. Use a <em>that</em>-clause (<em>suggested that we go / went / should go</em>) or, for an action, an <em>-ing</em> clause (<em>suggested going</em> — section E). The same goes for <strong>say</strong> and <strong>insist</strong>: a <em>that</em>-clause, not an infinitive.
</Callout>

## D — Verb + to-infinitive clause (*not* verb + that-clause)

The mirror image of C: a handful of verbs take a **to-infinitive** but reject a **that-clause** about their own intention.

<SayIt text="Carolyn intends to return to Dublin after a year in Canada." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">★ <em>not</em> Carolyn intends <em>that she should return</em>...</p>

Also infinitive-only: **long, offer, refuse, volunteer, want**.

<Callout type="tip" title="Why offer and volunteer resist that">
  These verbs report what the subject undertakes to do <em>themselves</em>, so a same-subject infinitive is natural: <em>She offered <strong>to host</strong> the talks</em>, <em>He volunteered <strong>to chair</strong> them</em>. A <em>that</em>-clause (★ <em>offered that a conference should be held</em>) is wrong here — rephrase with the infinitive.
</Callout>

## E — Reporting an action with an -ing clause

When you report an action that the reported person might do themselves, **or** that someone else might do, the verbs **advise, propose, recommend** and **suggest** can be followed by an **-ing clause** instead of a *that*-clause:

<SayIt text="The lecturer recommended reading a number of books before the exam." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">= ...recommended that the students should read a number of books before the exam.</p>

<SayIt text="To find my way around, Rosa suggested buying a travel card." />

This *-ing* pattern is the natural way to make **suggest** and **propose** work, since they refuse a *to*-infinitive altogether (section C).

<Callout type="info" title="Can these be rewritten with a to-infinitive — without an object?">
  No. <strong>suggest</strong> and <strong>propose</strong> take no <em>to</em>-infinitive at all, and <strong>advise</strong> / <strong>recommend</strong> need an <em>object</em> before one. So the only infinitive versions are <em>the police advised <strong>drivers</strong> to leave...</em>, <em>my mother advised <strong>me</strong> to see a doctor</em> — never ★ <em>advised to leave</em>. Keep the <em>-ing</em> clause when no object is mentioned.
</Callout>
