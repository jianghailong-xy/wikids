At intermediate level **some** and **any** are taught as a tidy pair: *some* in positive sentences, *any* in questions and negatives. At advanced level that rule leaks. *Some* can sit happily inside a negative, *any* can introduce something positive, and both can disappear altogether. This unit is about the meaning each one really carries — and the moment-by-moment choices fluent speakers make without thinking.

## A — Some or zero article with plural and uncountable nouns

Before **plural** and **uncountable** nouns we can often use **some** *or* **zero article** (no article at all) with very little difference in meaning.

<SayIt text="'Where were you last week?' 'I was visiting some friends.'" />
<br />
<SayIt text="Before serving, pour some yoghurt over the meat." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Drop <em>some</em> from either sentence and the meaning barely shifts: <em>visiting friends</em>, <em>pour yoghurt</em>.</p>

With both *some* and zero article we are referring to particular people or things, but in an **indefinite** way. Used like this, *some* is unstressed and pronounced **/səm/**.

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't use some for whole classes">
  We do <strong>not</strong> use <em>some</em> to make a general statement about a whole class of things or people: <br />
  ✅ <strong>Machinery</strong> can be dangerous unless used properly. &nbsp; ❌ Some machinery can be dangerous unless used properly. <br />
  ✅ <strong>Babies</strong> need a lot of attention. &nbsp; ❌ Some babies need a lot of attention.<br />
  (<em>Some machinery / Some babies</em> is fine — but it then means <em>not all</em>, which is a different claim.)
</Callout>

## B — Some + number = 'approximately'

Put **some** in front of a number and it means **'approximately'**. Used this way it is stressed: **/sʌm/**.

<SayIt text="Some eighty per cent of all residents took part in the vote." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">≈ <em>about eighty per cent</em>. Beginning the sentence with the bare figure — <em>Eighty per cent of all residents ...</em> — instead suggests a precise, exact count.</p>

## C — Some instead of a / an (identity unknown or unimportant)

When we **can't say exactly** which person or thing we mean — because we don't know, can't remember, or want to stress that it doesn't matter — we can use **some** instead of **a / an** with a *singular* noun. Stressed again: **/sʌm/**.

<SayIt text="He was interrupted by some troublemaker in the audience." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Whoever it was, the speaker can't (or won't) identify them — and signals as much with <em>some</em>.</p>

The phrase **some (thing) or other** works the same way, underlining the vagueness:

<SayIt text="I bought them from some shop or other in New Street." />

<Callout type="info" title="It's 'some ... or other', never 'a ... or other'">
  The pattern is fixed to <em>some</em>: <em>some shop or other</em>, <em>some excuse or other</em>, <em>some reason or other</em>. <br />
  ❌ <s>I bought them from a shop or other.</s>
</Callout>

## D — Any in non-affirmative and non-specific contexts

We usually use **any** (and **anyone, anything, anywhere** — not *someone, something, somewhere*):

- in **non-affirmative** contexts, that is, contexts lacking a positive, affirmative meaning;
- to refer to **non-specific, unspecified** things.

So we generally choose *any* in sentences with a **negative meaning**:

<SayIt text="There's hardly any sugar left." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I closed the windows to prevent any flies getting in." />
<br />
<SayIt text="It was impossible to see anything in the dark." />
<br />
<SayIt text="We got to the airport without any difficulty." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-2xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-5 shadow-sm">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">Also triggers any — words with a 'negative' flavour</p>
  <div className="mt-3 grid gap-3 sm:grid-cols-3 text-sm">
    <div>
      <p className="font-bold text-emerald-900">negative adverbs</p>
      <p className="text-emerald-800">barely, never, rarely, scarcely, seldom</p>
    </div>
    <div>
      <p className="font-bold text-emerald-900">negative verbs</p>
      <p className="text-emerald-800">deny, fail, forbid, prohibit, refuse</p>
    </div>
    <div>
      <p className="font-bold text-emerald-900">negative adjectives</p>
      <p className="text-emerald-800">reluctant, unable, unlikely</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

However, we **can** still use **some** alongside these negative words in three cases:

<div className="not-prose my-6 space-y-3">
  <div className="rounded-xl border border-slate-200 bg-white p-4 shadow-sm">
    <p className="font-semibold text-slate-800">1 · <span className="text-sky-700">some</span> (/sʌm/) carries the implication <em>'not all'</em></p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm text-slate-600">I talk to colleagues before I make <strong>some</strong> decisions, but I had to make this one on my own.</p>
  </div>
  <div className="rounded-xl border border-slate-200 bg-white p-4 shadow-sm">
    <p className="font-semibold text-slate-800">2 · the basic meaning is <span className="text-sky-700">positive</span></p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm text-slate-600"><strong>Somebody</strong> isn't telling the truth. <span className="text-slate-400">(= there <em>is</em> some person who isn't telling the truth)</span></p>
  </div>
  <div className="rounded-xl border border-slate-200 bg-white p-4 shadow-sm">
    <p className="font-semibold text-slate-800">3 · we mean a <span className="text-sky-700">particular but unspecified</span> person or thing</p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm text-slate-600">I was reluctant to repeat <strong>something</strong> so critical of Paul. <span className="text-slate-400">(= a specific criticism)</span></p>
  </div>
</div>

## E — Any in before-clauses and comparisons

We often use **any** in clauses that begin with **before**, and after **comparisons** (with *than*):

<SayIt text="I cleared up the mess before anyone saw it." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Switching to <em>before <strong>someone</strong> saw it</em> suggests a particular person you have in mind who might have seen it.</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="The material felt softer than anything she had ever touched before." />

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick test for any vs some">
  Ask yourself whether the context is <em>affirmative</em>. Negation, doubt, conditions, <em>before</em>-clauses, comparisons, and questions about whether something exists at all are all <strong>non-affirmative</strong> → reach for <em>any</em>. A plain positive claim, an offer, or a 'not-all' meaning → reach for <em>some</em>.
</Callout>
