At first glance **between** and **among** just carry you back to a school rule — *between* for two, *among* for more. That rule is real but only half the story. The genuine distinction is one of **perception**: *between* pictures the surrounding items as **separate individuals**, however many there are, while *among* dissolves them into a **group or mass**. And neither word stays behind as a preposition of place — both go on to do work with dividing, comparing, choosing, discussing and sharing, plus a handful of fixed expressions worth knowing by heart.

## A — Between and among as prepositions of place

As prepositions of *place* we use **between** with two or more people or things that we see as **individual or separate**, and we use **among** when we see the people or things as **part of a group or mass**. Note that **among** is only used with **three or more** people or things:

<SayIt text="She held the diamond between her thumb and forefinger." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Two clearly separate points.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Zimbabwe is situated between Zambia to the north, Mozambique to the east, Botswana to the west, and South Africa to the south." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Four countries — but each is seen as a separate, named boundary, so <em>between</em>, not <em>among</em>.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="He stood among all his friends at the party and felt very happy." />
<br />
<SayIt text="She eventually found her passport among the clothes in the drawer." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(The friends, the clothes — an undifferentiated mass, so <em>among</em>.)</p>

<Callout type="tip" title="It's about how you see them, not just how many">
  <em>Between</em> can take three, four, five items when you present each as a distinct, named thing (<em>between Zambia, Mozambique, Botswana and South Africa</em>). Switch to <em>among</em> only when the surrounding items lose their individual identity and become a crowd, a heap, a mass — and remember it then needs at least three.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="Amongst">
  <strong>Amongst</strong> is sometimes used instead of <strong>among</strong>, with no change of meaning, but it belongs to more <strong>literary</strong> or old-fashioned contexts (<em>amongst the ruins</em>). In everyday writing, prefer <em>among</em>.
</Callout>

## B — Beyond place: dividing, comparing, choosing, discussing, sharing

**Between** and **among** are not only prepositions of place. To talk about something **divided or shared** between people we can use **either**:

<SayIt text="The money is to be divided between / among the towns in the area." />
<br />
<SayIt text="The prize will be shared between / among the first six finishers in the race." />

Beyond that shared ground, each word has its own territory.

### We also use *between* —

**to talk about comparisons and relationships:**

<SayIt text="There should be a better balance on the committee between the various ethnic groups." />
<br />
<SayIt text="They are wrong to claim that there is a link between unemployment and crime." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-sky-200 bg-sky-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-700">Nouns of comparison &amp; relationship that take <em>between</em></p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-sky-900">association · comparison · connection · contrast · correlation · difference · distinction · link · relationship</p>
</div>

**to talk about choices:**

<SayIt text="I have to choose between the universities of Leeds, York and Manchester." />
<br />
<SayIt text="He felt torn between his family and his friends." />

**to talk about discussions or the results of discussions**, when we specify the two or more people or groups involved:

<SayIt text="There was a disagreement between Emma, Jade and Zoe." />
<br />
<SayIt text="The treaty was signed between Great Britain and France." />

**to say that people or things share an amount of something:**

<SayIt text="Between them, Will and Alice must earn about €100,000 a year." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Last year the three companies built 30,000 houses between them." />

### We also use *among* —

when we mean **'existing or happening in a particular group'**:

<SayIt text="The disease has now broken out among the hill tribes." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Their music is still very popular among teenagers." />

when we mean **'included in a particular group'**:

<SayIt text="They are among the best hockey players in the world." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Among the capital cities of South America, Quito is the second highest." />

<Callout type="warning" title="Two traps in this section">
  <strong>Relationship nouns are fixed on <em>between</em>.</strong> Even when several parties are involved, say <em>a correlation between diet and health</em>, <em>the distinction between amateurs and professionals</em> — never <em>among</em>.<br /><br />
  <strong>'Included in / one of a group' is <em>among</em>.</strong> To place someone inside a set — <em>among the best</em>, <em>among the finalists</em>, <em>among its clients</em> — use <em>among</em>, because the group is felt as a mass, not as separate individuals.
</Callout>

## C — Common fixed expressions

A few set phrases with **between** and **among** are worth learning whole:

<SayIt text="The concert features, among others, Karl Frisk and the Johnsons." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<strong>among others</strong> = other singers / groups are featured, too.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="I later found out that he had been a carpenter and a plumber, among other things." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<strong>among other things</strong> = he had had other jobs, too.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Between ourselves, I don't think Jack is as honest as he should be." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Between you and me, I don't think Jack is as honest as he should be." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(<strong>Between ourselves / Between you and me</strong> = keep this a secret.)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="Say 'between you and me' — not 'between you and I'">
  After a preposition, a pronoun takes its <strong>object</strong> form. The object of <em>between</em> is <em>you and me</em>, so <em>between you and me</em> is correct and <span className="text-rose-600">between you and I</span> is a hypercorrection to avoid.
</Callout>
