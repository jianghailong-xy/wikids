Unit 22 dealt with the plain **be + past participle** passive. This unit takes it one step further: what happens when the passive has to sit *inside* another verb's pattern — a verb that itself demands an **-ing** form or a **to-infinitive**. The result is stacked shapes like *was seen climbing*, *love being given*, *is expected to be proposed*. The catch is that not every active pattern has a passive at all — and some passives quietly change who is doing the wanting or hoping. This unit maps out which conversions work, which don't, and which are traps.

## A — Verb + **-ing** in the active → **be + past participle + -ing**

Verbs followed by **verb + -ing** in the active are made passive with **be + past participle + -ing**:

<SayIt text="They saw the monkey climbing over the fence." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(active — <em>They</em> is the subject)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="The monkey was seen climbing over the fence." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(passive — <em>was seen</em> + the -ing clause stays)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-2xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">Also used this way</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">bring, catch, find, hear, keep, notice, observe, send, show</p>
</div>

<SayIt text="She was heard telling the children to be quiet." />
<br />
<SayIt text="The two men were caught trying to leave the country." />

<Callout type="info" title="The -ing clause doesn't move">
  Only the reporting verb goes passive; the <em>-ing</em> clause after it is left exactly as it was. <em>They kept us <strong>waiting</strong></em> → <em>We were kept <strong>waiting</strong></em>. Learners often try to turn the second verb into a participle too — resist it.
</Callout>

## B — Verbs that take **being + past participle**

Some verbs that can be followed by an **-ing** form can take a *passive* **-ing** form — **being + past participle** — as their object:

<SayIt text="I really love being given presents." />
<br />
<SayIt text="The children enjoyed being taken to the zoo." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-2xl border-2 border-sky-200 bg-sky-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-700">Also</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-sky-900">avoid, deny, describe, dislike, face, hate, (not) imagine, like, remember, report, resent</p>
</div>

<SayIt text="He denied being involved in the robbery." />
<br />
<SayIt text="After the scandal she faced being expelled from the party." />

<Callout type="tip" title="Two -ing forms — active and passive">
  Compare <em>I remember <strong>locking</strong> the door</em> (I did the locking) with <em>I remember <strong>being locked</strong> in</em> (someone did it to me). The switch from <em>-ing</em> to <em>being + past participle</em> is exactly the switch from active to passive.
</Callout>

## C — Verb + **object + -ing**: usually **no passive**

Verbs which in the active are followed by an **object (a noun phrase) + an -ing clause** usually have **no passive** form:

<SayIt text="I dread him (or his) finding out." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(✅ active) — but <s>He is dreaded finding out.</s> (❌ no passive)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-2xl border-2 border-rose-200 bg-rose-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-rose-700">Also — object + -ing, no passive</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-rose-900">anticipate, appreciate, dislike, forget, hate, imagine, like, (not) mind, recall, remember</p>
</div>

<Callout type="warning" title="Watch the overlap">
  A few verbs sit in <strong>both</strong> B and C — <em>dislike, hate, like, remember</em>. They take <em>being + past participle</em> (B) but resist a passive when they govern <em>an object + -ing</em> (C). <em>I remember <strong>being told</strong></em> ✅ &nbsp;·&nbsp; <em>I remember <strong>him telling</strong> me</em> ✅ (active only) — there is no <s>He is remembered telling me</s>.
</Callout>

## D — Verb (+ object) + **to-infinitive** → **be + past participle + to-infinitive**

The active pattern **verb + object + to-infinitive** is made passive with **be + past participle + to-infinitive**. Compare:

<SayIt text="Mr Wang has taught Peter to sing for years." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(active)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Peter has been taught to sing (by Mr Wang) for years." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(passive — the object <em>Peter</em> becomes the subject)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-2xl border-2 border-violet-200 bg-violet-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-violet-700">Also</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-violet-900">advise, allow, ask, believe, consider, expect, feel, instruct, mean, order, require, tell, understand</p>
</div>

Note that in some contexts it is possible to make **both** verbs passive:

<SayIt text="Changes to the taxation system are expected to be proposed." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(compare the active <em>We expect the government to propose changes to the taxation system.</em>)</p>

Some verbs followed by an **object + to-infinitive** in the active have **no passive**:

<SayIt text="Susan liked Karl to be there." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(✅ active) — but <s>Karl was liked to be there.</s> (❌ no passive)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-2xl border-2 border-amber-200 bg-amber-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-amber-700">No passive — the 'liking' and 'wanting' verbs</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-amber-900">(can't) bear, hate, love, need, prefer, want, wish</p>
</div>

<Callout type="warning" title="Wanting and liking block the passive">
  The verbs that express what the subject <em>likes</em> or <em>wants</em> keep the person doing the liking firmly in subject position, so the object can't be promoted: <em>I want you to help</em> has no <s>You are wanted to help</s>. Contrast the group in the box above (<em>believe, expect, order, require…</em>), which convert freely.
</Callout>

## E — Verb + **to-infinitive + object** → verb + **to be + past participle**

The active pattern **verb + to-infinitive + object** can be made passive with **verb + to be + past participle**. Compare:

<SayIt text="Supermarkets started to sell fresh pasta only in the 1990s." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(active)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Fresh pasta started to be sold in supermarkets only in the 1990s." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(passive — <em>started</em> stays, the infinitive goes passive: <em>to be sold</em>)</p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 grid gap-3 sm:grid-cols-2">
  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">Group (i) — meaning is preserved</p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">appear, begin, come, continue, seem, tend &nbsp;(and start)</p>
  </div>
  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-rose-200 bg-rose-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-rose-700">Group (ii) — meaning changes</p>
    <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-rose-900">agree, aim, arrange, attempt, forget, refuse, want</p>
  </div>
</div>

The verbs in **group (i)** (and *start*) have **corresponding** meanings in the active and passive; the verbs in **group (ii)** do **not**. Compare:

<SayIt text="People have come to see organic food as something only the wealthy eat." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(active) <strong>corresponds to</strong> ↓</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Organic food has come to be seen as something only the wealthy eat." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(passive — same idea, <em>come</em> is group (i))</p>

<div className="my-4" />

<SayIt text="Petra wanted to help me." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(active) <strong>does NOT correspond to</strong> ↓</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="I wanted to be helped by Petra." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(passive — but now <em>I</em> do the wanting, not Petra: <em>want</em> is group (ii))</p>

<Callout type="info" title="The whole unit on one card">
  <ul className="mt-1 space-y-1 text-sm">
    <li><strong>A</strong> · <em>see/hear/catch… + -ing</em> → <em>be + past participle + -ing</em> (<em>was seen climbing</em>)</li>
    <li><strong>B</strong> · <em>love/enjoy/deny… + -ing</em> → <em>being + past participle</em> (<em>love being given</em>)</li>
    <li><strong>C</strong> · <em>dread/imagine… + object + -ing</em> → <span className="font-semibold text-rose-700">no passive</span></li>
    <li><strong>D</strong> · <em>teach/expect/order… + object + to-inf</em> → <em>be + past participle + to-inf</em> (<em>was taught to sing</em>); but <em>want/like/prefer…</em> → <span className="font-semibold text-rose-700">no passive</span></li>
    <li><strong>E</strong> · <em>begin/come/agree… + to-inf + object</em> → <em>+ to be + past participle</em>; group (i) keeps the meaning, group (ii) changes it</li>
  </ul>
</Callout>
