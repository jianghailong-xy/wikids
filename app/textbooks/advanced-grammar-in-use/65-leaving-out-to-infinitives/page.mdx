English lets you avoid repeating a whole *to-infinitive* clause once the listener already knows what it would say. Often you keep just the word **to** and drop everything after it — *I'd love to*, *you can go if you want to*, *I meant to*. This unit is about the precise rules: when **to** can stand alone, when you must repeat **to be**, when even **to** disappears, and the cases where leaving **to** out is simply wrong.

## A — Keeping just *to* in place of the whole clause

We can sometimes use **to** instead of a clause beginning with a **to-infinitive** when it is clear from the context what we are talking about:

<SayIt text="I wanted to come with you, but I won't be able to." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(instead of <em>… I won't be able to come with you</em>.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="It might have been better if Rosa had asked for my help, but she chose not to." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(instead of <em>… she chose not to ask for my help</em>. Note that the negative <strong>not</strong> stays in front of <strong>to</strong>.)</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="With the verb be, repeat to be — not just to">
  When the verb in the first clause is a form of <strong>be</strong>, the <em>to-infinitive</em> form of <strong>be</strong> is repeated in the next clause or sentence:
  <br /><br />
  <SayIt text="Leon was frightened — or maybe he just pretended to be." />
  <br />
  ✅ <em>… he just pretended <strong>to be</strong>.</em> &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ <em>… he just pretended to.</em>
</Callout>

## B — Nouns, adjectives and verbs: drop the clause, keep *(to)*

After most **nouns** and **adjectives** that can be followed by a *to-infinitive clause*, we can leave out the *to-infinitive clause* **or** use **to**:

<SayIt text="I'm not going to write another book — at least I don't have any plans (to)." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(or <em>… I don't have any plans to write another book</em>.)</p>
<br />
<SayIt text="'Could you and Tom help me move house?' 'Well, I'm willing (to), and I'll ask Tom.'" />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">Nouns &amp; adjectives that work like this — to is optional</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">nouns: chance · idea · opportunity · plan · promise · suggestion</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">adjectives: afraid · delighted · determined · willing</p>
</div>

We can **also** leave out a *to-infinitive*, or use **to**, with **some verbs**:

<SayIt text="Alex will collect us by 10 o'clock. He promised (to)." />
<br />
<SayIt text="'You were supposed to buy some sugar.' 'Sorry, I forgot (to).'" />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">Verbs where you can drop the infinitive or keep to</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-900">agree · ask · begin · forget · promise · refuse · start · try</p>
</div>

But after verbs which **must have a complement** — a phrase that completes the meaning of the verb — we **can't** leave out **to**:

<SayIt text="I admit that I took her watch, but I didn't mean to." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">✅ <em>… I didn't mean <strong>to</strong>.</em> &nbsp; ❌ <em>… I didn't mean.</em></p>
<br />
<SayIt text="Have you thought about getting vaccinated before going there? I'd certainly advise you to." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">✅ <em>… I'd certainly advise you <strong>to</strong>.</em> &nbsp; ❌ <em>… I'd certainly advise you.</em></p>

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-xl border-2 border-rose-200 bg-rose-50 p-4">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-rose-700">Verbs that keep to — never drop it</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-sm font-semibold text-rose-900">afford · be able · choose · deserve · expect · fail · hate · hope · intend · love · need · prefer</p>
</div>

## C — *want*, *would like* and *like* in *if-* and *wh-*clauses

After **want** and **would like** in **if-clauses** and **wh-clauses**, we can often leave out the *to-infinitive* **or** use **to**:

<SayIt text="You're welcome to dance if you'd like (to)." />
<br />
<SayIt text="You can do whatever you would like (to)." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Call me Ben if you want (to)." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Come and see us when you want (to)." />

In **other** clauses (not *if-* and *wh-*clauses) we **include** **to**:

<SayIt text="I was planning to see you tomorrow, and I would still like to." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I offered to clean your car because I really want to, not because I hope to be paid." />

<Callout type="tip" title="The bare verb 'like' usually drops 'to'">
  In <em>if-</em> and <em>wh-</em>clauses we usually leave <strong>to</strong> out after the plain verb <strong>like</strong> — but it is optional after <strong>would like</strong>. Compare:
  <br /><br />
  • You can have one if you <strong>like</strong>. &nbsp;<span className="text-slate-400">↔</span>&nbsp; You can have one if you'd <strong>like (to)</strong>.<br />
  • Leave whenever you <strong>like</strong>. &nbsp;<span className="text-slate-400">↔</span>&nbsp; Leave whenever you'd <strong>like (to)</strong>.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Negatives always keep to — even in if-/wh-clauses">
  We <strong>include to</strong> with the negative forms of <strong>want</strong>, <strong>would like</strong> and <strong>like</strong>:
  <br /><br />
  <SayIt text="'Shall we go and visit Laura?' 'I don't really want to.'" />
  <br />
  <SayIt text="I should have phoned Jo last night, but it was so late when I got home I didn't like to." />
  <br />
  <SayIt text="'He won't mind you asking him for a loan.' 'Oh, no, I wouldn't like to.'" />
</Callout>

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-violet-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-2 text-sm">
    <div className="bg-violet-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-violet-900">Drop the infinitive — keep (to) or nothing</div>
    <div className="bg-rose-100 px-4 py-2 font-bold text-rose-900">Keep to — it can't go</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">most nouns &amp; adjectives (<em>plans (to)</em>, <em>willing (to)</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-rose-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">a verb with the verb <em>be</em> (<em>pretended <strong>to be</strong></em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 bg-violet-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">agree, ask, begin, refuse, start, try (<em>promised (to)</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-rose-100 bg-rose-50/50 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">verbs needing a complement (<em>didn't mean <strong>to</strong></em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-violet-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700"><em>want / would like</em> in <em>if-/wh-</em>clauses (<em>if you want (to)</em>)</div>
    <div className="border-t border-rose-100 px-4 py-3 text-slate-700">negative <em>want / would like / like</em> (<em>don't want <strong>to</strong></em>)</div>
  </div>
</div>
