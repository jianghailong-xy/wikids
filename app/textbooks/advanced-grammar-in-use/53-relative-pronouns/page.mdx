## A — The relative pronoun, and when you can leave it out

Both **defining** and **non-defining** relative clauses begin with a *relative pronoun* — **who, whom, which** or **that** — and that pronoun can sometimes be omitted. Knowing *when* is the whole game.

<SayIt text="We went to a beach (which / that) Ali had recommended to us." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">The relative pronoun refers back to <em>a beach</em>, but the subject of the relative clause is <em>Ali</em> — a different person. Because the pronoun is the <strong>object</strong> here, it can be dropped.</p>

Now compare a clause where the pronoun *is* the subject:

<SayIt text="I know a man who / that ran in the New York Marathon last year." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Here the pronoun refers to <em>a man</em>, and the subject of the relative clause is also <em>a man</em>. The pronoun does the subject's job, so it <strong>can't</strong> be left out.</p>

<Callout type="tip" title="The one test that decides it">
  Look at the relative clause on its own. Is there <strong>already another subject</strong> right after the pronoun (<em>… which <u>Ali</u> recommended</em>)? Then the pronoun is the <strong>object</strong> — you can drop it. If the pronoun is the first thing the verb needs (<em>… who <u>ran</u></em>), it's the <strong>subject</strong> — keep it. And in a <strong>non-defining</strong> clause (the kind set off by commas), you can never drop it, whatever its job.
</Callout>

## B — Defining relative clauses: which, who, that, whom, or nothing

A **defining** relative clause pins down *which* person or thing you mean — no commas. These are the pronouns you can use:

**Adding information about things**

| role in the clause | you can use |
|---|---|
| subject | **which** · **that** |
| object | **which** · **that** · **—** (no relative pronoun) |

**Adding information about people**

| role in the clause | you can use |
|---|---|
| subject | **who** · **that** |
| object | **who** · **that** · **—** (no relative pronoun) · **whom** |

A few advanced refinements on those choices:

- For **things**, the natural object choice in conversation is **that** (or no pronoun at all); **which** sounds more formal.

<SayIt text="Decorating's a job (that) I hate." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">More natural here than <em>… which I hate</em> in informal speech.</p>

- For **people**, we generally prefer **that** (or no pronoun) as object in informal contexts, rather than **who** or **whom**.

<SayIt text="He's the man (that) I met at Aisha's party." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Rather than the heavier <em>… who / whom I met …</em></p>

- **whom** is very formal and rare in speech, but it is the "correct" object form:

<SayIt text="The boy whom Elena had shouted at smiled." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Less formally: <em>that</em>, <em>who</em>, or no relative pronoun.</p>

<Callout type="info" title="Where 'that' is the strong default — even as subject">
  Use <strong>that</strong> as the <em>subject</em> pronoun after <strong>something</strong> and <strong>anything</strong>; after <strong>all, little, much, none</strong> used as pronouns; and after noun phrases with a <strong>superlative</strong>. (<em>Which</em> is possible after <em>something/anything</em> but much less common.)
  <br />
  <SayIt text="These walls are all that remain of the city." />
  <span className="text-sm text-slate-500">Not <em>… which remain of the city</em>.</span>
</Callout>

As the **object** after those same words — *something / anything*, *all*, a superlative — use **that** or **no pronoun** (and avoid *who* with a superlative people-phrase):

<SayIt text="She's one of the kindest people (that) I know." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Not <em>… one of the kindest people who I know</em>.</p>

## C — Non-defining relative clauses: keep the pronoun

A **non-defining** relative clause adds extra, non-essential information and is marked off by **commas**. The choices are narrower:

**Adding information about things**

| role in the clause | you can use |
|---|---|
| subject | **which** (· *that*) |
| object | **which** (· *that*) |

**Adding information about people**

| role in the clause | you can use |
|---|---|
| subject | **who** |
| object | **who** · **whom** |

The key points:

- You **must** include a relative pronoun in a non-defining clause — you can never drop it.
- For people as object, use **who** or **whom**, though **whom** is very formal:

<SayIt text="Professor Johnson, who(m) I have long admired, is to visit the university next week." />

- For things, use **which** as subject or object. **That** is sometimes used instead, but many people consider that incorrect in a non-defining clause:

<SayIt text="The Master's course, which I took in 2001, is no longer taught." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">Or, less safely, <em>… that I took …</em></p>

<Callout type="warning" title="Two non-defining traps">
  ❌ <em>The singer, that was recovering from flu, …</em> &nbsp;→&nbsp; ✅ <em>The singer, <strong>who</strong> was recovering from flu, …</em><br />
  ❌ <em>She was influenced by her father, she adored.</em> &nbsp;→&nbsp; ✅ <em>… her father, <strong>who(m)</strong> she adored.</em> (the pronoun can't be dropped)
</Callout>
