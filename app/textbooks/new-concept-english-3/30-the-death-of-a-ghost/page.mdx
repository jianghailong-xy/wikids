## The Text 课文

For years, villagers believed that Endley Farm **was haunted**. The farm was owned by two brothers, Joe and Bob Cox. They employed a few farmhands, but no one was willing to work there long. Every time a worker **gave up his job**, he told the same story. Farm labourers said that they always woke up to find that work had been done overnight. Hay had been cut and cowsheds had been cleaned. A farm worker, who **stayed up all night**, claimed to have seen a figure cutting corn in the moonlight. In time, it became an accepted fact that the Cox brothers employed a **conscientious** ghost that did most of their work for them.

No one suspected that there might be someone else on the farm who had never been seen. This was indeed the case. A short time ago, villagers were astonished to learn that the ghost of Endley had died. Everyone went to the funeral, for the 'ghost' was **none other than** Eric Cox, a third brother who **was supposed to have died** as a young man. After the funeral, Joe and Bob revealed a secret which they had kept for over fifty years.

Eric had been the eldest son of the family, very much older than his two brothers. He had been obliged to join the army during the Second World War. As he hated army life, he decided to **desert his regiment**. When he learnt that he would be sent abroad, he returned to the farm and his father hid him until the end of the war. Fearing the authorities, Eric remained in hiding after the war as well. His father told everybody that Eric had been killed in action. The only other people who knew the secret were Joe and Bob. They did not even tell their wives. When their father died, they thought it their duty to keep Eric in hiding. All these years, Eric had lived **as a recluse**. He used to sleep during the day and work at night, **quite unaware of the fact** that he had become the ghost of Endley. When he died, however, his brothers found it impossible to keep the secret any longer.

## 课文注释 Notes on the text

<Callout type="info" title="6 个值得停下来嚼一嚼的点">
  1. **every time** (l.3) — "每当……"。**every time** 在此作连词，相当于 **whenever**，引导时间状语从句。同型：*Every time I see that old photo, I think of my grandfather.*（每次看到那张老照片，我就想起我祖父。）注意：every time 后面直接接从句，不用再加 when。

  2. **stay up** (l.7) — "熬夜"。短语动词，up 表"醒着、活跃"的状态。同型：*I stayed up until midnight to finish the report.* 它的反义是 **stay in bed**（赖床）。课文里那个农工 *stayed up all night* —— 彻夜未眠就是为了逮住那个"鬼"。

  3. **In time** (l.8) — "最后、终于"。注意和 **on time**（准时）区分。**in time** 强调"经过一段时间后、终究"：*In time, you'll understand why I did it.*（总有一天你会理解的。）课文 *In time, it became an accepted fact* —— 随着时间的推移，这件事竟成了公认的事实。另外，that 引导的从句作 **fact 的同位语**（= the fact **that** the Cox brothers employed a conscientious ghost ...）。

  4. **none other than** (l.12) — "不是别人，正是……"。用于揭晓一个出人意料的人物身份，带有戏剧性。同型：*The stranger was none other than my long-lost uncle.*（那陌生人不是别人，正是我失散多年的叔叔。）课文中用来揭示"鬼"的真身——这种先制造悬念再揭晓的笔法，是叙事文常用的技巧。

  5. **was supposed to have died** (l.13) — "本被认为已经死了"。**be supposed to + 完成式不定式** 表达"人们本以为（过去）发生了某事"，但事实可能相反。课文中人们以为 Eric 年轻时已经战死——当然不是。同型：*He is supposed to have arrived yesterday.*（按理他昨天应该到了——但实际未必。）注意这和第 7 课的 suppose 难点密切相关（见第 4 节）。

  6. **found it impossible to keep the secret** (l.22) — "发现无法再保守秘密"。这里的 **it** 是**形式宾语**，真正的宾语是后面的 **to keep the secret any longer**。结构是：**find + it + 形容词 + to do**。同型：*I found it hard to believe his story.*（我发现很难相信他的说法。）用这个结构比直接说 *his brothers couldn't keep the secret any longer* 更多了一层"他们一直努力在守，但终于守不住"的被动挣扎感——这就是 formal object 在叙事中的微妙力量。
</Callout>

## 3. 关键句型 短语动词 give / say & tell / do & make

<Callout type="tip" title="一分钟回顾">
  本课 Key structures 围绕三组常用词辨析展开，它们各自在课文里埋了线索：

  | 组别 | 关键区别 | 课文出处 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **give up** vs **give in** vs **give away** vs **give oneself up** | give up = 放弃（及物/不及物）；give in = 屈服/投降（不及物）；give away = 赠送/泄露；give oneself up = 自首 | l.3 *Every time a worker **gave up** his job* |
  | **say** vs **tell** | say 着重"说的话"（say sth / say that ...）；tell 着重"告诉谁"（tell sb sth） | l.4 *he **told** the same story* |
  | **do** vs **make** | do 强调"做动作/完成任务"；make 强调"制造/创造" | l.9 *that **did** most of their work* |

  ### ⚠️ 三个最容易踩的雷区

  1. **give up** 和 **give in** 都能译成"放弃"，但 give up 可以带宾语（give up smoking），give in 不能带宾语（He refused to give in, 不是 *give in smoking*）。
  2. **say** 不能直接跟人：不能说 *He said me the story*，要用 *He told me the story* 或 *He said to me that*。
  3. **do** 和 **make** 有很多固定搭配，不能互换：**do** one's best / a favour / the shopping；**make** a mistake / a speech / a decision / an effort。
</Callout>

## 4. 难点：suppose 与 used to Special difficulties

<Callout type="info" title="suppose 的三种用法">
  教材 SD 7：围绕课文 *a third brother who **was supposed to have died** as a young man*（l.12-13），掌握 suppose 的三副面孔：

  | 结构 | 含义 | 例句 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **I suppose (that)** | "我想/我猜"——表推测，没那么确定 | *I suppose you're right.*（我想你是对的。） |
  | **He is supposed to do ...** | "按理他应该做……"——表义务/预期（现在） | *He is supposed to be at work now.*（按理此刻他在上班。） |
  | **He was supposed to have done ...** | "本被认为已经……"——表过去预期（但事实可能相反） | *He was supposed to have died as a young man.*（人们本认为他年轻时就死了——但他其实还活着。） |

  ### used to do vs was doing

  教材 IKS 31：*He **used to sleep** during the day*（l.21）。
  - **used to + do** = 过去常常做（现在不做了）：*He **used to work** at night, but now he rests.*
  - **was/were + doing** = 过去某个时刻正在做：*He **was working** in the fields when I saw him.*（我看见他时他正在田里干活。）
</Callout>

## 11. 作文 Composition (≤ 250 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  Write an imaginary account in about **250 words** of **the night a farm worker saw a figure cutting corn**. Write in **the first person**. Use the ideas given below. Do not write more than four paragraphs.

  **Title: The ghost of Endley.**

  **Introduction:** I noticed that work had been done overnight. I decided to stay up all night — sat in barn.

  **Development:** I fell asleep — suddenly woke up — went to cowshed — it had already been cleaned — went out to fields — saw a figure working — rushed back to farmhouse — woke up others — we went out — no one there.

  **Conclusion:** Sure it was a ghost — decided not to work at Endley Farm any longer — told story to villagers.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="提示骨架">
  - **开头 (发现异常)**：连续几天早上发现活儿已被干完——干草切好，牛棚清理——决心彻夜盯守。
  - **展开 (那晚的经过)**：躲在谷仓 —— 不小心睡着 —— 被一声响动惊醒 —— 先去牛棚（已打扫完） —— 冲进田地 —— 月光下看见一个人影在割庄稼 —— 心跳加速 —— 跑回农场叫人 —— 一群人举灯冲出去 —— 田里空无一人。
  - **关键细节**：月光 (moonlight)、割庄稼 (cutting corn)、人影的轮廓 (silhouette / figure)、跑回时的恐惧与激动、众人返回田地时的寂静。
  - **收尾 (决心与传播)**：确信那就是鬼 —— 天一亮收拾东西走人 —— 从此恩得利闹鬼的故事传遍全村。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架">
  - **时态主线**：全篇用**一般过去时**（narrated in the past tense），因为是"那一夜的回忆"。
  - **第一人称**：全部用 *I / we* —— *I noticed ... I decided ... I hid ... I saw ... I ran ...*。
  - **感官词库**：*a pale moon hung over the fields, the cold air, the rustle of corn, my heart pounding, a dark silhouette, the silence after we rushed out*。
  - **结构亮点**：用短句制造紧张感 —— *I froze. There it was — a figure, cutting corn. I ran.* 用感官细节而非抽象叙述来"演"那一夜。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  **The ghost of Endley**

  For three mornings in a row I had woken to find the day's work already done — hay cut, the cowshed swept clean. No one else on the farm admitted to having done it. That evening I made up my mind: I would stay awake all night and catch whoever, or whatever, was out there.

  I sat in the barn with a lantern beside me. The hours dragged; the cold crept through my coat. I must have fallen asleep, for the next thing I knew the moon was high and an unfamiliar silence hung over the yard. I crept out to the cowshed first. It had already been cleaned — the straw was fresh and the pails were stacked. My heart began to beat faster. I walked out towards the cornfield, and then I saw him.

  A figure — a man — was working under the full moon, cutting the corn in steady, silent strokes. The blade caught the moonlight each time it rose. I could not see his face. I stood frozen for what felt like a minute, then turned and ran as fast as I could back to the farmhouse. I woke the others and, breathless, told them what I had seen. We took lamps and went out together, but the field was empty. Not a stalk of corn had been disturbed — or so it seemed in the dark.

  I knew then that the farm was haunted. At sunrise I packed my things and left Endley Farm for good. The story I told in the village that same morning was the story every farmhand would go on telling for the next fifty years. *(259 words)*
</Callout>

## 12. 书信写作 Letter writing (约 100 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  You have already written **several letters to a friend** but he has failed to answer them. Write a letter of about **100 words** in **three paragraphs** **complaining** that he has not written to you and **asking him to give news of himself**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架">
  - **称呼**：*Dear + 名*（朋友间随意亲切）。
  - **第一段（抱怨）**：I've written several letters to you but haven't heard back. What's happened? Are you all right? I'm starting to worry.
  - **第二段（催问近况）**：How are things? How's your work? Any news? Do write and let me know what you've been up to.
  - **第三段（收尾）**：Please drop me a line even if it's short — just to let me know you're alive! Hope to hear from you soon.
  - **语气要点**：是朋友间的抱怨，要用**半开玩笑半认真**的口气——不能冷冰冰的，但又要让对方感受到"你确实该回信了"。
  - **结束语**：*Yours / Best wishes / With love*（随你和朋友的关系选）。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  Dear Tom,

  This is my fourth letter to you and I still haven't had a single reply. Are you still alive over there, or have you joined the ghost of Endley? I'm beginning to wonder whether my letters are reaching you at all — or whether you've simply forgotten how to hold a pen.

  Do tell me what you've been up to. How is the new job? Has your family moved into the new house yet? I've been waiting for your news for months now, and my curiosity is rapidly turning into mild annoyance!

  Please drop me a line — even a postcard would do — just to let me know you're still on this planet. I promise to stop complaining the moment I hear from you.

  Yours,
  Jack *(about 100 words)*
</Callout>

## 13. 拓展练习 Going further

<Callout type="tip" title="自学者建议">
  - **影子跟读 (shadowing)**：用第 1 节的三个 SayIt 按钮跟读 3-5 遍。特别注意 *none other than* 那句中 *supposed to have died* 的连读和节奏——*səˈpəʊzd tə həv daɪd*——英式发音中 supposed 的 -ed 和 to 会连读成 /tə/，而 *have* 弱读为 /əv/。
  - **复述 (retell)**：合上课文，用 1 分钟把这个悬疑故事讲出来。**叙事骨架**：闹鬼传闻（雇工们的故事）→ 鬼死了（全村参加葬礼）→ 真相大白（鬼是大哥 Eric，本被认为已死）→ 背后的秘密（二战逃兵、父亲藏匿、两兄弟守密 50 年）→ 结局（人死，秘密终于公开）。
  - **造句迁移**：用今天的 5 个高价值结构造你自己的句子——
    1. **give up + 名词**（放弃）—— *He gave up his seat for the old lady.*
    2. **stay up**（熬夜）—— *We stayed up talking until three in the morning.*
    3. **none other than**（不是别人正是）—— *The winner was none other than my next-door neighbour.*
    4. **was supposed to have done**（本以为已……）—— *The letter was supposed to have arrived last week, but it never came.*
    5. **find + it + adj + to do**（发现做……很……）—— *I found it difficult to say no to her.*
  - **延伸思考**：这是一篇"**反转式叙事**"——作者先用一整段铺陈"鬼故事"的细节，把读者的预期牢牢锚定在"超自然恐怖故事"上，然后第二段用一句 *No one suspected that there might be someone else on the farm who had never been seen* 轻轻一转，故事从"鬼故事"变成了"人情故事"。这个技巧英文叫 **defamiliarization → reframing**（先制造陌生感的解释，再给出合理的、甚至更动人的解释）。下一次你写故事时试试：**先让读者相信一件事（哪怕是错的），再揭示真相**——比从第一句就坦白要动人得多。另一个值得咀嚼的点：故事的悲凉底色——Eric 为了逃避战争而躲藏了一生，最后活成了自己都不知道的"鬼"——这本身就是一个隐喻：**A man hiding from the world becomes a ghost long before he dies.**（一个逃避世界的人，远在死之前就已经成了鬼。）
</Callout>
