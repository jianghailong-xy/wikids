## The Text 课文

We are less **credulous** than we used to be. In the nineteenth century, a novelist would bring his story to a conclusion by presenting his readers with a series of coincidences — most of which were wildly **improbable**. Readers happily accepted the fact that an **obscure** maidservant was really the hero's mother. A long-lost brother, who was **presumed** dead, was really alive and **wickedly plotting** to bring about the hero's **downfall**. And so on. Modern readers would find such **naive** solutions totally **unacceptable**. Yet, in real life, circumstances do sometimes **conspire** to bring about coincidences which anyone but a nineteenth-century novelist would find incredible.

When I was a boy, my grandfather told me about a German taxi driver, Franz Bussman, who found a brother who was thought to have been killed twenty years before. While on a walking tour with his wife, he stopped to talk to a workman. After they had gone on, Mrs Bussman **commented on** the workman's close **resemblance** to her husband and even suggested that he might be his brother. Franz **poured scorn on** the idea, pointing out that his brother had been killed in action during the war. Though Mrs Bussman was fully **acquainted with** this story, she thought that there was a chance in a million that she might be right. A few days later, she sent a boy to the workman to ask if his name was Hans Bussman. **Needless to say**, the man's name was Hans Bussman, and he really was Franz's long-lost brother. When the brothers were reunited, Hans explained how it was that he was still alive. After having been wounded towards the end of the war, he was sent to hospital and was separated from his unit. The hospital had been bombed and Hans had made his way back to Western Germany on foot. Meanwhile, his unit was lost and all records of him had been destroyed. Hans returned to his family home, but the house had been bombed and no one in the neighbourhood knew what had **become of** the inhabitants. **Assuming** that his family had been killed during an air raid, Hans settled down in a village fifty miles away where he had remained ever since.

## 课文注释 Notes on the text

<Callout type="info" title="10 个值得停下来嚼一嚼的点">

**1. bring...to a conclusion** — 使……结束，为……收尾

> The author brought his novel *to a conclusion* by reuniting the two brothers.（作者通过让两兄弟重聚为小说画上了句号。）

**2. bring about** — 造成，导致（结果）

> Coincidences *brought about* a remarkable reunion.（一系列巧合促成了一次不同寻常的重聚。）

**3. and so on** — 等等，诸如此类

> Novels contained long-lost relatives, false identities, *and so on*.

**4. do sometimes conspire** — do 为助动词，对谓语动词 conspire 起强调作用

> Life does *sometimes* produce stranger plots than fiction.（生活有时候确实比小说还要离奇。）

**5. while on a walking tour** — while 后省略了与主句主语相同的主谓 (he was)

> *While on holiday*, she ran into an old friend.（度假期间，她偶遇了一位老朋友。）

**6. pour scorn on...** — 讥笑……，嘲弄……

> He *poured scorn on* the idea, insisting it was impossible.（他对这个想法嗤之以鼻，坚称这不可能。）

**7. point out** — 指出，提请注意

> She *pointed out* that his brother had been killed during the war.（她指出他的兄弟在战争中已经牺牲了。）

**8. be acquainted with** — 已知道，熟悉

> Though she was fully *acquainted with* the story, she still believed.（尽管她对这段往事了如指掌，她仍然相信。）

**9. needless to say** — 不用说，当然（用于引出意料之中的结果）

> *Needless to say*, the result surprised everyone.（当然，结果出乎所有人的意料。）

**10. become of** — 发生……情况，结果怎样

> What had *become of* the inhabitants?（居民们最终怎样了？）

</Callout>

我们不再像以往那样轻易相信别人了。在 19 世纪，小说家常常在小说结尾处给读者准备一系列巧合——大部分是牵强附会的，极不可能的。当时的读者却愉快地接受这样一些事实：一个低贱的女佣实际上是主人公的母亲；主人公一位长期失踪的兄弟，大家都以为死了，实际上一直活着，并且正在策划暗算主人公；如此等等。现代读者会觉得这种天真的结局完全无法接受。不过，在现实生活中，有时确实会出现一些巧合。这些巧合除了 19 世纪小说家外谁也不会相信。

当我还是个孩子的时候，我祖父给我讲了一位德国出租汽车司机弗朗兹·巴斯曼如何找到了据信已在 20 年前死去的兄弟的事。一次，他与妻子徒步旅行。途中，停下来与一个工人交谈，接着他们继续往前走去。巴斯曼夫人说那个工人与她丈夫相貌很像，甚至猜测他可能就是她丈夫的兄弟。弗朗兹对此不屑一顾，指出他的兄弟已在战争中阵亡了。尽管巴斯曼夫人熟知这段情况，但她仍然认为自己的想法仍有百万分之一的可能性。几天后，她派了一个男孩去问那人是否叫汉斯·巴斯曼。不出巴斯曼夫人所料，那人的名字真是汉斯·巴斯曼，他确实是弗朗兹失散了多年的兄弟。兄弟俩团聚之时，汉斯说明了他活下来的经过。战争即将结束时，他负伤被送进医院，并与部队失去联系。医院遭到轰炸，汉斯步行回到了西德。与此同时，他所在的部队被击溃，他的所有档案材料全部毁于战火。汉斯重返故里，但他的家已被炸毁，左邻右舍谁也不知原住户的下落。汉斯以为全家人都在空袭中遇难，于是便在距此 50 英里外的一座村子里定居下来，直至当日。

## 3. 关键句型：used to / would & After having been done Grammar focus

<Callout type="tip" title="一分钟回顾">

**① used to + 动词原形** — 表示过去的习惯（现在已不再如此）

> We are less credulous than we **used to be**.（我们不再像以前那样轻信了。）

⚠️ `used to` 只用于过去习惯，无现在时和进行时形式。

---

**② would + 动词原形** — 表示过去经常做的动作（比 used to 更强调重复性）

> In the nineteenth century, a novelist **would bring** his story to a conclusion with coincidences.

| 结构 | 例句 |
|------|------|
| used to | I **used to** live near here.（我以前住在这附近。）|
| would | She **would** visit us every Sunday.（她每周日都会来看我们。）|

⚠️ `would` 不能表示过去的**状态**：`I used to live here` ✓，`I would live here` ✗

---

**③ After having been + 过去分词** — 完成被动分词短语，表示主语“在被……之后"

> **After having been wounded** towards the end of the war, he was sent to hospital.（在战争即将结束时负伤后，他被送进了医院。）

= After he had been wounded...

</Callout>

## 4. 难点：巧合叙事与让步状语 Special difficulties

<Callout type="info" title="Though / Although / Even though — 让步从句的细微差别">

本课出现了一个典型让步结构：

> **Though** Mrs Bussman was fully acquainted with this story, she thought there was a chance in a million...（尽管巴斯曼夫人对这段往事了如指掌，她仍然认为……）

三个词都表“尽管”，但有细微差别：

| 词 | 语气 | 用法 |
|---|---|---|
| though | 较随意，口语书面均可 | 可置于句末作副词 |
| although | 较正式 | 只能置句首，不作副词 |
| even though | 强调让步，语气最强 | 用于出乎意料的对比 |

> She kept smiling, **though** it was hard.（她仍然微笑，尽管很难。）
> It was hard, **though**.（这很难，不过。）✓（though 作副词）
> It was hard, **although**.（✗ — although 不能置句末作副词）

</Callout>

## 11. 作文 Composition

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  用不超过 **250 个单词**，以弗朗兹·巴斯曼的口吻写一篇**想象中的自述**，讲述他从战争结束到与兄弟重聚之间的人生经历。分四段，标题：*The past*（往事）。

  **素材提示：**
  - *Introduction*: Tried to get information about Hans — none available — gave up search.
  - *Development*: Moved from place to place, from job to job — how he met Mrs Bussman — marriage — settled — became a cook — disliked it — went into partnership — became a taxi driver — once visited home town — block of flats where house used to be — no one remembered him.
  - *Conclusion*: Future plans now that Hans has been found.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="提示骨架">
- 开头：战后不断打听 Hans 的消息，一无所获，最终放弃
- 发展：颠沛流离，换工作，认识了妻子，结婚定居，某次回老家只看到了公寓楼
- 结尾：重聚后的感受与未来计划
- 注意用 **used to / would** 描述过去反复的状态，用 **After having been...** 引出经历转折
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  *The past*

  After the war, I tried for months to find out what had become of Hans. I wrote letters, visited offices, spoke to soldiers who had served near him. Nothing. I was finally told that all records of his unit had been destroyed. With great sadness, I accepted that my brother was dead.

  For years, life was unsettled. I moved from city to city, working as a cook, then as a delivery man. Eventually I met my wife, and things began to improve. We married and I became a taxi driver — a job I have kept ever since. Once, I drove back to our old street, hoping to find someone who remembered us. There was a large block of flats where our house used to stand. Not a single person recognised my name.

  Now everything has changed. Hans is alive. He was there all along, just fifty miles away. We have already agreed to spend more time together — to make up for the thirty years we lost. Next month, our families will meet for the first time. Life, as someone once told me, is stranger than any novel.
</Callout>
