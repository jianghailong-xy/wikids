</Callout>

## The Text 课文

It has been said that everyone lives **by selling something**. **In the light of** this statement, teachers live by selling knowledge, philosophers by selling wisdom and priests by selling spiritual comfort. Though it may be possible to measure the value of **material goods** in terms of money, it is extremely difficult to estimate the true value of the services which people perform for us. There are times when we would **willingly give** everything we possess to save our lives, yet we **might grudge** paying a surgeon a high fee for offering us precisely this service. The conditions of society are such that **skills have to be paid for** in the same way that goods are paid for at a shop. Everyone has something to sell.

**Tramps**, it seems, are the only exception to this general rule. Beggars almost **sell themselves as human beings** to **arouse the pity of** passers-by. But real tramps are not beggars. They have nothing to sell and require nothing from others. In seeking independence, they **do not sacrifice** their human dignity. A tramp may ask you for money, but he will never ask you to **feel sorry for** him. He has deliberately chosen to **lead the life** he leads and is **fully aware of** the consequences. He may never be sure where the next meal is coming from, but he is **free from** the thousands of anxieties which **afflict** other people. His few material possessions **make it possible for him** to move from place to place with ease. By having to sleep in the open, he gets **far closer to** the world of nature than most of us ever do. He may hunt, beg, or steal occasionally to keep himself alive; he may even, **in times of** real need, do a little work; but he will never sacrifice his freedom. We often speak of tramps **with contempt** and put them in the same class as beggars, but how many of us can honestly say that we have not felt a little **envious of** their simple way of life and their freedom from care?

## 课文注释 Notes on the text

<Callout type="info" title="7 个值得停下来嚼一嚼的点">
  1. **live by selling something** (l.1) — "靠卖……为生"。**by + 动名词** = 通过……方式。同型：*He lives by writing. / She earns her living by teaching.* 注意区别 **live on**（以……为食/为生）：*He lives on bread and water.* 教材 Key structures 专练这个结构——要求用 *by* 改写句子。

  2. **in the light of** (l.1) — "根据、按照、鉴于"。同型：*In the light of recent discoveries, the theory needs rethinking. / In the light of what you've told me, I've changed my mind.* 相当于 *in view of / considering*。

  3. **though ... yet ...** 让步转折 (ll.3-5) — 虽然……但是……。课文 *Though it may be possible to measure ..., yet we might grudge ...* ——注意 though 和 yet 不在同一个分句里（though 引出从句，yet 引出主句）。**though** 也可放句末表"不过、然而"——*It's hard work. I enjoy it, though.* 教材 Structure 第 5 题就是 though 的用法：把两个句子用 though 连起来。

  4. **might grudge paying** (l.6) — "可能会舍不得付……"。**grudge + 动名词** = 舍不得做、不愿给。同型：*He grudges spending money on clothes. / I grudge paying so much for a cup of coffee.* 注意 **grudge** 后接**动名词**（不是不定式）。

  5. **make it possible for sb. to do** (l.14) — "使某人有可能做……"。**it** 是形式宾语，真正宾语是后面的 **to move**。同型 Lesson 55 的 *make it possible to detect*……同型：*The internet makes it possible for us to work from home.* 这是 make + it + adj + for sb. + to do 的完整框架。

  6. **By having to sleep in the open, he gets far closer to the world of nature** (ll.15-16) — "由于不得不露宿野外，他反而比我们大多数人更亲近自然。" **By + 动名词** 在此表"通过/由于……的方式"——句首引出方式状语。**far closer to** 用 far 加强比较级（远更亲近）。同型：*By walking everywhere, she stays far healthier than those who drive. / By reading every night, he got far closer to understanding the subject.*

  7. **How many of us can honestly say that we have not felt ...?** (ll.19-21) — 这是一个**反问句**——"我们有多少人能老实说自己从未……？" 实际上在说"我们几乎都曾……"。以反问收束全篇，不直接下结论，而是把读者拉进来一起反思——这是英文议论文一个很高级的结尾手法。同型：*How many of us can truly claim that we have never wasted a single hour online?*
</Callout>

</Callout>

## 3. 关键句型 让步与对比 Concession & contrast

<Callout type="tip" title="一分钟回顾">
  本课是一篇"对比型"议论文——作者在"普通人"与"流浪汉"两种生活方式之间来回穿梭，核心靠**让步结构**和**对比结构**撑起来：

  | 结构 | 课文例句 | 用途 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **live by + doing** | *everyone lives **by selling** something* | "靠……为生" |
  | **though ..., yet ...** | ***Though** it may be possible ..., **yet** we might grudge ...* | "虽然……但是……"（让步） |
  | **may ..., but ...** | *A tramp **may** ask you for money, **but** he will never ...* | "也许会……但绝不会……"（先让步后否定） |
  | **make it possible for sb. to do** | *make it possible for him **to move*** | "使某人有可能做……"（it 形式宾语） |
  | **By + doing, ...** | ***By** having to sleep in the open, he gets ...* | "由于/通过……，……"（方式/原因状语） |
  | **far + 比较级** | ***far** closer to the world of nature* | "远远更……"（far 加强比较级） |
  | **in the light of** | ***In the light of** this statement* | "根据、按照" |

  ### ⚠️ 4 个本课最容易踩的雷区

  1. **live by doing** vs **live on sth.**——by 后面是"谋生方式"（动名词），on 后面是"赖以生存的东西"（名词）。*He lives **by** writing. / He lives **on** bread.* 不可混。
  2. **grudge + doing**——**grudge**（舍不得）后面跟动名词，不是不定式。*He grudges **paying** so much.*
  3. **though ... yet ...**——though 和 yet 不在同一个分句里；though 引从句，yet 引主句。但现代英文中，主句通常不加 yet——*Though it's hard, (yet) I enjoy it.* yet 加不加都行，但不可用 but。
  4. **make it possible for sb. to do**——it 是形式宾语，真正宾语是 **to do**，漏了 it 句子就缺宾语。*His few possessions make **it** possible for him to move.*
</Callout>

## 4. 难点：by + 动名词 vs with + 名词 Special difficulties

<Callout type="info" title="教材 Key structures 改写题">
  教材要求用 **by + 动名词** 改写句子。本课核心语法点之一：用 by + doing 表达"通过……方式"来做某事。

  > 例题：*He teaches people about values. He earns his living in this way.*
  > → *He earns his living **by teaching** people about values.*

  ### by + doing vs with + 名词

  | 结构 | 含义 | 示例 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **by + doing** | 通过做……（方式/手段） | *He earns money **by teaching**.* |
  | **with + 名词** | 用……（工具/伴随） | *He cut it **with a knife**.* |

  - **by** 强调"方式/手段"，后接动名词或抽象名词：*by working hard / by accident*
  - **with** 强调"使用的工具/伴随状态"：*with a pen / with a smile / with ease*

  课文两处经典示范：*live **by** selling*（方式） vs *move from place to place **with** ease*（伴随状态）。

## 10. 摘要写作 Summary writing (≤ 80 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  *In not more than 80 words* describe **how tramps differ from everyone else and what their attitude to life is**. **Do not include anything that is not in the last paragraph**（只用最后一段的信息）。

  按下面的提示点串成一段：

  1. 流浪汉与芸芸众生有何不同（什么都不卖、什么都不要）？
  2. 他们如何对待自己的尊严？
  3. 他们的物质条件与精神自由之间的对比是什么？
  4. 普通人对待流浪汉的态度里藏着怎样的矛盾？
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  Unlike everyone else, tramps have nothing to sell and require nothing from others. They do not sacrifice their human dignity: a tramp may ask for money, but never for pity. He has deliberately chosen this life and is fully aware of the consequences. Although he never knows where his next meal will come from, he is free from the anxieties that afflict other people. His few possessions allow him to move easily, and by sleeping in the open he gets closer to nature. He will never sacrifice his freedom. We may speak of tramps with contempt, yet many of us secretly envy their simple life and freedom from care. *(79 words)*
</Callout>

## 11. 作文 Composition (约 300 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  Write a composition in about **300 words** on one of the following:

  **a.** What do you think is the most important thing in life — material possessions or spiritual freedom?

  **b.** Suppose you had to live without money for one year. Describe how you would live and what you would learn from the experience.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="提示骨架（以 a 题为例）">
  - **开头（引用课文）**：*"It has been said that everyone lives by selling something." But is selling — and buying — all there is to life?*
  - **正方：物质不可缺**——*shelter, food, medicine, education* ——没有物质基础，自由只是空谈（*freedom on an empty stomach is an illusion*）。
  - **反方：精神自由更贵**——引用流浪汉的例子：*he is free from the thousands of anxieties which afflict other people*。现代社会"越有越焦虑"的悖论——*the more we own, the more we worry about losing*。
  - **自己的立场**（可折中）——*balance* 比极端重要：*enough material security to be free, but not so much that possessions own you.*
  - **结尾（呼应课文）**：*Perhaps the tramp's secret is not having nothing, but wanting nothing.*
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架">
  - **时态主线**：全篇用**一般现在时**（议论文原则——讨论的是普遍真理，不是特定事件）。
  - **"价值观/生活方式"词库**：*material possessions, spiritual freedom, peace of mind, anxiety, consumerism, contentment, dignity, independence, simplicity, pursuit of happiness*。
  - **"权衡/对比"句式**：*On the one hand ... On the other hand ... / While it is true that ..., it is equally true that ... / The question is not whether ... but whether ...*
  - **结构亮点**：用课文开篇句 *It has been said that ...* 作引子，再提出异议——"但真是这样吗？"——形成 **hook + challenge** 的开头模式。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  "It has been said that everyone lives by selling something." The opening line of our text captures a truth about modern life: we are all part of a vast marketplace, trading skills, time, and ideas for money. But is there more to life than buying and selling?

  Material possessions are undeniably important. Without a roof over our heads, food on the table, and access to medicine and education, life becomes a daily struggle for survival. Freedom on an empty stomach is, at best, a hollow concept. The tramp in our text may be free, but he also goes hungry, sleeps in the cold, and occasionally has to steal to keep himself alive. That is not a freedom most of us would willingly choose.

  Yet spiritual freedom — peace of mind, freedom from anxiety, the ability to live as one wishes — may be even more precious. Our text points to a paradox at the heart of modern life: the more we own, the more we worry. The tramp, with his few possessions, is free from "the thousands of anxieties which afflict other people." Meanwhile, the successful businessman lies awake at night, his mind racing with deadlines, mortgages, and market fluctuations. Who is truly richer?

  I believe the answer lies not in choosing one extreme over the other, but in finding a balance. We need enough material security to be free — free from hunger, free from fear of homelessness — but not so much that our possessions begin to own us. The tramp's secret, perhaps, is not that he has nothing, but that he wants nothing. In a world that constantly tells us to want more, that may be the most radical freedom of all.

  We speak of tramps with contempt, the writer notes. But as I put down my pen, I wonder: how many of us, deep down, have not felt a little envious of their freedom from care? *(约 300 词)*
</Callout>

## 12. 书信写作 Letter writing (约 100 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  You have just moved into a new flat. Write a letter to a friend of about **100 words** **inviting** him or her to come and see it, giving **directions** on how to get there, and offering to **meet** him or her at the station.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架">
  - **称呼**：*Dear + 名*（朋友间用名字，亲切自然）。
  - **开头**：搬家喜讯 + 邀请 —— *I've finally moved into my new flat! You must come and see it.*
  - **路线说明**（清晰三步）：
    1. *Get off at X station and take Exit B.*
    2. *Walk straight down Y Road until you see the post office on your left.*
    3. *My building is the red-brick one directly opposite — Flat 3B, second floor.*
  - **主动接站**：*Let me know what time your train arrives and I'll meet you at the station.*
  - **收尾**：期待见面 —— *Can't wait to show you around!* / *Looking forward to seeing you.*
  - **结束语**：*Yours, / With love, / [你的名字]*。
  - **语气要点**：热情、随意、朋友间的口吻；不要写成正式邀请函。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  Dear Lucy,

  I've finally moved into my new flat — you must come and see it! It's small but full of light, and there's a little balcony where we can have tea.

  Getting here is easy. Take the Northern Line to Kentish Town, come out of Exit B, and walk straight down Leighton Road for about five minutes. You'll see a red-brick building on the right, just past the bookshop. I'm in Flat 3B on the second floor — ring the buzzer and I'll buzz you in.

  Let me know what time your train gets in and I'll meet you at the station if you like.

  Can't wait to show you around!

  Yours,
  Anna *(约 100 词)*

## 13. 拓展练习 Going further

<Callout type="tip" title="自学者建议">
  - **影子跟读 (shadowing)**：用第 1 节的三个 SayIt 按钮跟读 3-5 遍。注意 *far closer to* / *with contempt* / *freedom from care* 这类短语里，重音分布和自然停顿。
  - **复述 (retell)**：合上课文，用 2 分钟把本文的逻辑线讲一遍。**四步推演**：人人都卖 → 流浪汉什么都不卖 → 他们不牺牲尊严、换来自由 → 我们嘴上蔑视、心里羡慕。
  - **造句迁移**：用今天的 5 个高价值结构造你自己的句子——
    1. *live by doing ...*（靠……为生）—— *My uncle lives by farming.*
    2. *Though ..., ...*（虽然……但是……）—— *Though the job is demanding, I find it deeply rewarding.*
    3. *grudge + doing*（舍不得做）—— *I grudge spending three hours a day commuting.*
    4. *make it possible for sb. to do*（使……有可能）—— *The new subway line made it possible for her to reach work in twenty minutes.*
    5. *be free from ...*（摆脱……）—— *On holiday, I am free from emails and meetings.*
  - **延伸思考**：本文是一篇"**价值观命题**"式议论文——作者不直接说"流浪汉的生活方式好"，而是用一个反问句把结论交给读者自己下。这种 **"陈述事实 → 对比 → 反问收束"** 的三段式是高级议论文的经典模板。另外值得品味的：全篇从头到尾没有用一个 should / must / ought to 来说教，但读者几乎一定会开始反思自己的生活方式——**好的议论文不说服你，而是让你自己说服自己**。下次写议论文时，试试把最后一段的结论改成一句反问。
</Callout>
