## The Text 课文

Insurance companies are normally willing to insure anything. Insuring public or private property is a standard practice in most countries in the world. If, however, you were holding an open air garden party or a fete it would be equally possible to insure yourself **in the event of** bad weather. **Needless to say, the bigger the risk an insurance company takes, the higher the premium you will have to pay.** It is not uncommon to hear that a shipping company has **made a claim for** the cost of salvaging a sunken ship. But the claim made by a local authority to recover the cost of salvaging a sunken pie dish **must surely be unique**.

**Admittedly** it was an unusual pie dish, for it was eighteen feet long and six feet wide. It had been purchased by a local authority so that an enormous pie could be baked for an annual fair. The pie committee decided that the best way to transport the dish would be by canal, so they insured it for the trip. Shortly after it was launched, the pie committee went to a local inn to celebrate. At the same time, a number of teenagers climbed on to the dish and held a little party of their own. Dancing **proved to be more than the dish could bear**, for during the party it capsized and sank in seven feet of water.

The pie committee telephoned a local garage owner who arrived in a recovery truck to salvage the pie dish. Shivering in their wet clothes, the teenagers looked on while three men dived repeatedly into the water to locate the dish. They **had little difficulty in** finding it, but hauling it out of the water **proved to be a serious problem**. The sides of the dish were **so smooth that** it was almost impossible to attach hawsers and chains to the rim without damaging it. **Eventually** chains were fixed to one end of the dish and a powerful winch was **put into operation**. The dish rose to the surface and was gently drawn towards the canal bank. **For one agonizing moment**, the dish was perched precariously on the bank of the canal, but it suddenly overbalanced and slid back into the water. The men were now **obliged to** try once more. This time they fixed heavy metal clamps to both sides of the dish so that they could fasten the chains. The dish now had to be lifted vertically because one edge was resting against the side of the canal. The winch was again put into operation and one of the men started up the truck. Several minutes later, the dish was successfully hauled above the surface of the water. Water streamed in torrents over its sides with such force that it **set up** a huge wave in the canal. There was danger that the wave would rebound off the other side of the bank and send the dish plunging into the water again. By working at **tremendous** speed, the men **managed to** get the dish on to dry land before the wave returned.

## 课文注释 Notes on the text

<Callout type="info" title="7 个值得停下来嚼一嚼的点">
  1. **in the event of** (l.3) — "倘若……"、"万一……"。等于 **in case of**，后接名词或动名词。课文 *insure yourself **in the event of** bad weather* = 万一碰上坏天气也能获得理赔。同型：*In the event of fire, break the glass. / In the event of his being late, we'll start without him.*

  2. **the + 比较级 ..., the + 比较级 ...** (ll.4-5) — "越……就越……"。课文 ***The bigger** the risk ..., **the higher** the premium*——保险公司承担的风险越大，你的保费就越高。前后都用 **the + 比较级** 引导，前面是从句，后面是主句。同型：*The sooner, the better. / The more you practise, the easier it becomes.*

  3. **make a claim for** (l.5) — "为……提出索赔"。**claim** 在这里是名词，作"索赔"解。课文 *a shipping company has **made a claim for** the cost of salvaging a sunken ship* = 航运公司为打捞沉船的费用提出索赔。同型：*He made a claim for compensation. / The insurance company agreed to pay her claim.*

  4. **prove (to be)** (l.12, l.15) — "证明是……"、"结果发现……"。**prove** 在这里是系动词用法，to be 可省略。课文两处：*Dancing **proved to be** more than the dish could bear*（跳舞证明是盘子承受不了的）、*hauling it out **proved** a serious problem*（结果证明把盘子拖出来是真正的难题）。同型：*The task proved (to be) impossible. / His prediction proved correct.*

  5. **so ... that ...** (l.16) — "如此……以至于……"。课文 *The sides of the dish were **so smooth that** it was almost impossible to attach hawsers*——边太滑了，根本挂不住缆绳。同型：*The box was so heavy that nobody could lift it. / He ran so fast that I couldn't catch up.*

  6. **be obliged to do** (l.19-20) — "被迫做……"、"不得不做……"。比 *have to* 更正式，带一层"别无选择"的强制感。课文 *The men were now **obliged to** try once more*——第一次失败后，工人们只得再来一次。同型：*I was obliged to cancel the trip. / We felt obliged to help.*

  7. **manage to do** (l.27) — "设法做成……"、"终于……"。强调结果（成功了），不仅仅是尝试。课文 *the men **managed to** get the dish on to dry land*——工人们终于把盘子弄上了岸。区别于 *try to do*（尝试但不一定成功）。同型：*Despite the traffic, she managed to arrive on time. / How did you manage to fix it?*
</Callout>

## 3. 关键句型 比较级 + prove + so...that 综合 Structure & comparison

<Callout type="tip" title="一分钟回顾">
  本课是一篇"叙述 + 技术打捞"文——前一半讲盘子为什么翻了（**prove to be**、**the + 比较级**），后一半讲怎么把它捞上来（**so ... that**、**manage to**、**be obliged to**）。三组结构撑起全篇的逻辑感：

  | 结构 | 课文例句 | 用途 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **the + 比较级, the + 比较级** | *The bigger the risk ..., the higher the premium ...* | 越……就越……：前因后果的"比例"关系 |
  | **prove (to be)** | *Dancing proved to be more than the dish could bear.* | 结果证明是……：强调"事后验证" |
  | **so + adj + that ...** | *The sides were so smooth that it was almost impossible to attach hawsers.* | 如此……以致……：因果链 |
  | **be obliged to do** | *The men were now obliged to try once more.* | 被迫……：别无选择 |
  | **manage to do** | *the men managed to get the dish on to dry land* | 设法做成：强调最终成功 |
  | **in the event of** | *insure yourself in the event of bad weather* | 倘若：正式的"以防万一" |

  ### ⚠️ 4 个本课最容易踩的雷区

  1. **the + 比较级 ..., the + 比较级 ...** 表示"越……就越……"，两个分句都用比较级，不是原级也不是最高级。前面是从句，后面是主句——中间通常用逗号。
  2. **prove** 作系动词时 = "最终显示/证明是"，直接加形容词或 to be + 形容词/名词。不要跟被动语态混淆——此处 proved 不是"被证明"，而是主动表系动。
  3. **so ... that** 与 **too ... to** 的区别：so ... that 只描述"如此……以致……"（不表"能不能"）；too ... to 表"太……以致不能……"（含否定含义）。
  4. **manage to do** ≠ **try to do**。manage = 成功做到了；try = 尝试但不一定成功。课文结尾 *managed to get the dish on to dry land* 强调"终于成功"。
</Callout>

## 4. 难点：易混词辨析 Special difficulties

<Callout type="info" title="教材 Special difficulties：易混词辨析">
  本课 Special difficulties 聚焦两组易混词：

  ### 1. haul / pull / drag / tug / tow 的区别

  | 词 | 含义 | 例句 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **haul** | 用力拖、拉（强调费力） | *They hauled the dish out of the water.* |
  | **pull** | 拉（最广泛、中性） | *He pulled the door open.* |
  | **drag** | 拖着走（有摩擦阻力） | *He dragged the heavy sack across the floor.* |
  | **tug** | 猛地一拉、拽 | *She tugged at his sleeve.* |
  | **tow** | 牵引（用车/船拉） | *The broken car was towed away.* |

  **课文考法**（教材 Vocabulary MCQ 第 9 题）：*They managed to ___ the dish out of the water.* → **haul**（从水中用力拖出）。

  ### 2. precarious / dangerous / hazardous / perilous 的区别

  | 词 | 含义 | 侧重 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **precarious** | 不稳固的、岌岌可危的 | 不稳定、随时可能坍塌/掉落 |
  | **dangerous** | 危险的最通用词 | 任何可能造成伤害 |
  | **hazardous** | 有风险的、碰运气的 | 冒险或概率性的危险 |
  | **perilous** | 极其危险（正式/文学） | 程度最强，常含致命危险 |

  **课文考法**：*the dish was perched **precariously** on the bank*——盘子摇摇晃晃地搁在岸边，时刻可能掉下去，这正是 precarious 的核心含义（不稳固导致的危险）。
</Callout>

## 10. 摘要写作 Summary writing (≤ 80 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  *In not more than 80 words* describe **how the pie dish came to be in the water and how it was eventually recovered**. **Do not include anything that is not in the passage.**

  按下面的提示点串成一段：

  1. 盘子为什么下水？（地方当局买的，运河运输，投了保）
  2. 盘子为什么沉了？（青少年爬上去跳舞）
  3. 打捞遇到了什么困难？（边太滑，挂不住链条）
  4. 第一次尝试为什么失败了？（失去平衡，滑回水中）
  5. 最终怎样成功的？（金属夹钳 + 垂直提升 + 抢在大浪前）
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  A local authority purchased an enormous pie dish and had it transported by canal, insuring it for the trip. During the journey, teenagers climbed on to the dish and danced, causing it to capsize and sink. A salvage team had little difficulty finding it, but its smooth sides made attaching chains almost impossible. When the dish was finally hauled up, it overbalanced and slid back into the water. The men succeeded only after fixing metal clamps to both sides, lifting the dish vertically with a winch, and working at tremendous speed to beat the returning wave. *(79 words)*
</Callout>

## 11. 作文 Composition (约 300 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  Write a composition in about **300 words** on one of the following:

  **a.** Write an account of an **unusual insurance claim**（一次不寻常的保险索赔）.

  **b.** Describe **a salvage operation** you have read about or seen（一次你读过或见过的打捞行动）.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="提示骨架（以 a 题为例）">
  - **开头 (引子)**：Insurance is a business built on the probable — but every now and then, an adjuster opens a file and can hardly believe what he reads.
  - **事件背景**：What was insured? Who owned it? Why was insurance taken out?
  - **事故经过**：What went wrong? How did the damage/loss occur?（注意时态——事故用一般过去时）
  - **索赔过程**：Who made the claim? What costs were claimed? Were there any disputes?
  - **结局与余味**：Was the claim paid? What made this case unusual enough to be remembered?（可呼应课文开头的 *must surely be unique*）
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架">
  - **时态主线**：叙述事件用**一般过去时**；背景描述用**过去完成时**（had been purchased）和**过去进行时**（was being transported）。
  - **\"保险/索赔\"词库**：*take out a policy, insure against, make a claim, premium, compensation, adjuster, dispute, settle, payout*。
  - **\"意外/打捞\"词库**：*capsize, sink, salvage, winch, haul, precarious, overbalance, recover, stream in torrents*（可直接从课文中借词）。
  - **结构亮点**：用 **Admittedly** 开篇第二段做让步（模仿课文）；用 **Needless to say** 做总结过渡；用 **prove to be** 描述\"事后验证\"的结果。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  Insurance is a business built on the probable. Traffic accidents, burst pipes, stolen jewellery — these are the everyday files on an adjuster's desk. But every now and then, someone opens a folder and can hardly believe what it contains. This is the story of one such claim.

  A local gardening club in a small English town had spent months growing a single giant pumpkin for the annual village show. The pumpkin, which eventually weighed over four hundred kilograms, was the pride of the club. Fearing that an accident during transport might ruin their year's work, the club took out an insurance policy covering the pumpkin for its journey to the showground.

  The pumpkin was loaded on to a flatbed trailer and driven carefully towards the village hall. Halfway there, the driver swerved to avoid a dog that had run into the road. The trailer tipped. The ropes that had been used to secure the pumpkin were, admittedly, too thin for the job. They snapped, and the pumpkin rolled off the trailer, down a short slope, and straight into the village duck pond.

  The club made a claim not only for the pumpkin itself — which split on impact — but also for the cost of recovering it from the pond, since the local council insisted it be removed. A recovery truck with a small crane was called. The salvage, however, proved trickier than expected: the pumpkin was so slippery and heavy that lifting it without a net proved almost impossible. Eventually a net was thrown under it, and by working carefully, the men managed to haul it out.

  The insurance company paid the claim. Admittedly, they had never insured a pumpkin before — nor had they ever paid for the salvage of one from a duck pond. The file, the adjuster later said, would remain in the office as long as anyone could remember. *(约 295 词)*
</Callout>

## 12. 书信写作 Letter writing (约 100 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  You have just been to see a friend **in another town** and, when you were there, you **borrowed a book** which you are now returning. Write a letter of about **100 words**. **Thank him for the loan** and say how much you liked the book. Supply a suitable **Introduction** and **Conclusion**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架">
  - **称呼**：Dear + 名（朋友间用名字，亲切）。
  - **开头**：感谢借书 + 寄回说明 —— *Thank you so much for lending me your copy of ... — I'm returning it with this note.*
  - **主体（喜欢哪里）**——挑 2-3 点具体说：
    1. *I couldn't put it down — I finished it in two days.*（爱不释手）
    2. *The chapter on ... was particularly fascinating.*（某章特别精彩）
    3. *It gave me a completely new perspective on ...*（打开了新视角）
  - **收尾**：再次致谢 + 回报之意 —— *Do let me know if there's anything I can lend you in return.*
  - **结束语**：*With thanks, / Yours, / [你的名字]*。
  - **时态要点**：感谢用现在时（*I'm writing to thank you ...*）；回顾读书体验用一般过去时（*I read it ... / I found ... / It was ...*）。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  Dear Emma,

  Thank you so much for lending me your copy of *Sapiens* — I'm returning it with this note. It arrived safely in the parcel this morning.

  I honestly could not put it down. I finished it in two days, which says everything about how absorbing it was. The chapter on the Agricultural Revolution gave me a completely new perspective on human history — I had never thought of farming as a "luxury trap" before. I found myself reading passages aloud to my husband, who eventually asked to borrow it next.

  Thank you again for the loan. Do let me know if there's anything on my shelves you'd like to borrow in return.

  With thanks,

  Sarah *(约 105 词)*
</Callout>

## 13. 拓展练习 Going further

<Callout type="tip" title="自学者建议">
  - **影子跟读 (shadowing)**：用第 1 节的三个 SayIt 按钮跟读 3-5 遍。注意重音和节奏——*the BIGGER the RISK, the HIGHER the PREmium* 这种平行重音要读出来；*so SMOOTH that it was ALmost imPOSSible* 中 smooth 和 impossible 是重读焦点。
  - **复述 (retell)**：合上课文，用 2 分钟把这场\"盘子打捞记\"讲一遍。**四幕结构**：① 盘子是什么 + 为什么在水上（18 英尺、地方当局、运河运输）→ ② 怎么沉的（青少年跳舞、倾覆）→ ③ 打捞波折（找到了但拉不出 → 拉出来了又滑回去）→ ④ 最终成功（夹钳 + 垂直提升 + 抢在大浪前）。
  - **造句迁移**：用今天的 5 个高价值结构造你自己的句子——
    1. *the + 比较级, the + 比较级* —— *The more I learn, the more I realize how little I know.*
    2. *prove to be* —— *The exam proved to be far easier than I had expected.*
    3. *so ... that ...* —— *The lecture was so boring that half the audience fell asleep.*
    4. *manage to do* —— *Despite the storm, the pilot managed to land the plane safely.*
    5. *be obliged to do* —— *I was obliged to borrow money from my parents when I lost my job.*
  - **延伸思考**：本课是一篇\"**叙事 + 技术说明**\"混合文——开头用一段保险科普做铺垫，中间是戏剧性的沉没 + 一波三折的打捞，结尾以\"成功\"收束。这种从\"一般（保险常识）\"到\"特殊（这个奇怪的索赔）\"再到\"具体（打捞过程）\"的漏斗结构，是说明性叙事的经典模式——下次你要讲一件\"怪事\"，不妨先铺垫\"通常怎样\"（normally / standard practice / not uncommon），再抛出\"但这回不一样\"（must surely be unique），读者立刻就被勾住了。另一个值得学习的技巧：作者把 first attempt 的**失败**写得极其细致（perched precariously → overbalanced → slid back），正是这个\"差点成功又失败\"的转折，让一个\"捞盘子\"的简单故事变成了一波三折的微型惊险片——好的叙事，往往靠\"差一点\"制造张力。
</Callout>
