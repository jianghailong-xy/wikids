  你有没有经历过这样的一天——早上还一切正常，突然一件小事就像推倒了第一块多米诺骨牌，接着所有倒霉事都在同一时刻凑到一起？本课先讲了一个日常版的"连环灾难"（做饭 + 接电话 + 看孩子 = 灾难配方），再讲了一个悉尼马路上的"升级版"（撞车 → 争吵 → 新手司机急停 → 蛋糕飞出去 → 卡车急刹车 → 啤酒瓶滚一地……）。最妙的是作者在开头用了一句 **as if** 的虚拟语气——*"就好像一件无足轻重的小事，引爆了一整条连锁反应"*——把人生里那种"说不清怎么就全乱了"的感觉写得活灵活现。**What incident began the series of traffic accidents?** 带着这个问题读课文，重点关注全篇如何用 **过去时 + 分词短语** 把一连串动作串成"多米诺链"，以及 **as if + 虚拟语气**、**What ... is that ... 主语从句** 和 **keep an eye on / reduce you to tears / on a big scale** 这些地道表达的用法。
</Callout>

## The Text 课文

We have all experienced days when everything goes wrong. A day may begin well enough, but suddenly everything seems to **get out of control**. **What invariably happens is that** a great number of things choose to go wrong at precisely the same moment. It is **as if** a single unimportant event **set up a chain of reactions**. Let us suppose that you are preparing a meal and **keeping an eye on** the baby at the same time. The telephone rings and this marks the prelude to an unforeseen series of catastrophes. While you are on the phone, the baby pulls the tablecloth off the table, smashing half your best crockery and cutting himself **in the process**. You hang up hurriedly and attend to baby, crockery, etc. Meanwhile, the meal gets burnt. As if this were not enough to **reduce you to tears**, your husband arrives, unexpectedly bringing three guests to dinner.

Things can go wrong **on a big scale**, as a number of people recently discovered in Parramatta, a suburb of Sydney. During the rush hour one evening two cars collided and both drivers began to argue. The woman immediately behind the two cars happened to be a learner. She suddenly got into a panic and stopped her car. This made the driver following her brake hard. His wife was sitting beside him holding a large cake. As she was thrown forward, the cake went right through the windscreen and landed on the road. Seeing a cake flying through the air, a lorry driver who was drawing up alongside the car, pulled up **all of a sudden**. The lorry was loaded with empty beer bottles and hundreds of them slid off the back of the vehicle and on to the road. This led to **yet another** angry argument. Meanwhile, the traffic **piled up** behind. It took the police nearly an hour to **get the traffic on the move** again. In the meantime, the lorry driver had to **sweep up** hundreds of broken bottles. Only two stray dogs benefited from all this confusion, for they greedily devoured **what was left of** the cake. It was just one of those days!

## 课文注释 Notes on the text

<Callout type="info" title="8 个值得停下来嚼一嚼的点">
  1. **get out of control** (l.2) — "失去控制"。**get + out of + 名词** 表"从……中摆脱出来"→ 反过来说就是"失控"。同型：*The fire got out of control within minutes. / The situation is getting out of hand.* 注意 **out of control** 与 **under control**（受控）互为反义。

  2. **What invariably happens is that ...** (l.3) — "一成不变的是……"。**What + 从句** 作主语（主语从句），is 后面用 **that** 引导表语从句。整个结构 = "所……的是……"。同型：*What surprised me was that he never complained. / What matters is that you tried your best.*

  3. **as if + 虚拟语气** (ll.4-5) — "就好像……"。**as if** = as though，当后面的内容**与事实相反**时，从句谓语用**虚拟语气**（一般用过去式）。课文 *as if a single unimportant event **set** up a chain* —— set 是过去式（不是 set up 的现在式，set-set-set），表达"其实不是单一事件导致的，只是看起来好像是这样"。对比：*He talks as if he knew everything.*（其实他并不全知道）vs *It looks as if it is going to rain.*（真的要下雨了，不用虚拟——这是 is going to，非虚拟）。

  4. **keeping an eye on** (l.6) — "照看、留意"。**keep an eye on + 人/物** = watch / look after。同型：*Could you keep an eye on my bag for a minute? / Keep an eye on the time — we mustn't be late.*

  5. **in the process** (l.9) — "在这个过程中、与此同时"。用于补充一个**伴随发生的（通常是不好的）结果**。同型：*He tried to fix the sink and flooded the kitchen in the process. / She deleted the spam folder and lost an important email in the process.*

  6. **reduce you to tears** (l.10) — "把你逼哭"。**reduce + sb + to + n.** = 使某人陷入（某种糟糕状态）。**reduce ... to tears** = 使……流泪；**reduce ... to silence** = 使……哑口无言；**reduce ... to despair** = 使……绝望。注意 to 是介词，后接名词。

  7. **on a big scale** (l.12) — "大规模地"。**on a + adj + scale** = 以……规模。同型：*on a small scale*（小规模地）、*on a national scale*（全国范围）、*on an unprecedented scale*（以前所未有的规模）。

  8. **all of a sudden** (l.17) — "突然"。= suddenly，口语和书面都常用。同型：*All of a sudden, the lights went out. / He turned around all of a sudden and walked away.* 注意本课还有另一个"突然"表达：*She suddenly got into a panic* (l.14)。
</Callout>

  就像许多人最近在悉尼郊区帕拉马塔所发现的那样，有时乱子会闹得很大。一天傍晚交通最拥挤时，一辆汽车撞上前面的车，两个司机争吵起来。紧跟其后的一辆车上的司机碰巧是个初学者。她一惊之下突然把车停了下来。她这一停使得跟在后面的司机也来了个急刹车。司机的妻子正坐在他身边，手里托着一大块蛋糕。她往前一冲，蛋糕从挡风玻璃飞了出去，掉在马路上。此时，一辆卡车正好从后面开到那辆汽车边上，司机看见一块蛋糕从天而降，紧急刹车。卡车上装着空啤酒瓶，成百只瓶子顺势从卡车后面滑出车外落在马路上。这又引起了一场唇枪舌剑的争吵。与此同时，后面的车辆排成了长龙。警察花了将近一个小时才使车辆又开起来。在这段时间里，卡车司机不得不清扫那几百只破瓶子。只有两只野狗从这一片混乱中得到了好处，它们贪婪地吃掉了剩下的蛋糕。这就是事事不顺心的那么一天！
</Callout>

## 3. 关键句型 虚拟语气与连锁叙述 Subjunctive mood & event chains

<Callout type="tip" title="一分钟回顾">
  本课讲两种完全不同的"一连串"——前半篇（家里）用**泛泛的"你"** (you) 写一个每个人都可能经历的日常连锁灾难；后半篇（悉尼马路）用**具体的第三人称过去叙事**写好几个人一个接一个出错。语法上本课有两组核心结构：**as if + 虚拟语气** 和 **用分词短语串事件链**。

  | 结构 | 课文例句 | 用途 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **as if + 过去式（虚拟）** | *It is as if a single unimportant event **set** up a chain of reactions.* (l.4-5) | 好像……（其实不是） |
  | **What + 从句 + is that ...** | ***What** invariably happens **is that** a great number of things choose to go wrong ...* (l.3) | 主语从句：所……的是…… |
  | **分词作伴随状语** | *You hang up hurriedly and attend to baby, ...* / ***Seeing** a cake flying through the air, a lorry driver ... pulled up.* (l.17) | 把两个动作串成一步 |
  | **keep an eye on** | *keeping an eye on the baby* (l.6) | 照看、留意 |
  | **reduce + sb + to + n.** | *reduce you to tears* (l.10) | 逼得某人进入某种状态 |
  | **on a ... scale** | *on a big scale* (l.12) | 以……规模 |

  ### ⚠️ 4 个本课最容易踩的雷区

  1. **as if + 虚拟语气 vs 陈述语气**：as if 后面说的是**与事实相反**的假设时，动词用**过去式**（虚拟）。课文 *as if a single unimportant event **set** up ...*（set 是过去式），因为作者认为"其实不是一件小事导致的"。但如果 as if 后面是**可能真实**的情况，就用正常时态：*It looks as if it **is** going to rain.*
  2. **What ... is that ...**：What 引导的主语从句后面的 that **不能省**。*What matters is **that** you tried.* 不能说 *What matters is you tried.*
  3. **分词短语的逻辑主语**：*Seeing a cake flying through the air, a lorry driver ... pulled up.*——**Seeing** 的逻辑主语是紧跟在后面的 **a lorry driver**（卡车司机看见了蛋糕）。如果主语不一致就会变成"悬垂分词"（dangling participle），是错句。
  4. **reduce ... to + n. (不是 to do)**：*reduce you **to tears***，to 是介词，后接名词，不是不定式。"reduce you to cry" 是错的。
</Callout>

## 4. 难点：词义辨析与固定短语 Special difficulties

<Callout type="info" title="教材 Key structures 精选">
  本课 Special difficulties 包含 7 个辨析/改写点，来自第 2 册对应课次的复习。我们挑最核心的 4 个：

  ### 1. experience（n.）vs experience（v.）

  | 用法 | 例句 |
  |---|---|
  | **experience** (n. 可数) = 经历 | *We have all **experienced** days when everything goes wrong.* (v. l.1) / *It was a terrible **experience**.* (n.) |
  | **experience** (n. 不可数) = 经验 | *She has a lot of **experience** in teaching.* |
  | **experienced** (adj.) = 有经验的 | *an **experienced** driver*（与课文 learner 形成对比） |

  ### 2. beside vs besides

  | 词 | 含义 | 例句 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **beside** (prep.) | 在……旁边 | *His wife was sitting **beside** him.* (l.15) |
  | **besides** (prep. / adv.) | 除……之外（还）/ 况且 | ***Besides** English, he speaks French. / I don't feel like going out; **besides**, it's raining.* |

  ### 3. discover vs invent

  | 词 | 含义 | 例句 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **discover** | 发现（原本就存在的东西） | *A number of people recently **discovered** in Parramatta ...* (l.12) / *Columbus **discovered** America.* |
  | **invent** | 发明（原本不存在的东西） | *Edison **invented** the light bulb.* |

  ### 4. draw up / draw back / draw off

  | 短语 | 含义 | 例句 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **draw up** | 追上、靠近停下 | *a lorry driver who was **drawing up** alongside the car* (l.17) |
  | **draw back** | 后退、撤回 | *He **drew back** in horror when he saw the snake.* |
  | **draw off** | 撤退、抽走 | *The troops **drew off** under cover of darkness.* |
</Callout>

## 11. 作文 Composition (≤ 250 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  Write a composition in **not more than 250 words** describing a similar **'chain of reactions'**. Expand the ideas given below into a plan and provide a suitable title. Your composition should be in **three or four paragraphs**.

  **Ideas:** Man loaded with parcels — looking for his car — saw one exactly like it — mistook it for his own — found his key with difficulty — tried to open the door — key wouldn't turn — forced the lock — broke the key — dropped the parcels — infuriated — deliberately broke the window of the car — the owner saw him — rushed towards him — called a policeman — the man arrested — tried to explain — was not believed.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="提示骨架">
  - **第 1 段 (触发)**：Man leaves the shopping centre, arms loaded with parcels. Spots his car（or so he thinks）. Fumbles for his key ...
  - **第 2 段 (升级)**：Key won't turn. Tries harder — forces it — snap! The key breaks. Drops all his parcels. Now furious. Picks up a stone and smashes the window.
  - **第 3 段 (高潮)**：The real owner, sitting in a café nearby, sees everything. Rushes out, shouting. Calls a policeman.
  - **第 4 段 (收尾 & 反讽)**：The man tries to explain — "I thought it was my car!" — but the owner and the policeman don't believe him. He is arrested. The real irony? His own car was parked just two spaces further down, a different colour altogether.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架">
  - **时态主线**：全篇用**一般过去时**叙事（与课文第二段同）—— *was looking for, saw, tried, wouldn't turn, forced, broke, smashed, rushed, called, tried to explain, was not believed*。
  - **"连锁"动词库**：*mistake A for B, fumble for, force the lock, snap (break suddenly), infuriate, deliberately, rush towards, be arrested*。
  - **"误解/反转"词库**：*exactly like, identical, mistook it for, turned out to be, the real owner, the irony is that ...*
  - **结构亮点**：用一连串**逗号分隔的短动作**模仿"失控加速"的节奏（同课文 *Seeing a cake ... the lorry ... pulled up*）。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  **The Wrong Car**

  John left the supermarket loaded with parcels and made his way across the car park. He spotted a blue car — his was blue — and, without a second glance, headed straight for it. Balancing his parcels with one arm, he fumbled in his pocket for the key.

  To his annoyance, the key would not turn. He pushed harder, then forced it with all his strength. There was a sharp crack: the key had snapped in the lock. His parcels tumbled to the ground. Furious, John picked up a stone from a nearby flower bed and, without pausing to think, smashed the driver's window.

  A man sitting outside a café across the street jumped to his feet and rushed towards him, shouting. "That's my car! What on earth do you think you're doing?" Before John could get a word out, the man had called a policeman.

  John tried to explain — the same model, the same colour, an honest mistake — but the furious owner was in no mood to listen, and the policeman was not convinced either. John was arrested on the spot. The bitterest pill of all? His own car, also blue, was parked three rows further down, with a child seat in the back that this one did not even have. *(约 230 词)*
</Callout>

## 13. 拓展练习 Going further

<Callout type="tip" title="自学者建议">
  - **影子跟读 (shadowing)**：用第 1 节的三个 SayIt 按钮跟读 3-5 遍。注意 *as if a single unimportant event **set** up* 中 set 的虚拟语气不需加 s，以及 *Seeing a cake flying through the air* 的重音落在 **SEE**ing ... **FLY**ing ... **AIR** 上——分词短语开头往往轻读，重音后移。
  - **复述 (retell)**：合上课文，用 90 秒把 Parramatta 马路事故串一遍。**链条记忆法**：两车相撞 → 争吵 → 新手惊慌停车 → 后车急刹 → 蛋糕飞出 → 卡车司机看见蛋糕紧急停车 → 啤酒瓶滑落 → 又一场争吵 → 交通瘫痪 → 警察疏通 → 司机清扫 → 野狗吃蛋糕。一个扣一个，像多米诺骨牌。
  - **造句迁移**：用今天的 5 个高价值结构造你自己的句子——
    1. *as if + 过去式（虚拟）*：*He spends money as if he were a millionaire.*（他花钱大手大脚，好像自己是百万富翁一样。）
    2. *What ... is that ...*：*What impressed me most was that she never gave up.*（最打动我的是她从没放弃。）
    3. *reduce ... to ... (n.)*：*The bad news reduced her to silence.*（坏消息让她沉默了。）
    4. *on a ... scale*：*The company plans to expand on a global scale.*（公司计划全球扩张。）
    5. *keep an eye on*：*Could you keep an eye on the soup while I answer the door?*（我应个门，帮我看一下汤好吗？）
  - **延伸思考**：本课最厉害的地方不是词汇有多难，而是**叙事节奏**——第一段用"你"（you）和一般现在时把你拉进一个你**亲身经历过的**日常小灾难；第二段切换到真实的第三人称叙事和过去时，把一个更夸张但同样可信的马路事故讲得"像多米诺骨牌一样一个接一个倒下"。下次你写英文叙事时试试：①用 **一般现在时写假设场景**（*Suppose you are ...*）；②用 **分词短语串事件**（*Seeing ..., ... pulled up.*）；③用**一个黑色幽默收尾**让读者会心一笑（*Only two stray dogs benefited ...*）。这些技巧，让一篇"讲倒霉事"的文章变成了一篇让人愿意反复读的经典。
</Callout>
