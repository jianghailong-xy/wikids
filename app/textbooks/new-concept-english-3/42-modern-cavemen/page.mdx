## The Text 课文

Cave exploration, or pot-holing, as it **has come to be known**, is a relatively new sport. Perhaps it is the **desire for solitude** or the chance of **making an unexpected discovery** that lures people down to the depths of the earth. It is impossible to give a satisfactory explanation for a pot-holer's motives. For him, caves have the same peculiar fascination which high mountains have for the climber. They **arouse instincts** which can **only be dimly understood**.

Exploring really deep caves is not a task for the Sunday afternoon rambler. Such undertakings require the precise planning and foresight of military operations. It can take as long as eight days to **rig up** rope ladders and to **establish supply bases** before a descent can be made into a very deep cave. Precautions of this sort are necessary, for it is impossible to foretell the exact nature of the difficulties which will **confront** the pot-holer. The deepest known cave in the world is the Gouffre Berger near Grenoble. It extends to a depth of 3,723 feet. This immense chasm has been formed by an underground stream which has tunnelled a course through a flaw in the rocks. The entrance to the cave is on a plateau in the Dauphiné Alps. As it is only six feet across, it is barely noticeable. The cave might never have been discovered **had not the entrance been spotted** by the distinguished French pot-holer, Berger. Since its discovery, it has become **a sort of pot-holers' Everest**. Though a number of descents have been made, much of it still remains to be explored.

A team of pot-holers recently went down the Gouffre Berger. After entering the narrow gap on the plateau, they climbed down the steep sides of the cave until they came to a narrow corridor. They had to **edge their way** along this, sometimes wading across shallow streams, or swimming across deep pools. Suddenly they came to a waterfall which dropped into an underground lake at the bottom of the cave. They plunged into the lake, and after loading their gear on an **inflatable rubber dinghy**, **let the current carry them** to the other side. To protect themselves from the icy water, they had to wear special rubber suits. At the far end of the lake, they came to huge piles of rubble which had been washed up by the water. In this part of the cave, they could hear an **insistent booming sound** which they found was caused by a small waterspout shooting down into a pool from the roof of the cave. **Squeezing through a cleft** in the rocks, the pot-holers arrived at an enormous cavern, the size of a huge concert hall. After switching on powerful arc lights, they saw great stalagmites — some of them over forty feet high — rising up like tree-trunks to meet the stalactites suspended from the roof. Round about, piles of limestone **glistened in all the colours of the rainbow**. In the eerie silence of the cavern, the only sound that could be heard was made by water which dripped continuously from the high dome above them.

## 课文注释 Notes on the text

<Callout type="info" title="7 个值得停下来嚼一嚼的点">
  1. **as it has come to be known** (l.1) — "正如人们逐渐了解的那样"。**as** 在此引导非限制性定语从句，指代前面整个概念 pot-holing。**come to do** = 逐渐……、终于……。同型：*As has come to be understood, regular exercise is essential for health.* / *She has come to realize that honesty is the best policy.*

  2. **it is ... that ...** 强调句型 (ll.2-3) — "正是……使得……"。课文 *Perhaps it is the desire ... or the chance ... **that** lures people ...* —— 强调主语（desire / chance），谓语 lures 用的第三人称单数与 that 从句保持一致。把 **it is ... that** 抽掉，句子依然完整：*... the desire ... or the chance ... lures people ...* 同型：*It is the love of adventure that draws people to extreme sports.* / *It was his determination that got him through the ordeal.*

  3. **rig up** (l.8) — "装配、搭起"（临时地、急就地搭起来）。同型：*They rigged up a shelter using branches and a tarpaulin.* / *We rigged up a makeshift antenna to catch the signal.*

  4. **虚拟倒装：had not the entrance been spotted ...** (ll.14-15) — "若不是洞口被……发现"。这是**省掉 if 的虚拟条件句倒装**：*had not the entrance been spotted* = *if the entrance had not been spotted*。否定词 not 跟在 had 之后，主语 the entrance 放在 been 之前。这是与过去事实相反的虚拟语气——事实是"洞口被发现了，所以洞穴才为人所知"。同型：*Had I known about the meeting, I would have attended.* / *Had it not been for his help, we would have failed.*

  5. **a sort of pot-holers' Everest** (l.16) — "洞穴探险者心目中的珠穆朗玛峰"。用 **Everest** 作比喻，表示"终极挑战、最高目标"。**a sort of** = 一种、类似……的东西（不是真正的 Everest，而是"相当于 Everest"的概念）。同型：*For many pianists, Rachmaninoff's Third Concerto is a sort of Everest.* / *Getting into that university has become a sort of Holy Grail for the students.*

  6. **edge one's way** (l.20) — "侧身挤过去、小心翼翼地挪动"。**edge** 作动词 = 缓慢地横向移动。这个结构的模式是 **动词 + one's way** —— 表示"以某种方式行进"（课文里还有 squeeze through / climb down / wade across）。同型：*He edged his way through the crowded room.* / *She felt her way along the dark corridor.* / *They fought their way through the jungle.*

  7. **which they found was caused ...** 插入语 (ll.25-26) — "他们发现这声音是由……引起的"。**they found** 是**插入语**（parenthetical），去掉后句子依然成立：*which was caused by a small waterspout ...* 这种插入语让句子更灵活，表达"他们发现/认为/相信"的判断意味。同型：*He made a decision which I thought was unwise.* / *She mentioned a book which she said had changed her life.*
</Callout>

## 3. 关键句型 强调句与虚拟倒装 Emphasis & subjunctive inversion

<Callout type="tip" title="一分钟回顾">
  本课是一篇叙事说明文，前半段解释"洞穴探险是什么"，后半段讲一队人"下去看到了什么"。语法上最值得抓的两个结构：

  | 结构 | 课文例句 | 用途 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **it is ... that ...** 强调句 | *Perhaps it is the desire ... or the chance ... **that** lures people ...* | 强调主语，让句子重心落在"究竟是什么"吸引人下去 |
  | **虚拟倒装（省略 if）** | *The cave might never have been discovered **had not the entrance been spotted** by ...* | 与过去事实相反的假设，had 提前省略 if |
  | **inserted clause 插入语** | *an insistent booming sound **which they found** was caused by ...* | 在主句中插入"他们发现"，表达判断意味 |
  | **edge one's way** | *They had to **edge their way** along this ...* | "动词 + one's way" = 以某种方式行进 |

  ### ⚠️ 3 个本课最容易踩的雷区

  1. **had not ... been ... 不是疑问句，是倒装**—— *had not the entrance been spotted* = *if the entrance had not been spotted*。去掉 if 后 had 提前，not 跟在 had 后，主语 the entrance 放在 been 前。千万不要当成疑问句去读。
  2. **it is ... that ... 强调句型**中，被强调的部分和 that 从句的谓语必须一致。课文强调 *the desire ... or the chance*（单数），所以 that 从句用 **lures**（第三人称单数）。
  3. **which they found was caused ...** 中的 **they found** 是插入语，不是句子的主干——去掉后结构是 *which was caused by ...*。插入语后面接谓语时，直接用动词（不加 that），即 *which they found **was** caused*。
</Callout>

## 4. 难点：插入语与定语从句 Special difficulties

<Callout type="info" title="教材 Key structures 改写题">
  教材要求：在**不改变意思**的前提下改写下面这句（参照 ll.25-26）：

  > The insistent booming sound they could hear was found to be caused by a small waterspout shooting down into a pool from the roof of the cave.

  改为以 **They could hear ... which they ...** 开头：

  > **They could hear an insistent booming sound which they found was caused by a small waterspout shooting down into a pool from the roof of the cave.**

  ### 解题钥匙

  原句的主干是 *The ... sound ... was found to be caused by ...*，即"声音被发现是由……引起的"。改写后把"他们能听到"作为主句 *They could hear ...*，然后用 **which 引导定语从句**修饰 sound，在从句中插入 **they found** 表达"他们发现"的判断。这种插入语（parenthetical clause）不改变定语从句的语法结构，只是附加说话者的主观判断。

  ### 插入语的 3 条规则

  1. 插入语通常是 **主谓结构**（如 they found / I think / she said），放在关系代词 which/that/who 之后、定语从句谓语之前。
  2. 去掉插入语后，定语从句的语法结构必须**完整且正确**：*which ~~they found~~ was caused by ...*
  3. 插入语后面**不加 that**——不能说 *which they found **that** was caused*。
</Callout>

## 11. 作文 Composition (约 300 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  Write a composition in about **300 words** on one of the following:

  **a.** Describe a man's efforts to escape from a cave by the seashore in order to avoid the incoming tide.

  **b.** The most popular sport in your country.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="提示骨架（以 a 题为例）">
  - **开头 (设定场景)**：A lone explorer enters a sea cave at low tide, drawn by its beauty — unaware of how quickly the tide will turn.
  - **险情出现**：The tide begins to rise — waves crash at the entrance, water fills the lower passages, narrowing the escape route by the minute.
  - **逃生过程**：He wades through waist-deep water → clambers up a ledge → squeezes through a narrowing cleft → swims the last stretch as waves surge in.
  - **心理描写**：Fear vs. calm — the moment he nearly gives up and the thought that pulls him through.
  - **结尾 (脱险 + 反思)**：He emerges gasping on the rocks outside — the cave that was so welcoming an hour ago is now entirely swallowed by the sea.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架">
  - **时态主线**：全篇用**一般过去时**叙事；心理活动用**过去进行时**或直接引语。
  - **\"海水上涨/逃生\"动词库**：*rise, surge, swallow, flood, creep up, narrow, squeeze, clamber, wade, scramble, gasp, haul oneself up*。
  - **\"恐惧/决心\"词库**：*panic, dread, adrenaline, calm determination, a surge of hope, the will to survive*。
  - **结构亮点**：用**时间链**推紧迫感——*at first ... then ... by the time ... already ... at last ...* 让读者跟着水位一起紧张。可学习课文用具体数字（\"仅六英尺宽\"）的手法，给洞穴加两三处精确的尺寸感。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  Tom had walked along the shore a hundred times without ever noticing the cave. It was low tide, and a narrow opening in the cliff face, barely four feet across, caught his eye. He squeezed through and found himself in a long, echoing chamber that sloped gently downward. The walls glistened with damp, and pools of seawater lay still on the floor. He went further in, fascinated.

  He did not notice the tide had turned until he heard a boom that was not the wind. A wave had crashed against the rocks outside and sent a tongue of water rushing down the passage. By the time he reached the entrance, the gap was already half submerged. Another wave surged in, driving him back.

  He waded as far as he could, but the water was rising faster than he could move. Then he spotted a ledge of rock a few feet above his head. With cold-numbed fingers he hauled himself up and lay flat, gasping. The water climbed after him. Further down the chamber, the roof dipped into a cleft — barely wide enough for a man's shoulders. He forced himself through, scraping skin against stone, and dropped into chest-deep water on the other side. The last stretch was a swim. The light from the entrance grew larger. One final wave lifted him and threw him onto the rocks outside, coughing and shaking.

  He lay there a long time, watching the sea swallow the last trace of the entrance. The cave that had been so silent and beautiful an hour ago was now a roaring tomb. He never walked past that stretch of shore again without checking the tide tables first. *(约 290 词)*
</Callout>

## 12. 书信写作 Letter writing (约 100 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  You had a party at your home recently but **unintentionally neglected to invite a close friend** of yours. Write him or her a letter of about **100 words apologizing** for this and **explaining how the mistake came about**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架">
  - **称呼**：*Dear + 名*（朋友间用名字）。
  - **开头**：直接道歉 + 说明原因 —— *I'm so sorry you weren't at the party last Saturday. I only realized you hadn't been invited when someone asked where you were.*
  - **主体（怎么出错的）**——可供选择的理由：*I sent the invitations in a rush and must have missed your name on the list.* / *I used an old group chat and your number wasn't in it.* / *I was sure I'd told you in person, but obviously I hadn't.*
  - **收尾**：再次道歉 + 补救 —— *Let me make it up to you — dinner is on me this weekend.*
  - **结束语**：*Yours, / With apologies, / [名字]*。
  - **语气要点**：诚恳但不卑微——用 **I'm so sorry / how awful I felt / please forgive me** 恰当地表歉意；避免过度自贬。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  Dear Jenny,

  I'm so sorry you weren't at the party last Saturday. I only realized you hadn't been invited when Tom asked where you were — and I felt awful.

  The mistake came about in the stupidest way. I'd made a list on my phone weeks ago, but when I sent out the invitations last Monday, I was in such a rush that I copied the names from an old group chat. Your number wasn't in it because you'd changed it recently, and in the chaos I never checked against my original list.

  Please forgive me. It was thoughtless, not deliberate. Let me make it up to you — dinner this Friday, anywhere you like, my treat.

  With apologies,

  Rachel *(约 120 词)*
</Callout>

## 13. 拓展练习 Going further

<Callout type="tip" title="自学者建议">
  - **影子跟读 (shadowing)**：用第 1 节的三个 SayIt 按钮跟读 3-5 遍。注意 *has come to be known* 的连读 (come-to-be) 和 *had not the entrance been spotted* 的倒装语序（不要读成疑问调，这是陈述句的倒装）。
  - **复述 (retell)**：合上课文，用 2 分钟把洞中探险的路线串一遍。**路线链**：高原入口（6 英尺宽）→ 陡壁 → 窄走廊（涉水 + 游泳）→ 瀑布 → 地下湖（充气艇渡湖）→ 碎石堆 → 水柱轰鸣 → 挤过岩缝 → 大洞穴（钟乳石/石笋/彩虹石灰石/滴水声）。
  - **造句迁移**：用今天的 5 个高价值结构造你自己的句子——
    1. *it is ... that ...*（强调句）—— *It is the sense of achievement that keeps me going.*
    2. *had not ... been ...*（虚拟倒装）—— *Had it not been for her encouragement, I would have given up long ago.*
    3. *which I found was ...*（插入语）—— *He recommended a restaurant which I later found was closed on Mondays.*
    4. *edge one's way*（侧身挪动）—— *I had to edge my way through the crowd to reach the exit.*
    5. *as long as + 时间*（长达……）—— *The queue was as long as two hours on opening day.*
  - **延伸思考**：本课是一篇\"双节拍\"叙事——前半段铺垫（什么是洞穴探险 + 伯格洞有多厉害），后半段行动（一队人真的下去了，一镜到底写到洞底奇观）。值得学的手法：**把抽象写具体**——\"要周密计划\"不如\"需要长达八天、像军事行动一样\"；\"洞口很小\"不如\"仅六英尺宽、几乎注意不到\"；\"洞穴很美\"不如\"石笋像树干般矗立、石灰石闪着彩虹色\"。下次你写叙事文时，试试作者\"用数字和比喻替掉形容词\"的方法：不写\"very deep\"，写成\"it extends to a depth of 3,723 feet\"；不写\"very beautiful\"，写成\"limestone glistened in all the colours of the rainbow\"。
</Callout>
