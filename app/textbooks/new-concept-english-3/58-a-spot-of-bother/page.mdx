  一位老太太购物回来，又累又饿，满脑子只想着午饭和休息。可就在电梯门打开的那一刻——她发现自家大门敞开着。接下来是一场步步惊心的「贼窝探秘」：每一扇房间门都开着，客厅一片狼藉，她先想自己冲进去，转念又觉得年纪大了还是找个人陪着比较稳妥……全文最妙的是结尾：老太太打电话给女儿求助，却轻描淡写地把这桩入室盗窃案说成 *"a little spot of bother"*——一点点小麻烦！这种英国式的 **understatement**（轻描淡写），正是本课最值得品味的语言风格。
  **What did the old lady find when she got home?** 带着问题读课文，同时留意作者如何用 **分词短语** 做伴随状语（*being careful to touch nothing / following her regular practice*）来串起一连串动作，以及最后那一记漂亮的含蓄收尾。
</Callout>

## The Text 课文

The old lady was glad to be back at the block of flats where she lived. Her shopping **had tired her** and her basket **had grown heavier with every step** of the way home. In the lift her thoughts were on lunch and a good rest; but when she got out at her own floor, both **were forgotten in her sudden discovery** that her front door was open. She was thinking that she must **reprimand** her home help the next morning for such a **monstrous** piece of negligence, when she remembered that she had gone shopping after the home help had left and she knew that she **had turned both keys in their locks**. She walked slowly into the hall and at once noticed that all the room doors were open, yet **following her regular practice** she had shut them before going out. Looking into the drawing room, she saw a scene of confusion over by her writing desk. **It was as clear as daylight** then that burglars **had forced an entry** during her absence. Her first impulse was to go round all the rooms looking for the thieves, but then she decided that at her age it might be **more prudent** to have someone with her, so she went to fetch the porter from his basement. By this time her legs **were beginning to tremble**, so she sat down and accepted a cup of very strong tea, while he telephoned the police. Then, **her composure regained**, she was ready to **set off** with the porter's assistance to search for any **intruders** who might still be **lurking** in her flat.

They went through the rooms, **being careful to touch nothing**, as they did not want to **hinder** the police in their search for **fingerprints**. The chaos was **inconceivable**. She had lived in the flat for thirty years and was a **veritable magpie** at **hoarding**; and it seemed as though everything she possessed had been **tossed out** and turned over and over. At least **sorting out** the things she should have **discarded** years ago was now being made easier for her. Then a police inspector arrived with a **constable** and she told them of her discovery of the **ransacked** flat. The inspector began to look for fingerprints, while the constable checked that the front door locks had not been forced, **thereby proving** that the burglars had either used **skeleton keys** or entered over the **balcony**. There was no trace of fingerprints, but the inspector found a dirty red bundle that contained jewellery which the old lady said was not hers. So their entry into this flat was apparently not the burglars' first job that day and they **must have been disturbed**. The inspector then asked the old lady to try to check what was missing by the next day and advised her not to stay alone in the flat for a few nights. The old lady thought he was a **fussy** creature, but since the porter agreed with him, she rang up her daughter and asked for her help in what she described as **a little spot of bother**.

## 课文注释 Notes on the text

<Callout type="info" title="8 个值得停下来嚼一嚼的点">
  1. **with every step of the way home** (l.2) — 在回家的路上每走一步。**with** 在此表「伴随、随着」——*with every step* = 每走一步（篮子就重一分）。同型：*With every passing day, the exam drew nearer.*（日子一天天过去，考试越来越近。）

  2. **her thoughts were on lunch and a good rest** (l.3) — 她脑子里想的是午餐和好好休息。**one's thoughts are on + 名词** = 心里惦记着某事。注意下文 *both were forgotten* —— both 指代 lunch 和 a good rest 两项。

  3. **reprimand sb. for (doing) sth.** (l.6) — 为某事训斥某人。同型：*The teacher reprimanded him for being late.* 注意本课中 reprimand 用于 *must reprimand ... for such a monstrous piece of negligence*——因为如此严重的疏忽而训斥。

  4. **following her regular practice** (ll.9-10) —— 按照她平时的习惯。**following** 是现在分词，引导伴随状语 = *as she followed her regular practice*。同型：*Following his doctor's advice, he started exercising every day.* 注意 practice 在此 = 惯常做法（不是「练习」）。

  5. **as clear as daylight** (l.11) — 一清二楚、明明白白（直译：像白天一样清楚）。固定比喻。同型表达：*as plain as the nose on your face*（一目了然）；*It's as clear as daylight that he's lying.*

  6. **it might be more prudent to have someone with her** (l.13) — 找个人陪着她也许更谨慎。**it is prudent (for sb.) to do sth.** =（某人）做某事是谨慎的。**might** 在此表委婉推测。重点：*to have someone with her* 中的 **with her** 是宾语补足语（让她跟自己在一起）。

  7. **being careful to touch nothing** (l.17) — 小心地什么也不碰。**being careful** 是现在分词短语作伴随状语，说明 *went through the rooms* 的方式。**to touch nothing** 是 *careful* 的补足成分（= careful not to touch anything）。同型：*She tiptoed into the room, being careful not to wake the baby.*

  8. **skeleton key** (l.24) — 万能钥匙（直译：骨架钥匙）。一种能开多种锁的钥匙。同类的「万能」表达：*master key*（万能钥匙，侧重总钥匙）、*a skeleton key that opens all doors*。注意后文 *the front door locks had not been forced*（门锁没有被撬的痕迹）——说明盗贼是「技术开锁」而非暴力破门。
</Callout>

## 3. 关键句型 分词短语作状语 Participle phrases as adverbials

<Callout type="tip" title="一分钟回顾">
  本课是叙事文，作者大量使用 **分词短语**（现在分词/过去分词）来压缩时间线、串起一连串动作，让叙事紧凑不拖沓。这在书面英语中极其常见——尤其是 **being + adj./过去分词** 和 **独立主格**（名词 + 过去分词 / 现在分词）。

  | 结构 | 课文例句 | 功能 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **现在分词短语（主动/伴随）** | *They went through the rooms, **being careful to touch nothing**.* | 伴随状语：一边搜房间，一边小心不碰任何东西 |
  | **现在分词短语（时间/方式）** | ***Following her regular practice** she had shut them before going out.* | = *As she followed her regular practice ...*（按她平时的习惯） |
  | **现在分词短语（时间）** | ***Looking into** the drawing room, she saw a scene of confusion.* | = *When she looked into the room ...* （朝里一看……就看见） |
  | **独立主格（名词 + 过去分词）** | ***Her composure regained**, she was ready to set off.* | = *When her composure was regained ...*（镇定恢复后）——注意 regained 是过去分词（被动含义：「被恢复」） |
  | **thereby + 现在分词（结果）** | *... had not been forced, **thereby proving** that ...* | 「由此证明……」——thereby = by that means（借此、由此） |

  ### ⚠️ 3 个本课最容易踩的雷区

  1. **being careful to touch nothing**：be careful 在此是 **be + 形容词**，变成现在分词 **being** careful。不要把 being 漏掉写成 *careful to touch nothing, they went through...*（这样就成了形容词悬垂，主语 they 不直接等于 careful 这个状态）。
  2. **her composure regained**：这是 **独立主格（absolute construction）**——名词 + 过去分词。regained 是过去分词表被动（「镇定被恢复」= 她恢复了镇定），不是过去式。不能写成 *Her composure was regained, she was ready*——两个独立句子不能用逗号直接拼接。
  3. **thereby proving**：thereby 是正式书面用词 = 由此、从而，后接现在分词引导结果状语。口语中更常用 *which proved*——本课 Structure MCQ 第 8 题正是考察这个替换：*thereby proving = which proved*。
</Callout>

## 4. 难点：understatement — 英式轻描淡写 Special difficulties

<Callout type="info" title="教材 Special difficulties：轻描淡写的艺术">
  本课的 Special difficulties 聚焦一个核心语言现象：**understatement**（轻描淡写/低调陈述）。老太太的家被翻了个底朝天，她却对女儿说只是 *"a little spot of bother"*——这不是撒谎，而是一种典型的英式修辞：**故意往轻里说，反而更让人感受到事情的「重」**。

  ### 英式 understatement 的几种常见手法

  | 手法 | 例句 | 实际意思 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **a bit / a little / a spot of** | *"I'm in a spot of trouble."* | 我麻烦大了。 |
  | **not + 正面词（曲言法 litotes）** | *"He's not the friendliest person."* | 他简直是个刺头。 |
  | **rather / quite / fairly** 弱化 | *"The exam was rather difficult."* | 难得要命。 |
  | **just / only / merely** | *"It's just a scratch."* | 伤口深可见骨。 |

  本课标题 *A spot of bother* 就是这个技巧的范例——把 burglary（入室盗窃）叫成「一点点小麻烦」。读到这句时不妨停下来想一想：如果老太太对女儿哭诉 *"We've been burgled! Everything is ruined!"*——文章就失去了那种英国老太太特有的镇定、克制和一点点幽默感。**understatement 的魅力正在于它的克制之中。**

  ### 教材 Key structures 改写题

  教材要求：在**不改变意思**的前提下改写下面这句（参照 ll.26-27）：

  > 'Try to check what's missing by tomorrow and don't stay alone in the flat for a few nights,' the inspector said.
  > The inspector asked the old lady ... and advised her ...

  **解题钥匙**：这是**直接引语 → 间接引语**的改写——祈使句变成不定式：
  - *Try to check ...* → **asked her to try to check** what was missing by the next day
  - *don't stay alone ...* → **advised her not to stay** alone in the flat for a few nights
  - 时间词切换：tomorrow → **the next day**
</Callout>

## 11. 作文 Composition (250–350 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  Write a composition on **one** of the following subjects; the length should be between **250 and 350 words**.

  **a.** Public festivals in your own country.

  **b.** Your adventures when you first visited a foreign country.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="提示骨架（以 b 题为例）">
  - **开头 (背景 + 情绪)**：Which country? When? Why was it your "first" time abroad? What were you expecting? Set the scene with one vivid detail — the smell of the air, a sign you couldn't read, the heat hitting you as you stepped off the plane.
  - **第 1 个冒险**：Language trouble — *ordering food by pointing at pictures; getting on the wrong bus because you couldn't read the destination; someone mistaking your attempt at the local language for a completely different question*.
  - **第 2 个冒险**：Culture shock or a practical mishap — *trying to pay with the wrong currency; not knowing you had to validate your train ticket; mistaking a public holiday for a normal weekday and finding everything closed*.
  - **第 3 个冒险**：A moment of connection — *a stranger who helped despite the language barrier; an invitation to someone's home; discovering that laughter needs no translation*.
  - **结尾 (反思/升华)**：What did the "adventures" teach you? That the best travel stories come from things going *wrong* rather than right; that being a beginner in a foreign place is humbling and exhilarating in equal measure.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架">
  - **时态主线**：全篇用**一般过去时**叙事（*I arrived / the train pulled in / a stranger pointed*），夹杂过去完成时交代背景（*I had never been abroad before*），用 would 表达当时的意愿或习惯性动作（*I would point at pictures to order food*）。
  - **冒险/困境词库**：*misadventure, mishap, blunder, mix-up, predicament, lost in translation, culture shock, a fish out of water, out of one's depth*。
  - **克服/连接词库**：*gesture, mime, improvise, makeshift sign language, a warm smile, go out of one's way to help, break the ice*。
  - **结构亮点**：用 3 个具体的冒险场景（不是泛泛而谈「很难」），每个场景用一两句话交代清楚「出了什么事 + 我是怎么应对/感受到的」。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  My first trip abroad was to Japan, and everything that could go wrong did — which is precisely why it remains my favourite journey.

  I arrived in Tokyo on a sweltering August afternoon, armed with six months of evening-class Japanese and the quiet confidence that I would manage just fine. Within an hour, I had already taken the wrong train, confidently boarded a "limited express" that shot past my intended stop without even slowing down, and ended up in a rural station where the ticket machine flatly refused to accept the suburban pass I waved at it. An elderly station attendant watched my pantomime for a full minute before walking over and, with a single gentle sentence I did not understand a word of, resolved the situation by pressing a button I had not seen.

  Things did not improve. At a restaurant that evening, I pointed to a picture on the plastic menu and received a dish so fiercely spicy that my eyes streamed. The next day I discovered — after an hour of confused wandering — that it was a national holiday and every museum I had circled on my map was closed. On day three, I left my umbrella in a café and, returning to retrieve it, found the waitress running down the street after me, holding it aloft with both hands like a relay baton.

  Yet the mishaps were also the doorways. The station attendant and I ended up sharing a can of cold green tea from the vending machine on the platform. The waitress's kindness made me feel less like a traveller and more like a guest. By the time I flew home, I realized that the best adventures are not the ones where nothing goes wrong, but the ones where every wrong turn teaches you something — about the place, and about yourself. *(约 280 词)*
</Callout>

## 12. 书信写作 Letter writing (80–100 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  Write a letter of between **80 and 100 words** in length on **one** of the following subjects. You should make the beginning and ending like those of an ordinary letter, but the address is not to be counted in the total number of words.

  **a.** You are ill and cannot meet a friend (who has no telephone) as arranged. Write explaining why you cannot meet, and invite him or her to visit you.

  **b.** An English friend has written asking you to help in finding, for a seventeen-year-old English student, a school or place for training or study in your country. Answer the letter giving any helpful information.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架（以 a 题为例）">
  - **称呼**：*Dear + 名*（朋友之间用名字）。
  - **开头（致歉 + 说明缘由）**：*I'm so sorry, but I won't be able to meet you tomorrow as we arranged. I've come down with a bad cold / the flu and the doctor has told me to stay in bed for a couple of days.*
  - **中间（安抚对方 + 补救方案）**：*I know you don't have a telephone, which is why I'm sending this note round. Could you come and visit me instead — say, on Saturday afternoon? I'll be recovered by then, and we can have a proper catch-up. I'll make us some tea and you can tell me everything I've missed.*
  - **结尾（再次致歉 + 期待）**：*Sorry again for messing up our plans. Do come on Saturday — I'll be looking forward to it.*
  - **结束语**：*Yours / With love / Take care,* + 签名。
  - **时态要点**：致歉/说明用现在完成时或一般现在时（*I have come down with / I am ill / I cannot meet*）；补救方案用将来/祈使（*Could you come ... / I'll be ...*）。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  Dear Sophie,

  I'm so sorry, but I won't be able to meet you at the cinema tomorrow as we planned. I've come down with a dreadful cold — the kind that makes you sound like a foghorn — and the doctor says I ought to stay indoors for a couple of days.

  Since you haven't got a telephone, I'm sending this note round with my brother. Here's a better idea: why don't you come over to my place on Saturday afternoon instead? I'm sure I'll be right as rain by then. I'll make us a pot of tea and you can tell me all about the film I missed.

  Sorry again for spoiling our plans. Do come on Saturday — I'll be counting on it.

  With love,

  Helen *(约 100 词)*

## 13. 拓展练习 Going further

<Callout type="tip" title="自学者建议">
  - **影子跟读 (shadowing)**：用第 1 节的三个 SayIt 按钮跟读 3–5 遍。注意 **being careful to touch nothing** 中 being 与 careful 之间几乎不换气，一气呵成；**her composure regained** 中 regained 的 -ed 与 she 之间可以稍作停顿——独立主格与主句之间有一个自然的「语法换气点」。
  - **复述 (retell)**：合上课文，用 2 分钟把老太太的经历讲一遍。**四步推进**：回家发现门开着（震惊）→ 叫看门人一起搜查（谨慎）→ 警察到场检查 + 发现不属于她的珠宝（转折）→ 给女儿打电话说是「一点点小麻烦」（含蓄收尾）。注意：复述时自觉用上 **being careful to ...** 和 **her composure regained** 这样的分词结构。
  - **造句迁移**：用今天的 5 个高价值结构造你自己的句子——
    1. *with every + 名词*（每……就……）—— *With every passing year, the city felt less like home.*
    2. *as clear as daylight*（一清二楚）—— *It was as clear as daylight that the team had given up.*
    3. *it might be more prudent to do ...*（做……也许更谨慎）—— *Given the weather forecast, it might be more prudent to postpone the picnic.*
    4. *分词短语作伴随状语（being + adj.）*—— *He left the room, being careful not to slam the door.*
    5. *独立主格（名词 + 过去分词）*—— *Her suitcase packed, she was ready to leave at dawn.*
  - **延伸思考**：本课最值得回味的语言手法是 **understatement**（轻描淡写）。一位老太太家里被翻了底朝天，警察都上门了，她给女儿打电话却说只是 *a little spot of bother*。这种修辞之所以有力，恰恰因为它**不说满**——读者自己会脑补出「实际上有多严重」。下次你写叙事文或写邮件时想一想：你是一直把话说透、说尽，还是像这位老太太一样，用一个克制的措辞让读者自己品出分量？好的写作，往往是把「不言」的部分留给读者去体会。
  - **英式 understatement 小测验**：试着把下面的英式轻描淡写「破译」成真实的严重程度——
    - *"I have a bit of a headache."* → （头痛欲裂，需要躺着。）
