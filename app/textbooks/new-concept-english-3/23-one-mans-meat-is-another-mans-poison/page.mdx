## The Text 课文

People become **quite illogical** when they try to decide what can be eaten and what cannot be eaten. If you lived in the Mediterranean, for instance, you would consider octopus a great delicacy. You would not be able to understand why some people find it repulsive. On the other hand, your stomach would **turn at the idea of** frying potatoes in animal fat — the normally accepted practice in many northern countries. The sad truth is that most of us **have been brought up to** eat certain foods and we **stick to** them all our lives.

No creature has received more praise and abuse than the common garden snail. Cooked in wine, snails are a great luxury in various parts of the world. There are countless people who, ever since their early years, have learned to **associate snails with** food. My friend, Robert, lives in a country where snails are despised. As his flat is in a large town, he has no garden of his own. For years he has been asking me to collect snails from my garden and take them to him. The idea never **appealed to** me very much, but one day, after a heavy shower, I happened to be walking in my garden when I noticed a huge number of snails taking a stroll on some of my prize plants. **Acting on a sudden impulse**, I collected several dozen, put them in a paper bag, and took them to Robert. Robert was delighted to see me and equally pleased with my little gift. I left the bag in the hall and Robert and I went into the living room where we talked for a couple of hours. I had forgotten all about the snails when Robert suddenly said that I must stay to dinner. Snails would, of course, be the main dish. I did not fancy the idea and I reluctantly followed Robert out of the room. To our dismay, we saw that there were snails everywhere: they had escaped from the paper bag and had **taken complete possession of** the hall! I have never been able to look at a snail since then.

## 课文注释 Notes on the text

<Callout type="info" title="8 个值得停下来嚼一嚼的点">
  1. **One man's meat is another man's poison**（标题）——英语谚语，直译"一个人的肉是另一个人的毒药"，即对甲有利的未必对乙也有利。课文取其"饮食文化差异"的语境，译作"各有所爱"。同型谚语：*One man's trash is another man's treasure.*（一个人的垃圾是另一个人的宝贝。）

  2. **turn at the idea of ...**（l.5）——"一想到……就反胃/恶心"。**at the idea of** 是固定短语，表"一想到……就……"。同型：*She shuddered at the idea of speaking in public.* 注意 turn 在此是不及物动词，主语是 stomach（胃翻腾），而非人。教材 Vocabulary 第 9 题考的就是 turn = feel sick 的替换。

  3. **have been brought up to do ...**（ll.6-7）——"从小被训练/教养得做……"。**bring up** 是短语动词，意为"养育、教养"，此处用现在完成时的被动语态。同型：*I was brought up to respect my elders.* 注意 bring up 还可表"提出（话题）""呕吐"等含义。

  4. **Cooked in wine, snails ...**（l.9）——"用酒烹制的话，蜗牛……"。**Cooked in wine** 是过去分词短语作状语，相当于一个表示条件的从句（= *If they are cooked in wine*）。过去分词短语的逻辑主语是主句的主语 snails（蜗牛被烹饪）。同型：*Seen from the hill, the city looks magnificent.*

  5. **happen to be doing ...**（l.14）——"碰巧正在做……"。**happen + 不定式** 表"碰巧、恰好"，后接 to be doing 强调"正在进行"。同型：*I happened to be passing by when the accident occurred.* 另可用 **It happened that ...**：*It happened that I was walking in the garden.* 教材 SD 第 3 题专门练这个结构。

  6. **Acting on a sudden impulse**（l.15）——"凭一时冲动"。**on impulse** 是固定短语，表"冲动之下"；**act on** = "按照……行动"。同型：*On a sudden impulse, he bought the expensive watch. / Acting on a sudden impulse, she quit her job.* 这里用现在分词短语作状语，修饰后面的主句谓语。

  7. **take possession of ...**（l.22）——"占有、占领"。常与 take / have / gain 搭配。课文 *had taken complete possession of the hall* 用过去完成时，表"（在我们发现之前）蜗牛已经占领了门厅"。同型：*The rebels took possession of the capital. / You may have possession of the house as soon as the contract is signed.*

  8. **I have never been able to look at a snail since then.**（l.23）——"打那以后我再也不敢正眼看蜗牛了。"用现在完成时 **have never been able to** 强调从过去某个时刻（since then）至今持续的状态。这句话用幽默的笔调收束全文——"一朝被蛇咬，十年怕井绳"的蜗牛版。
</Callout>

## 3. 关键句型 虚拟条件句与现在完成时 Subjunctive & present perfect

<Callout type="tip" title="一分钟回顾">
  本课是一篇"文化差异 + 幽默叙事"的短文，前半段用**虚拟条件句 (if ... would)** 推演"如果你生活在不同地方，饮食观就会不同"，后半段用 **现在完成时 (has been asking / have never been able)** 讲一个持续到现在的故事。教材 Key structures 的训练点集中在两组：

  | 结构 | 课文例句 | 用途 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **If + 过去式 ..., would + V** | *If you **lived** in the Mediterranean ... you **would consider** octopus a delicacy.* | 与现在/将来事实相反的虚拟条件 |
  | **If + had done ..., would have done** | *If you **had lived** ... you **would have considered** ...* | 与过去事实相反的虚拟条件（教材 KS 64b 改写题） |
  | **has/have been doing** | *For years he **has been asking** me to collect snails.* | 现在完成进行时：从过去持续到现在，强调反复/持续 |
  | **has/have done** | *He **has asked** me many times.* | 现在完成时：强调结果/次数（教材 KS 2 区分题） |
  | **most / the most** | *most of us / the most delicious* | most + 名词 = 大多数（限定词）；the most + 形容词 = 最……（最高级） |
  | **since / for / ago** | *ever since their early years / for a couple of hours* | since + 起点 / for + 时段 / ago 用于过去时 |

  ### ⚠️ 4 个本课最容易踩的雷区

  1. **if ... would 是虚拟，不是"过去"**——*If you lived ... you would ...* 表"假如你住……你就会……"，说的是**与现在事实相反**的假设（事实是：你并不住在地中海）。注意从句用过去式（lived），主句用 would + 原形。
  2. **has been asking ≠ has asked**——现在完成进行时（has been asking）强调"多年来一直在问（反复、持续）"；现在完成时（has asked）只强调"已经问过"这个结果。前者更生动，更适合描述一个持续到现在的习惯性动作。
  3. **most 还是 the most？**——*most of us*（我们中的大多数）中的 most 是**限定词**，不加 the；*the most delicious food*（最美味的食物）中的 most 是**最高级**，必须加 the。教材 KS 5a 专门考这个区别。
  4. **happen to do 中 happen 无"发生"之意**——*I happened to be walking* = 我碰巧在散步。happen 在此是"碰巧"，非"发生"。*It happened that I was walking* 是等效改写（教材 SD 3a）。
</Callout>

## 4. 难点：quite/quiet · practice/practise · as 的多义 · happen to do Special difficulties

<Callout type="info" title="四个易混考点，逐项攻破">
  ### 1. quite vs quiet（SD 3b）

  - **quite** /kwaɪt/ adv. = 相当、十分：*The film is **quite** interesting.*
  - **quiet** /ˈkwaɪət/ adj. = 安静的：*The classroom is very **quiet**.*
  
  二者拼写差一个字母，发音完全不同。记法：**quite** 里有"quit"（相当好→可以 quit 了）；**quiet** 里有"et"（安静的-ette 小后缀）。

  ### 2. practice vs practise（SD 69a）

  - **practice** n.（名词）= 练习、惯例、做法：*the normally accepted **practice***（课文 l.6）
  - **practise** v.（动词）= 练习、实践：*You need to **practise** the piano every day.*
  
  英式英语严格区分（同 advice/advise）；美式英语均拼作 practice。课文 l.6 取名词义，所以用 **practice**。

  ### 3. as 的多义（SD 17a）

  课文 *As his flat is in a large town*（l.12）中的 **as** = **because**（因为）。as 还有以下常见含义：
  
  - **当……时**：*As I was walking home, I met an old friend.*
  - **作为**：*He works as a teacher.*
  - **像……一样**（as ... as）：*He is as tall as his father.*
  - **尽管**（倒装）：*Young as he is, he knows a lot.*

  ### 4. happen to do / it happened that ...（SD 3a）

  - **happen + 不定式**：*I **happened to be** walking in my garden.*（碰巧在散步）
  - **It happened that ...**：***It happened that** I was walking in my garden.*（碰巧我在散步）
  - **as it happens / as it happened**：*As it happened, I was in the garden.*

  三种表达可互换，注意 happen 在此不含"发生"义。
</Callout>

## 11. 作文 Composition (≤ 250 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  Write a **continuation of the passage** in not more than **250 words**. Expand the ideas given below into a plan and provide a suitable title. Your composition should be in **three or four paragraphs**.

  **Ideas:** Snails — walls, ceiling — coat pockets — effort to collect them — ladders, etc. — marks everywhere — Robert amused — cooked the snails — a meal for one.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="提示骨架">
  - **第 1 段 (找回蜗牛)**：描述蜗牛散布在门厅各处——墙上、天花板上、外套口袋里——作者和 Robert 用梯子、椅子等工具，两个人花了好一阵才把它们一个个抓回来。场面又狼狈又滑稽。
  - **第 2 段 (善后)**：蜗牛留下的黏液痕迹到处都是——墙上、门上、甚至 Robert 的外套上。Robert 倒是一点不生气（amused），反而觉得整件事很好笑。
  - **第 3 段 (晚餐)**：Robert 把找回的蜗牛洗刷干净、用酒烹制——做成了一顿单人蜗牛大餐。作者坚决不吃，只在一旁看着 Robert 津津有味地享用。Robert 说这是他吃过最鲜美的一顿蜗牛。
  - **结尾**：作者总结——从此以后他再也不从花园里捡蜗牛了。每当雨后看到蜗牛，他都绕道而行。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架">
  - **时态主线**：全篇用**一般过去时**叙述（collected, climbed, wiped, cooked），描写\"找回蜗牛\"的过程。最后一段可用过去完成时或现在完成时收束。
  - **\"抓回蜗牛\"动作库**：*pick up, scoop into, reach for, climb a ladder, stretch, balance, grab, drop, retrieve*。
  - **位置/方向词库**（描述蜗牛到处爬）：*on the walls, across the ceiling, inside coat pockets, under the table, along the skirting board, behind the mirror, on the curtain rail*。
  - **结构亮点**：用 **There were / I noticed / We found ...** 等存在句和感官动词铺陈\"蜗牛无处不在\"的画面，呼应课文结尾的戏剧性；结尾用 **since that evening** 与课文 *since then* 呼应。
</Callout>

  **A Meal for One**

  When the initial shock had passed, we set about the grim task of rounding up the snails. There must have been several dozen of them. They were on the walls, climbing towards the ceiling; they were under the hall table and along every inch of the skirting board. Two had made their way inside the pockets of Robert's overcoat. One, incredibly, was clinging to the lampshade. We fetched a stepladder from the kitchen, and I balanced on it while Robert held a cardboard box underneath. For the next half hour we worked our way around the hall, picking them off one by one. By the end, my back ached and my fingers were sticky with snail trails.

  When the last snail had been dropped into the box, we surveyed the damage. Silvery tracks criss-crossed every surface — the walls, the mirror, the coat. Robert, however, seemed more amused than annoyed. He laughed at the sight of me standing on tiptoe on the stepladder and said he had never seen anyone look so uncomfortable.

  'Right,' he said, 'let's get these into the pot.' Within an hour, the snails had been scrubbed, cooked in wine and garlic, and served in a single bowl. The smell was, I admit, rather wonderful — but not wonderful enough to change my mind. Robert dined alone, savouring every mouthful, while I made do with bread and cheese. 'Best snails I've ever had,' he declared, 'and all thanks to your garden.' I smiled weakly and made a silent promise: never again. *(248 words)*

## 13. 拓展练习 Going further

<Callout type="tip" title="自学者建议">
  - **影子跟读 (shadowing)**：用第 1 节的三个 SayIt 按钮跟读 3-5 遍。特别注意 *If you lived ... you would consider ...* 的虚拟语气语调——if 从句通常用升调或平调，主句用降调收束。*Acting on a sudden impulse ...* 中的三个并列谓语 collected → put → took 节奏要紧凑，一气呵成。
  - **复述 (retell)**：合上课文，用 2 分钟讲这个故事。**四步讲法**：为什么人们饮食观不同（虚拟条件，前半段）→ Robert 为什么需要蜗牛（没有花园 + 多年索要）→ 作者为什么终于捡了蜗牛（雨后、花木、冲动）→ 蜗牛大逃亡 + 从此不敢看蜗牛（高潮 + 收尾）。
  - **造句迁移**：用今天的 5 个高价值结构造你自己的句子——
    1. *If + 过去式 ..., would + V*（虚拟条件）——*If I had a million dollars, I would travel around the world.*
    2. *happen to do ...*（碰巧做）——*I happened to meet an old friend at the airport.*
    3. *Acting on ... impulse*（冲动之下）——*Acting on a sudden impulse, she bought a ticket to Paris.*
    4. *bring up*（养育、教养）——*I was brought up to believe that hard work always pays off.*
    5. *To one's dismay / surprise / relief*（使某人……的是）——*To my great relief, the train was ten minutes late and I caught it just in time.*
  - **延伸思考**：本文的幽默来自一个经典叙事模式——"好心办坏事"（a good deed gone wrong）。作者出于好意（帮朋友捡蜗牛 + 顺便保护花木），结果却招来一场灾难（蜗牛大逃亡 + 被迫面对自己不想吃的晚餐）。这种"irony of circumstance"（情境反讽）是英文幽默写作的利器。下次你想写一个 funny story，试试这个公式：**善意动机 → 微小疏忽 → 灾难性后果 → 一句轻描淡写的收尾**（*I have never been able to look at a snail since then.*）。另外，标题 *One man's meat is another man's poison* 不仅是谚语，还为全文定下了"文化相对主义"的基调——饮食无对错，习惯使然而已。这种"从具体故事上升到普遍哲理"的写法，正是 NCE3 区别于 NCE2 的关键特征之一。
</Callout>
