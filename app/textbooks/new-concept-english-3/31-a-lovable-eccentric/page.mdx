## The Text 课文

We often read newspaper articles about people who have done extraordinary things, but we rarely **set out to do** something as strange as Dickie Colson. Though he was very wealthy, Dickie was known for his lovable eccentricities. His name had become a household word in the town where he lived, and his eccentric behaviour was **legendary**.

A true eccentric is a person who **disregards social conventions** deliberately. He is not interested in drawing attention to himself; he sincerely does not care what other people think of him. As a result, he is invariably true to himself. He follows his own **routine** without being **conscious** of how strange he appears to others.

Though Dickie owned a large car, he **preferred going on foot**. He never took an umbrella with him, whatever the weather. One day, he was **caught in** a heavy shower and arrived at one of the most exclusive shops in town completely **bedraggled**. He wanted to buy his wife a birthday present — a watch priced at £300. A young shop assistant, noticing his **bedraggled** appearance, did not attend to him immediately. When at last she came over, she was politely **apologetic**, but it was clear she thought little of this dripping customer.

Dickie said nothing. He produced a huge cloth bag from under his coat and **dumped** it on the counter. 'Please count this,' he said. The bag was full of pennies — exactly 30,000 of them. **Recognizing who the customer was**, the assistant tried to spare him the trouble, but Dickie **insisted on** her **counting** every single penny.

Dickie's most **elaborate** eccentricity, however, was a trick he played on some art critics. He spent four years secretly producing what he thought were the ugliest, most meaningless 'paintings' imaginable. He then **staged** an exhibition and invited a large number of distinguished critics. The critics were **intensely** enthusiastic and **shrewdly** praised every work. Dickie then showed that he himself had painted them all. In this way, he proved that critics do not always know what they are talking about.

## 课文注释 Notes on the text

<Callout type="info" title="9 个值得停下来嚼一嚼的点">

**1. set out to do sth** — 着手做某事，有意图地开始

> He *set out to* create the ugliest paintings imaginable.（他着手创作他所能想象到的最丑陋的画作。）

**2. draw attention to oneself** — 引起别人的注意

> She didn't want to *draw attention to* herself by wearing bright colours.（她不想穿鲜艳的衣服来引起别人的注意。）

**3. without being conscious of...** — 没有意识到……（介词短语 + 动名词）

> He walked past without *being conscious of* the crowd staring at him.（他走过去，没有意识到人群在盯着他。）

**4. win sb. sth / win sth for sb** — 为某人赢得某物

> His eccentricities *won* him a kind of celebrity.（他的古怪行为为他赢得了某种名气。）

**5. add...to** — 增添，加剧

> Wealth doesn't always *add* happiness *to* one's life.（财富并不总能给人的生活增添幸福。）

**6. this side of his life** — 他生活的这一面（指古怪的一面）

> Few people knew *this side of his life*.（很少有人了解他生活的这一面。）

**7. be caught in** — 突然遭遇（恶劣天气）

> We were *caught in* a thunderstorm on our way home.（我们回家途中遭遇了一场雷雨。）

**8. Recognizing who the customer was** — 这是分词短语作状语，表示“认出了这位顾客是谁（之后）"

> *Recognizing* the danger, he turned and ran.（认识到危险后，他转身跑开了。）

**9. insist on sb's doing / insist on doing** — 坚持要求（某人）做某事

> He *insisted on* the assistant's *counting* every single penny.（他坚持要求那位助理数清每一枚铜板。）

</Callout>

## 参考译文 Chinese reference

<Callout type="tip" title="先读原文，遇到读不懂的句子再回来对照">

我们常常在报纸上读到关于做了非凡之事的人的文章，但我们很少见到像 Dickie Colson 那样立意去做如此奇怪之事的人。尽管他非常富有，Dickie 却以其可爱的古怪行为而闻名。他的名字在他所居住的小镇中家喻户晓，他那古怪的行为也成了传奇。

一个真正的怪人是一个故意无视社会习俗的人。他对引起别人的注意不感兴趣；他真心不在乎别人对他的看法。结果，他总是忠于自我。他遵循自己的日常习惯，却没有意识到自己在别人眼中显得多么奇怪。

尽管 Dickie 拥有一辆大轿车，他却偏爱步行。不管天气如何，他从不带伞。有一天，他被一场大雨淋湿，浑身狼狈地走进镇上一家最高档的商店。他想给妻子买一件生日礼物——一块标价 300 英镑的手表。一位年轻的女售货员注意到他落汤鸡般的样子，并没有立刻招待他。当她终于走过来时，她礼貌地道了歉，但很明显，她对这位浑身湿透的顾客不以为然。

Dickie 一言不发。他从大衣下面掏出一个巨大的布袋，砰的一声扔在柜台上。“请数一数这个，”他说。袋子里全是铜板——整整 30,000 枚。认出了这位顾客是谁，那位女售货员试图替他省去这番麻烦，但 Dickie 坚持要她一枚一枚地数清楚。

然而，Dickie 最精心策划的一次古怪行为，是他对一些艺术评论家开的一个玩笑。他花了四年时间秘密创作了一批他认为是最丑陋、最毫无意义的“画作”。然后，他举办了一场画展，并邀请了大批著名评论家。评论家们热情洋溢，对每一件作品大加赞扬。Dickie 随后揭示，这些画全是他自己画的。通过这种方式，他证明了评论家们并不总是言之有物。

</Callout>

## 3. 关键句型：insist on doing & 间接疑问句 Grammar focus

<Callout type="tip" title="一分钟回顾">

**① insist on (sb's) doing** — 坚持（要求某人）做某事

| 结构 | 例句 |
|------|------|
| insist on doing | He insisted on paying the full amount. |
| insist on sb's doing | He insisted on the assistant's counting every penny. |
| insist that sb (should) do | He insisted that the assistant (should) count every penny. |

⚠️ 易错：`insist on` 后接**动名词**，不接不定式。`insisted to count` ✗

---

**② 间接疑问句（嵌入式疑问）**

直接疑问：*Who is the customer?*
嵌入后：*Recognizing **who the customer was**, the assistant…*

间接疑问语序：**疑问词 + 主语 + 谓语**（陈述句语序）

⚠️ 易错：`Recognizing who was the customer` ✗ → 不用倒装

</Callout>

## 4. 难点：分词短语作状语 Participial phrases

<Callout type="info" title="现在分词短语 vs. 过去分词短语">

本课出现了一个经典的分词短语作状语结构：

> **Recognizing who the customer was**, the assistant immediately became very apologetic.

分词短语的逻辑主语必须与主句主语一致。这里 *recognizing* 的主语是 *the assistant*，主句主语也是 *the assistant*，完全正确。

**对比：**

| 类型 | 例句 |
|------|------|
| 现在分词（主动） | **Walking** into the shop, he noticed the display. |
| 过去分词（被动） | **Caught** in the rain, he arrived completely wet. |
| 完成式分词 | **Having spent** four years on the paintings, he held an exhibition. |

⚠️ 悬垂分词（主语不一致）：`Recognizing the customer, the watch was shown.` ✗（watch 不能 recognize）

</Callout>

## 11. 作文 Composition

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  Write about **250 words** describing an eccentric person you have known or read about. Your composition should be in four paragraphs:

  1. Introduce the person — who they are and why they are considered eccentric.
  2. Describe one or two of their most memorable habits or actions.
  3. Describe how other people react to them.
  4. Give your own opinion — do you find them lovable or troublesome?
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="提示骨架">
- 第一段：身份 + 一句话点出“怪在哪里"
- 第二段：具体举两个行为例子，用 **would always / insisted on / preferred...to** 等结构
- 第三段：他人反应——先是困惑/嘲笑，后来变成佩服/喜爱
- 第四段：你的看法——用 **In my opinion / What I admire most is...**
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架">
**有用词汇：** eccentric, disregard conventions, set out to, without being conscious of, insist on doing, lovable, extraordinary, unconventional, add colour to

**句型亮点：**
- Though he/she owned…, he/she preferred doing…
- Without being conscious of how strange…, he/she would…
- Recognizing that…, people soon came to admire…
- What struck me most was that…
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  My neighbour, Mr Huang, is perhaps the most lovable eccentric I have ever met. Though he is a retired professor with considerable means, he insists on cycling everywhere, whatever the weather, and has not taken a bus in thirty years.

  His most memorable habit is his refusal to use a mobile phone. Instead, he writes letters by hand — sometimes three pages long — to friends who live just around the corner. He also insists on paying for everything in exact change, which he counts out slowly and carefully, oblivious to any queue forming behind him.

  Most people are puzzled by him at first. Shopkeepers sigh when they see him approaching with his coin purse. Yet, without being conscious of any inconvenience he causes, Mr Huang smiles warmly at everyone and enquires after their families. Gradually, people have come to adore him. His eccentricities, once seen as oddities, are now viewed as part of his charm.

  In my opinion, eccentrics like Mr Huang add enormous colour to everyday life. They remind us that social conventions, though useful, need not be followed blindly. What I admire most is that he is entirely, effortlessly himself — something very few of us manage to be.
</Callout>

## 12. 书信写作 Letter writing

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  A friend of yours died recently. Write a letter of about **100 words** to his wife. Express your sympathy and offer her your help.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架">
**书信格式：** 日期 → 称谓（Dear Mrs …）→ 正文（3–4 句）→ 结束语（Yours sincerely）

**结构建议：**
1. 表达得知噩耗后的震惊与悲痛
2. 回忆与逝者的友情，表达对他的敬重
3. 向妻子表达真诚慰问，说明她并不孤单
4. 具体提出帮助——不要只说“anything I can do”，要说得具体

**有用表达：**
- I was deeply shocked and saddened to hear of…
- He was a wonderful friend who will be greatly missed.
- Please do not hesitate to call on me if…
- I would be more than happy to help with…
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  15 June 2026

  Dear Mrs Colson,

  I was deeply shocked and saddened to hear of Richard's sudden passing. He was one of the warmest and most remarkable people I have ever known, and his friendship meant a great deal to me over the years.

  I can only imagine how difficult these days must be for you. Please know that you are not alone. I would be more than happy to help in any practical way — whether with arrangements, correspondence, or simply being there to talk.

  Please do not hesitate to call on me at any time.

  Yours sincerely,
  David
</Callout>
