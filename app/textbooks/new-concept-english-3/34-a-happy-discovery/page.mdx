## The Text 课文

Antique shops **exert a peculiar fascination on** a great many people. The more expensive kind of antique shop where rare objects are beautifully displayed in glass cases **to keep them free from dust** is usually a forbidding place. But no one has to **muster up courage** to enter a less pretentious antique shop. There is always hope that in its **labyrinth of musty, dark, disordered rooms** a real rarity will be found amongst **the piles of assorted junk** that litter the floors.

No one discovers a rarity by chance. A truly dedicated **bargain hunter** must have patience, and **above all**, the ability to recognize the worth of something when he sees it. To do this, he must be **at least as knowledgeable as** the dealer. Like a scientist **bent on making a discovery**, he must **cherish the hope** that one day he will be **amply rewarded**.

My old friend, Frank Halliday, is just such a person. He has often described to me how he **picked up** a masterpiece for a mere £50. One Saturday morning, Frank visited an antique shop in my neighbourhood. As he had never been there before, he found a great deal to interest him. The morning passed rapidly and Frank was **about to leave** when he noticed a large packing case lying on the floor. The dealer told him that it had just come in, but that he **could not be bothered** to open it. Frank begged him to do so and the dealer reluctantly **prised it open**. The contents were disappointing. **Apart from** an interesting-looking carved dagger, the box was full of crockery, much of it broken. Frank gently lifted the crockery out of the box and suddenly noticed a miniature painting at the bottom of the packing case. As its **composition and line** reminded him of an Italian painting he knew well, he decided to buy it. **Glancing at it briefly**, the dealer told him that it was worth £50. Frank could hardly **conceal his excitement**, for he knew that he had made a real discovery. The tiny painting proved to be an unknown masterpiece by Correggio and was worth **hundreds of thousands of pounds**.

## 课文注释 Notes on the text

<Callout type="info" title="7 个值得停下来嚼一嚼的点">
  1. **exert a peculiar fascination on ...** (l.1) — "对……施加/产生一种特殊的魅力"。**exert ... on ...** = 把某种影响施加在……之上。同型：*The moon exerts a gravitational pull on the earth. / Teachers exert a strong influence on young minds.* 这里用 **exert** 而不是 *have* 或 *hold*，强调古玩店是"主动散发"魅力——拟人化的笔法。

  2. **muster up courage** (l.4) — "鼓起勇气"。**muster** 原义为"集合、召集"（军队），引申为"鼓起（勇气、力量）"。同型：*I mustered up enough courage to ask for a raise. / He mustered all his strength for the final sprint.* 文中用的是否定 *no one has to muster up courage*——"谁都不必壮着胆子"，反衬普通古玩店的平易近人。

  3. **above all** (l.8) — "最重要的、首先"。用于列举中强调最核心的一项。文中：a bargain hunter 需要 patience **和** the ability to recognize worth，其中 **above all** 将后者拔高为第一要素。同型：*Be polite, be honest, and above all, be yourself.* 注意它的位置——可放在句中或句末，但不能放在句首当"第一点"用（那是 *first of all* 的工作）。

  4. **bent on (doing) sth.** (l.10) — "一心想要、决心要做"。**bent** 是 **bend** 的过去分词，但在这里是**形容词**（= determined）。同型：*He is bent on becoming a musician. / She was bent on getting her money back.* 注意：bent on 后面接**名词或动名词**，不是不定式。

  5. **pick up** (l.12) — 此处意思是"（偶然、便宜地）买到、捡到"。这是一个极为多义的短语动词，本文取"无意中获得"义。同型：*I picked up a nice jacket at the market for ten pounds. / Where did you pick up that accent?* 注意与 *pick out*（挑选）和 *pick on*（欺负）区分。

  6. **apart from ...** (l.17) — "除……以外"。既可表"除了……还有"（= besides），也可表"除了……都"（= except for），需要靠上下文判断。本文：*Apart from an interesting-looking carved dagger, the box was full of crockery*——"除了一把好看的匕首，箱子里全是陶器"（= except for，匕首是例外）。同型：*Apart from a few spelling mistakes, the essay is excellent. / Apart from English, she also speaks French.*（这两句的 apart from 含义就不同——前句 = except for，后句 = besides。）

  7. **conceal one's excitement** (l.21) — "掩饰兴奋"。**conceal** = hide（隐藏、掩盖），比 hide 更书面。*could hardly conceal his excitement* = "几乎无法抑制自己的兴奋"——**hardly** + 肯定动词 = 近乎否定（"几乎不"），比 *could not conceal* 更有"强忍着、差点就藏不住"的画面感。同型：*I could hardly believe my eyes. / She could hardly keep a straight face.*
</Callout>

## 3. 关键句型 must be 的双重身份 & 时间状语从句变换

<Callout type="tip" title="一分钟回顾">
  本课的语法练习集中在两组结构：**must be 的两种用法**（表义务 vs 表推测）和**时间状语从句的连词变换**（when → the moment / until / before）。

  ### must be：同一种形式，两种截然不同的含义

  | 用法 | 课文例句 | 含义 | 如何区分 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **must = 必须（义务/必要）** | *He **must be** at least as knowledgeable as the dealer.* (ll.9-10) | "他必须至少和行家一样懂行"——这是成为一名 bargain hunter 的**必要条件**。 | 主语是**人** + must + 动作——表"必须做……" |
  | **must = 想必/一定（推测）** | *You **must be** very tired after driving so many miles.* | "你开了这么远，一定很累吧"——这是对现状的**逻辑推断**。 | 主语 + must + 状态/形容词——表"想必是……" |

  **关键点**：*He must be knowledgeable*（l.9-10）是说"他必须具备这种知识"（必要条件），不是"他一定很懂行"（推测）。判断依据是上文 *To do this, he must be ...*——这里的 *To do this* 指明了**目的**，所以 must 是"义务/必要"。

  ### 时间状语从句：从 when 到 the moment / until / before

  课文 *when he sees it* (l.9) 是一个 **when** 引导的时间状语从句。教材 Key structures 要求用以下连词改写同类句子：

  | 连词 | 含义 | 例句 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **the moment** | 一……就……（= as soon as） | *The moment he arrives, I'll tell him the news.* |
  | **until** | 直到……（为止） | *I'll wait here until he comes back.* |
  | **before** | 在……之前 | *Call me before you leave the office.* |

  ### ⚠️ 3 个本课最容易踩的雷区

  1. **must 的歧义**：看到 *He must be ...* 不要不假思索就翻译成"他一定是……"。先看上下文——如果前面有目的状语（*To do this*、*In order to ...*），must 几乎一定是"必须"。
  2. **时间从句用现在时表将来**：在 *when / until / before / the moment* 引导的时间状语从句里，即使主句是将来时，从句也要用**一般现在时**——*The moment he **arrives** (NOT will arrive), I'll tell him.*
  3. **the moment** 引导从句**不加 when**：不能说 *the moment when he arrives*——**the moment** 本身就是一个连词，直接加从句。
</Callout>

## 4. 难点：notice vs. remark & 冠词 a/an/the Special difficulties

<Callout type="info" title="notice vs. remark — 两个“注意”有何不同？">
  教材 Special difficulties 第 4 题要求区分 **notice** 和 **remark**（参见第 2 册第 41 课难点）。

  | 词 | 含义 | 用法 | 课文例句 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **notice** | 注意到、看到、察觉到（= become aware of, see） | 强调**感官上的察觉**——眼睛看到、耳朵听到，不一定要说出来 | *he **noticed** a large packing case* (l.15) —— "他看见地板上有一个大货箱" |
  | **remark** | 评论、说起（= say, comment） | 强调**用嘴说出来**——发表看法、做出评论 | *He **remarked** that the painting was well preserved.* —— "他评论说这幅画保存得很好" |

  **关键区别**：notice = 用眼睛/耳朵察觉到（不一定出声）；remark = 用嘴说出来（一定出声）。

  同型对比：
  - *I **noticed** a strange smell in the room.*（闻到 → 感官察觉）
  - *She **remarked** that the room smelled strange.*（说出来 → 口头评论）

  ### 教材冠词填空

  教材 Key structures 第 3 题要求为 Frank 逛古玩店那段文字填入适当的冠词 a/an/the 或留空。这段文字正是课文 ll.13-18——一道经典的**冠词综合练习**，考察三条规则：

  1. **第一次提到的单数可数名词**用 a/an（*an antique shop, a great deal, a large packing case*）
  2. **再次提到、或语境已限定的名词**用 the（*the morning, the dealer, the box*）
  3. **专有名词（人名）前不加冠词**（*Frank* 前永远不填）
</Callout>

## 11. 作文 Composition (≤ 250 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  Write a composition in **not more than 250 words** on the following topic. Do not write more than **four paragraphs**.

  **Title: An antique shop.**

  按以下提示组织四段：
  - **第一段 Introduction**：外观——橱窗陈列——吸引什么样的人。
  - **第二段 Development**：内部——古董商——他的外表与性格——店铺本身——物品如何陈列——白天店内的典型场景——对顾客的描写。
  - **第三段 Conclusion**：在古玩店里寻宝的乐趣。
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="提示骨架">
  - **第一段 (外景)**：*The shop sits on a quiet side street, its window a jumble of silver spoons, oil paintings, and dusty clocks that have not ticked for a century. The kind of people it attracts are seldom in a hurry — retired collectors, curious tourists, and the occasional bargain hunter with a sharp eye.*
  - **第二段 (内部 & 人物)**：*Inside, the air smells of old paper and beeswax. The dealer — a thin, grey-haired man in his sixties — sits behind a cluttered desk, peering over half-moon spectacles. He rarely speaks unless spoken to, but when a customer picks up an object, his eyes follow every movement. The shop is a labyrinth: stacks of leather-bound books lean against brass candlesticks; a Victorian doll stares from a corner; chipped teacups share shelves with carved ivory. On a typical afternoon, two or three customers drift in, some asking the price of everything, others touching nothing, simply breathing in the past.*
  - **第三段 (寻宝之乐)**：*The pleasure of searching for unusual things lies in never knowing what the next box or shelf might hold. Every visit is a small adventure. You might leave empty-handed nine times out of ten — but the tenth time, your fingers close around something that has waited decades just for you.*
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架">
  - **时态主线**：全篇用**一般现在时**——描写一个\"典型的\"古玩店，不是某一次具体的访问。
  - **感官描写词库**：*smell of, dust, cobweb, gleam, creak, clutter, stack, lean, peer, drift, breathe*。
  - **空间描写词库**：*labyrinth, corner, shelf, display case, pile, on the floor, at the back, behind the counter*。
  - **结构亮点**：用 **contrast**（对比）——把\"高档店（forbidding）\"和\"小店（welcoming）\"做对比；用课文学的 **apart from** / **above all** 等短语融入作文。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  **An antique shop**

  The antique shop sits at the end of a narrow cobbled lane, its bowed window crammed with objects that belong to another century — brass carriage clocks, silver teaspoons, oil paintings darkened by age, and a porcelain doll whose painted eyes seem to follow you as you pass. It attracts people who are not in a hurry: retired collectors, curious tourists, and the dedicated bargain hunter who hopes to find a rarity among the clutter.

  Inside, the air is thick with the scent of old paper, wood polish, and dust. The dealer, a stout man in his sixties with ink-stained fingers, sits behind a scratched mahogany counter, reading. He looks up briefly when the bell above the door rings, then returns to his book — he is not one to pester his customers. The shop itself is a labyrinth of narrow aisles. Books are stacked in tottering piles on the floor; a stuffed owl glares from a high shelf; teacups, candle-sticks and old photographs jostle for space in glass cases. A grandfather clock ticks solemnly at the back. On a typical afternoon, two or three customers drift through, some picking up objects and putting them down, others simply wandering, as if through a museum of forgotten things.

  The pleasure of such a place lies in the search itself. You never know what you might find — it could be a worthless trinket, or, as Frank Halliday discovered, it could be an unknown masterpiece hiding at the bottom of a packing case. The hunt is its own reward. Each visit is a small act of hope. *(~245 words)*
</Callout>

## 13. 拓展练习 Going further

<Callout type="tip" title="自学者建议">
  - **影子跟读 (shadowing)**：用第 1 节的三个 SayIt 按钮跟读 3-5 遍。注意 *exert a peculiar fascination* / *could hardly conceal his excitement* 这类长短语中，重音通常落在最核心的实词上（exert, **peculiar** fascination, **hardly** conceal his **excitement**）。尤其注意 **musty**、**labyrinth**、**miniature** 的发音——这三个词中国学生容易读错重音位置。
  - **复述 (retell)**：合上课文，用 2 分钟把 Frank 发现名画的故事讲出来。**四步叙事链**：Frank 去古玩店 → 发现货箱 → 恳求打开发现箱底的微型画 → 50 英镑买下 → 后来证明是 Correggio 的真迹，价值几十万英镑。
  - **造句迁移**：用今天的 5 个高价值结构造你自己的句子——
    1. *exert ... on ...*（对……施加……）—— *Social media exerts a powerful influence on young people's spending habits.*
    2. *above all*（最重要的是）—— *To learn a language well, you need practice, a good teacher, and above all, patience.*
    3. *be bent on + doing*（决心要……）—— *She is bent on finishing the marathon, no matter how long it takes.*
    4. *can't be bothered to do*（嫌麻烦不想做）—— *I can't be bothered to cook tonight — let's order pizza.*
    5. *The moment + 从句 ...*（一……就……）—— *The moment I walked into the room, I knew something was wrong.*
  - **延伸思考**：本课是一篇**叙事 + 论说混合型**文章——第一段先\"破题\"（古玩店的魅力 + 淘宝者的素质），第二段转入了一个**具体的故事**来\"论证\"。这是一种经典的英文写作套路：*general statement → concrete example*。下次你写议论文时，不妨也试试这个公式——先提出一个判断（\"没有人是靠运气发现珍品的\"），再用一个生动的故事来\"证明\"它。另一个值得回味的点是：Frank 捡漏的根本原因不是 knowledge 本身，而是 **knowledge + perseverance + courage to ask**（如果他没有 begged the dealer to open the box，Correggio 可能永远埋在那箱陶器底下）——好的故事总是在\"知识\"之上，再叠加一层\"性格\"。
</Callout>
