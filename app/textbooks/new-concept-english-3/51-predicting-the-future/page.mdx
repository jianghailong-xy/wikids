  预测未来 **notoriously difficult**（出了名的难）。可早在 20 世纪 60 年代，一个英国人 **Leon Bagrit** 就准确预言了今天电脑的许多用途——掌上电脑、导航、辅助医生诊断、替办公室白领做重复劳动……几乎全说中了。本课先用第一段铺一条悬念：1970 年代电脑还又大又少（**mainframe** 主机要占满整间空调房），怎么会有人想到日后电脑会像电视机一样飞入寻常百姓家？
  不过，连 Bagrit 这样的预言家也有一样没能预见到。带着课文开头那道题去读：**What was the “future” electronic development that Leon Bagrit wasn't able to foresee?**（莱昂·巴格瑞特未能预见到的那项“未来”电子技术是什么？）读的时候顺手留意全篇是怎样用 **过去将来时 would**、**情态完成式 could have foreseen**、**虚拟语气 If he were ... he would** 把“站在过去看未来”这层时间感织出来的。

## The Text 课文

Predicting the future is **notoriously difficult**. **Who could have imagined**, in the mid 1970s, for example, that by the end of the 20th century, computers **would be as common** in people's homes **as TV sets**? In the 1970s, computers were **common enough**, but only in big business, government departments, and large organizations. These were the **so-called** mainframe machines. Mainframe computers were very large indeed, **often occupying** whole air-conditioned rooms, **employing** full-time technicians and **run on** specially-written software. Though these large machines still exist, **many of their functions have been taken over** by small powerful personal computers, **commonly known as** PCs.

In 1975, a primitive machine called the Altair, was launched in the USA. **It can properly be described as** the first 'home computer' and it **pointed the way to the future**. This was followed, at the end of the 1970s, by a machine called an Apple. In the early 1980s, the computer giant, IBM produced the world's first Personal Computer. This **ran on** an 'operating system' called DOS, produced by **a then small company** named Microsoft. The IBM Personal Computer was widely copied. **From those humble beginnings**, we have seen the development of the user-friendly home computers and multimedia machines which are **in common use** today.

**Considering how recent these developments are**, it is even more remarkable that **as long ago as** the 1960s, an Englishman, Leon Bagrit, **was able to predict** some of the uses of computers which we know today. Bagrit **dismissed the idea that** computers would learn to 'think' for themselves and **would 'rule the world'**, which people **liked to believe** in those days. Bagrit **foresaw a time when** computers would be small enough to hold in the hand, **when they would be capable of** providing information about traffic jams and suggesting alternative routes, when they would be used in hospitals to help doctors to diagnose illnesses, when they would **relieve office workers and accountants of** dull, repetitive clerical work. All these computer uses **have become commonplace**. Of course, Leon Bagrit **could not possibly have foreseen** the development of the Internet, the worldwide system that **enables us to communicate instantly** with anyone in any part of the world by using computers linked to telephone networks. **Nor could he have foreseen** how we could use the Internet to obtain information on every known subject, so we can read it on a screen in our homes and even print it as well if we want to. Computers have become **smaller and smaller, more and more powerful and cheaper and cheaper**. This is **what makes** Leon Bagrit's predictions particularly remarkable. **If he, or someone like him, were still alive today, he would probably be able to tell us** what to expect in the next fifty years.

## 课文注释 Notes on the text

<Callout type="info" title="8 个值得停下来嚼一嚼的点">
  1. **in the mid 1970s** (l.1) — “20 世纪 70 年代中叶”。**mid-** 表“……中期”；**the 1970s** = 70 年代（数字 + s，前加 the）。同型：*in the early 1980s*（80 年代初）/ *in the late 1990s*（90 年代末）。
  2. **Who could have imagined ... that ...?** (ll.1-4) — **情态动词 + have + 过去分词**用于对**过去**的推测 / 感叹：“当年谁能想到……？”这里是**反问句**，实际意思是“谁也想不到”。同型：*Who could have known? / Nobody could have predicted this.*
  3. **so-called** (l.6) — “号称的、所谓的”，作定语，常含一点保留语气。课文 *the so-called mainframe machines* = 所谓的主机。
  4. **Mainframe computers were very large indeed ... software** (ll.6-8) — 句中 *occupying* 和 *employing* 是**现在分词短语作伴随状语**，*run on ...* 中的 **run** 是过去分词（与前面并列，被动含义“靠……运行”），它们都在补充说明 mainframe computers。
  5. **take over** (l.8) — “接管、接替”。课文 *many of their functions have been taken over by ...*（被动）= 它们的许多功能已被……接替。同型：*Robots have taken over many factory jobs.*
  6. **a then small company** (l.14) — “那时候规模尚小的一家公司”。**then** 在这里作形容词置于名词前 = “当时的”。同型：*the then president*（当时的总统）。
  7. **dismiss the idea that ...** (l.18) — “不考虑 / 否定……这个想法”。**dismiss** = 摒弃、不予理会；后接**同位语从句** *that computers would ...*。同型：*He dismissed the rumour as nonsense.*
  8. **the Internet, the worldwide system that ...** (l.24) — 逗号后的 *the worldwide system that ...* 是**同位语**，进一步解释名词 *the Internet*。这种“专有名词 + 逗号 + 解释性同位语”的写法在科技类文章里很常见。
</Callout>

## 3. 关键句型 谈论“过去眼中的未来”：would / could have / 虚拟语气

<Callout type="tip" title="一分钟回顾">
  本课通篇在做一件事——**站在过去的某个时间点，谈论“那时的未来”**。这需要几把专门的“时间尺”：

  | 结构 | 课文例句 | 用途 |
  |---|---|---|
  | **could have + 过去分词** | *Who **could have imagined** ...? / he **could not possibly have foreseen** ...* | 对**过去**的推测 / 感叹：“（当年）怎么可能想到” |
  | **过去将来时 would + V** | *computers **would be** as common ... / they **would be used** in hospitals* | 从“过去时间点”往后看的“将来” |
  | **was able to + V** | *Bagrit **was able to predict** ...* | 过去“成功做到了”（强调结果，比 could 更实) |
  | **there would be** | *Who could have imagined that **there would be** as many computers as TV sets?* | “将来会有……”的过去将来存在句 |
  | **what + V（名词性从句）** | *This is **what makes** his predictions remarkable.* | 主语 / 表语从句，= the thing that ... |
  | **if + were ... , would ...** | ***If he ... were** still alive today, he **would** probably be able to tell us ...* | **虚拟语气**：与现在事实相反的假设 |

  ### ⚠️ 4 个本课最容易踩的雷区

  1. **could have done ≠ 一般过去**：*could have imagined* 是“（本可以 / 怎么可能）想到”，针对过去；不要写成 *could imagine*。
  2. **过去将来只用 would / should，绝不用 will**：主句是过去时（*foresaw / dismissed*），从句里看“未来”一律 **would**。
  3. **believe in + 动名词 / 名词**：*believe in computers **taking over and ruling** the world*——介词 in 后接 V-ing（动名词复合结构），不接 that 从句、也不接动词原形。
  4. **虚拟语气 be 动词用 were**：与现在相反的假设里，所有人称都用 **were**（*If he were alive*），主句配 **would + 动词原形**。
</Callout>

## 4. 难点：倒装还原 + 分词作状语 Special difficulties

<Callout type="info" title="把“倒装的复杂句”还原成“分词紧凑句”">
  教材 *Key structures and Special difficulties* 给的改写题是：

  > **Indeed, very large were those mainframe computers which often occupied whole air-conditioned rooms, were required to employ full-time technicians and ran on specially-written software.**

  要求**不改变意思**地改写成 *Mainframe computers ...*。标准答案就是课文 ll.6-8 的原句：

  > **Mainframe computers were very large indeed, often occupying whole air-conditioned rooms, employing full-time technicians and run on specially-written software.**

  对照看两处变化：

  | 原（倒装 + 定语从句） | 改（正常语序 + 分词） |
  |---|---|
  | *very large **were** those mainframe computers*（表语提前的**完全倒装**） | *Mainframe computers **were** very large indeed*（恢复主系表） |
  | *which **often occupied** ... , **were required to** employ ... and **ran** on ...*（三个并列谓语的定语从句） | *often **occupying** ... , **employing** ... and **run** on ...*（**现在分词 / 过去分词作状语**，更紧凑） |

  ### ⚠️ 改写时的 3 个雷区
  1. **主动动作 → 现在分词**（occupy → occupying；employ → employing）；**被动含义 → 过去分词**（run on = 靠……运行，含被动色彩 → **run**，正是 run 的过去分词形式）。
  2. **indeed 的位置**：作“确实”讲修饰形容词时，放在 *very large* 之后——*were very large indeed*。
  3. 分词短语的**逻辑主语**必须与主句主语一致：这里都是 *Mainframe computers*，所以三个分词并列挂在它后面，读起来一气呵成。
</Callout>

  动笔前先列“他预见到的 4 项 + 他没料到的 1 项”：
  1. 掌上电脑（small enough to hold in the hand）
  2. 提供路况、推荐替代路线（traffic jams / alternative routes）
  3. 帮助医生诊断疾病（help doctors to diagnose illnesses）
  4. 把白领 / 会计从重复文书工作中解放（relieve ... of repetitive clerical work）
  5. **他没能预见**：互联网（the Internet / obtaining information on any subject）
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  As early as the 1960s, Leon Bagrit predicted several uses of computers we know today. He foresaw that they would become small enough to hold in the hand, would give information about traffic jams, would help doctors to diagnose illnesses and would relieve office workers of repetitive clerical work. However, he failed to foresee the development of the Internet, which now lets us obtain information on any subject and communicate instantly with anyone. *(72 words)*

## 11. 作文 Composition (约 300 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  Write a composition in about 300 words on **one** of the following:
  - **a** Predict the next 20 years in computer development.
  - **b** Describe your attitude to computers and give reasons for it.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="提示骨架（以 a 题“预测未来 20 年”为例）">
  - **开篇**：呼应课文——既然 Bagrit 都能预见这么多，不妨大胆推演下一个 20 年。
  - **主体三块**（每块一个领域 + 一个 *would / will be able to* 句）：
    1. **人工智能**：电脑将能听懂、看懂、写作，成为日常助手。
    2. **健康 / 可穿戴**：设备能持续监测身体、提前预警疾病。
    3. **交通 / 城市**：自动驾驶 + 智慧城市让出行更安全高效。
  - **隐忧一句**（让文章不单薄）：隐私、就业、对技术的依赖。
  - **收尾**：呼应 *predicting the future is notoriously difficult*——唯一确定的是变化本身。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架">
  - **时态主线**：谈未来用 *will / is going to / is likely to*；谈“将能够”用 *will be able to*；谈趋势用现在进行 *is increasingly ...*。
  - **“预测”动词库**：*foresee, anticipate, expect, envisage, it is likely that, there is every chance that, in all probability*。
  - **“能力 / 用途”句型**：*Computers will be capable of -ing / will be used to do / will enable us to do / will relieve us of*。
  - **程度递进**：*smaller and smaller, faster and faster, more and more powerful*（仿课文结尾的叠加比较级）。
  - **让步亮点**：*Useful as they are, computers also raise concerns about privacy and jobs.*（as 引导让步倒装）。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  Predicting the future is notoriously difficult, yet the past fifty years suggest that change will only accelerate. Over the next twenty years, computers are likely to become so deeply woven into daily life that we will scarcely notice them.

  First, artificial intelligence will mature. Computers will be able to understand speech, recognise images and draft documents almost as well as people, acting as tireless assistants in schools, offices and homes. Many routine tasks that still occupy us today will quietly be taken over.

  Secondly, computing will move closer to the body. Wearable and implantable devices will monitor our health around the clock, warning us of illness long before symptoms appear and helping doctors to diagnose problems with far greater accuracy than before.

  Thirdly, our cities and roads will be transformed. Self-driving vehicles, linked through fast networks, will be capable of choosing the safest routes and easing the traffic jams that Bagrit himself foresaw. Energy, water and transport will be managed by systems that learn and adapt.

  Useful as all this is, it will not come without cost. As computers gather ever more information about us, questions of privacy will grow sharper, and some jobs will disappear even as new ones are created. We may also become uneasily dependent on machines we no longer fully understand.

  In the end, the only safe prediction is that the future will surprise us. If Leon Bagrit, who could not foresee the Internet, were writing today, even he would hesitate to say exactly what the next fifty years will bring. What seems certain is that computers will go on becoming smaller, cheaper and more powerful — and that, in twenty years, today's marvels will already feel commonplace. *(约 290 words)*
</Callout>

## 12. 书信写作 Letter writing (约 100 词)

<Callout type="info" title="任务">
  You have arranged to place your house at the disposal of a friend of yours while you are away on holiday. You are now about to leave. Write him or her a letter of about **100 words** telling him or her **what final arrangements you have made**.（你要外出度假，已安排好让朋友在此期间住进你家。临行前写信告诉对方你做的最后安排。）
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="写作脚手架">
  - **称呼**：*Dear + 名字*（朋友之间用名）。
  - **开头**：交代背景 + 写信目的 —— *Just a quick note before I leave for ... tomorrow.*
  - **主体（按“安排”逐条列）**：
    1. **钥匙**：*I've left a spare key with the neighbour at No. 12.*
    2. **水电 / 设备**：*The boiler is a little temperamental — the instructions are on the kitchen table.*
    3. **植物 / 宠物**：*Do please water the plants twice a week and feed the cat in the mornings.*
    4. **邮件 / 紧急联系**：*If anything goes wrong, you can reach me on my mobile.*
  - **结尾**：*Make yourself completely at home. Help yourself to whatever is in the fridge. See you in two weeks!*
  - **时态**：已做好的安排用**现在完成 have left / have arranged**；请求对方用**祈使句 / Do please ...**；将来用 *will be back*。
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="参考范文（自己写完再看）">
  Dear Tom,

  Just a quick note before I fly off tomorrow morning. Thank you again for agreeing to look after the house. I've left a spare key with Mrs. Lee next door at No. 12, in case you lose yours.

  The boiler can be temperamental, so I've written the instructions and stuck them on the fridge. Do please water the plants twice a week and feed Misty every morning — she eats far less than she pretends to.

  The post will be held at the office, so don't worry about it. If anything goes wrong, ring me on my mobile any time.

  Make yourself completely at home, and help yourself to whatever is in the fridge. I'll be back in two weeks.

  Best wishes,

  Anna *(约 110 words)*
</Callout>

## 13. 拓展练习 Going further

<Callout type="tip" title="自学者建议">
  - **影子跟读 (shadowing)**：用第 1 节的三个 SayIt 按钮跟读 3-5 遍。重点啃两处节奏——反问句 *Who could have **imagined** ...?* 的语调上扬，以及一连串 *when they would ... , when they would ...* 排比时句首的轻读、句尾的略升。
  - **复述 (retell)**：合上课文，用 2 分钟把全文讲一遍。**叙事三段**：① 1970 年代电脑又大又少（mainframe 占满空调房）→ ② 1975 Altair → Apple → IBM PC（靠 DOS / Microsoft）→ 家用电脑普及 → ③ 早在 60 年代 Bagrit 就预言了掌上电脑 / 导航 / 医疗 / 办公，唯独没料到**互联网**。
  - **造句迁移**：用今天的 5 个核心结构造你自己的句子——
    1. *It is + adj + to do*（做某事是……的）—— *It is notoriously hard to learn a language well.*
    2. *Who could have + 过去分词 ...?*（当年谁能料到）—— *Who could have predicted the rise of smartphones?*
    3. *foresee a time when ...*（预见到……的那一天）—— *I can foresee a time when cars drive themselves.*
    4. *could not possibly have foreseen ...*（根本不可能预见）—— *They could not possibly have foreseen the consequences.*
    5. *If ... were ... , ... would ...*（与现在相反的虚拟）—— *If I were you, I would buy it now.*
  - **延伸思考**：本课题为 *Predicting the future*，妙就妙在它**一边赞叹预言的准，一边提醒预言的限**——Bagrit 算准了体积、性能、价格三条曲线，却没算到“互联网”这个把所有电脑连起来的拐点。写英文议论文谈“未来”时，不妨学这种**两面笔法**：先肯定可预见的趋势（用 *will / would be able to*），再补一句不可预见的变量（用 *could not have foreseen / no one can be sure*）——文章立刻有了分寸感与思辨味。
</Callout>
