## A — Study this example

Look at this sentence:

<SayIt text="This house was built in 1981." />

**This house was built** is passive. Compare active and passive:

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| Somebody **built** this house in 1981. | This house **was built** in 1981. |

When we use an **active** verb, we say what the subject **does**:

<SayIt text="My grandfather was a builder. He built this house in 1981." />
<br />
<SayIt text="It's a big company. It employs two hundred people." />

When we use a **passive** verb, we say what **happens to** the subject:

<SayIt text="'How old is this house?' 'It was built in 1981.'" />
<br />
<SayIt text="Two hundred people are employed by the company." />

<Callout type="tip" title="The core shift">
  **Active:** I tell you *who did it*. The doer is the star.<br />
  **Passive:** I tell you *what happened*. The action is the star — and the doer may not even appear.
</Callout>

## B — When to use the passive

When we use the passive, **who or what causes the action** is often unknown or unimportant:

<SayIt text="A lot of money was stolen in the robbery." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Somebody stole it, but we don't know who.)</p>

<SayIt text="Is this room cleaned every day?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(Does somebody clean it? — it's not important who.)</p>

<Callout type="info" title="The passive test">
  Ask yourself: **Do I care who did it?** If the answer is "not really" or "I don't know", the passive is probably your best choice.
</Callout>

A few more examples where the doer isn't the point:

<SayIt text="My car was stolen last week." />
<br />
<SayIt text="The streets are swept every night." />
<br />
<SayIt text="English is spoken all over the world." />

## C — Adding *by ...* when you need the doer

If we **do** want to say who does or what causes the action, we use **by**:

<SayIt text="This house was built by my grandfather." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Two hundred people are employed by the company." />

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't overuse by">
  Only add <strong>by ...</strong> when the doer is new, interesting, or surprising information. Saying <em>"The rubbish is collected by the bin men every Tuesday"</em> is grammatically correct but sounds unnatural — unless the bin men are the surprise of the story.
</Callout>

## D — The formula: be + past participle

The passive is always:

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-2xl border-2 border-purple-200 bg-gradient-to-br from-purple-50 to-indigo-50 p-5 text-center shadow-sm">
  <p className="text-2xl font-bold text-purple-800">
    <span className="text-purple-400">be</span> + <span className="text-indigo-600">past participle</span>
  </p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-slate-500">is done &nbsp;·&nbsp; was cleaned &nbsp;·&nbsp; are damaged &nbsp;·&nbsp; were built &nbsp;·&nbsp; is seen</p>
</div>

The past participle often ends in **-ed** (cleaned, damaged, invited etc.), but many important verbs are **irregular** (built, done, stolen, seen etc.).

## E — Present simple passive

| | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| **Form** | clean(s) / see(s) etc. | am/is/are + cleaned/seen etc. |
| **Example** | Somebody **cleans** the room every day. | The room **is cleaned** every day. |

<SayIt text="Many accidents are caused by careless driving." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I'm not invited to parties very often." />
<br />
<SayIt text="How is this word pronounced?" />

## F — Past simple passive

| | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| **Form** | cleaned / saw etc. | was/were + cleaned/seen etc. |
| **Example** | Somebody **cleaned** the room yesterday. | The room **was cleaned** yesterday. |

<SayIt text="We were woken up by a loud noise during the night." />
<br />
<SayIt text="'Did you go to the party?' 'No, I wasn't invited.'" />
<br />
<SayIt text="How much money was stolen in the robbery?" />

<Callout type="info" title="Present vs past — the only difference">
  The passive doesn't add extra rules for each tense. The **be** verb carries the tense, and the past participle stays the same:<br />
  • Present: <strong>is</strong> cleaned &nbsp;·&nbsp; <strong>are</strong> caused &nbsp;·&nbsp; <strong>am</strong> invited<br />
  • Past: <strong>was</strong> cleaned &nbsp;·&nbsp; <strong>were</strong> caused &nbsp;·&nbsp; <strong>was</strong> invited
</Callout>
