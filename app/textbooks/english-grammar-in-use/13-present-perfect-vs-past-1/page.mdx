You already know the **present perfect** (*I have done* — Units 7–12) and the **past simple** (*I did* — Unit 5). On their own, each is fairly tidy. The hard part is **choosing** between them — *I've lost my key* or *I lost my key*? This unit is about that choice, and there's one idea that sorts out almost every case: **is the sentence about the situation *now*, or only about the *past*?**

## A — Study this example situation

Tom is looking for his key. He can't find it.

<SayIt text="He has lost his key." />

**This means:** he doesn't have his key **now**. We use the **present perfect** (*has lost*) because we're really talking about the present — the result you can see right now.

Ten minutes later:

<SayIt text="Now Tom has found his key. He has it now." />

So which tense answers which question?

Both tenses are true about Tom, but they look in different directions:

<SayIt text="He lost his key, but now he has found it." />

- **He lost his key** — *past simple*. This tells us only about the **past**.
- **He has found it** — *present perfect*. This tells us about the **situation now** (he has the key again).

<Callout type="tip" title="The one idea to hold on to">
  The <strong>present perfect</strong> (<em>something has happened</em>) is really a <strong>present</strong> tense — it tells us about the situation <strong>now</strong>. <br />
  The <strong>past simple</strong> (<em>something happened</em>) tells us <strong>only about the past</strong> — it says nothing about now.
</Callout>

So if somebody says *'Tom **lost** his key'*, we don't know whether he has the key now or not. We only know that he lost it at some time in the past. But *'Tom **has lost** his key'* tells us about now: he doesn't have it.

## B — Compare the two

Look at how the meaning shifts when only the tense changes:

| Present perfect → about **now** | Past simple → only about the **past** |
|---|---|
| They**'ve gone** away. They'll be back on Friday. *(they are away now)* | They **went** away, but I think they're back home now. |
| It **has stopped** raining, so we don't need the umbrella. *(it isn't raining now)* | It **stopped** raining for a while, but now it's raining again. |
| I**'ve lost** my key. *(I can't get in)* | I **lost** my key, but then I found it again. |

<SayIt text="They've gone away. They'll be back on Friday." />
&nbsp;
<SayIt text="It has stopped raining, so we don't need the umbrella." />

## C — New or recent happenings

We often use the **present perfect** for **new** or **recent** happenings, especially to give news — because the point is the result we have *now*:

<SayIt text="I've repaired the washing machine. It's working OK now." />
&nbsp;
<SayIt text="Hannah has had a baby! It's a boy." />

But we use the **past simple** (not the present perfect) for things that are **not** recent or new — finished history with no special link to the present moment:

<SayIt text="Mozart was a composer. He wrote more than 600 pieces of music." />
&nbsp;
<SayIt text="My mother grew up in Italy." />

We don't say *Mozart has been a composer* or *He has written more than 600 pieces* — that's all finished, long ago. Compare:

<Callout type="info" title="New vs general">
  - **New / recent:** *Somebody **has invented** a new type of washing machine.* (it's news) <br />
  - **General history:** *Who **invented** the telephone?* &nbsp;(not <s>has invented</s>)
</Callout>

## D — News, then details

Here's a pattern you'll hear constantly. We use the **present perfect** to **give** a piece of new information. But once we **continue** talking about it — asking *how, when, where* — we switch to the **past simple**:

<ChatDialog
  title="Give the news, then ask for the details"
  participants={[
    { name: "Sam", emoji: "🧑", side: "left", tone: "amber" },
    { name: "Lee", emoji: "🧑‍🦱", side: "right", tone: "sky" },
  ]}
  turns={[
    { speaker: "Sam", text: "Ow! I've burnt myself." },
    { speaker: "Lee", text: "How did you do that?" },
    { speaker: "Sam", text: "I picked up a hot dish." },
    { speaker: "Lee", text: "Look! Somebody has spilt something on the sofa." },
    { speaker: "Sam", text: "Well, it wasn't me. I didn't do it!" },
  ]}
/>

Notice the wrong versions a learner might say: *How **have you done** that?* ❌ and *I **have picked** up a hot dish* ❌. Once we're into the details, it's **past simple** all the way: *did you do*, *I picked up*, *it wasn't me*, *I didn't do it*.

<Callout type="tip" title="The rhythm of news">
  <strong>Headline → present perfect.</strong> <em>I've broken my arm.</em> <br />
  <strong>Details → past simple.</strong> <em>How did it happen? I fell off a ladder.</em>
</Callout>

## E — Two friends of the past simple: *ago* and finished times

This is the most reliable signal of all. A **finished time word** — *yesterday, last week, in 2010, when I was a child*, and especially **ago** — locks you into the **past simple**. *ago* literally means *a finished point before now*, so it can never go with the present perfect:

<div className="not-prose my-6 grid grid-cols-1 gap-3 sm:grid-cols-2">
  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">✅ past simple</p>
    <p className="mt-1 font-semibold text-slate-800">Tom <strong>lost</strong> his key ten minutes <strong>ago</strong>.</p>
    <p className="mt-1 font-semibold text-slate-800">I <strong>saw</strong> Lucy <strong>yesterday</strong>.</p>
  </div>
  <div className="rounded-xl border-2 border-rose-200 bg-rose-50 p-4">
    <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-rose-700">❌ present perfect</p>
    <p className="mt-1 font-semibold text-slate-500 line-through">Tom has lost his key ten minutes ago.</p>
    <p className="mt-1 font-semibold text-slate-500 line-through">I have seen Lucy yesterday.</p>
  </div>
</div>

The flip side: a period that **continues up to now** — *today, this week, this morning, this year* — is **unfinished**, so the present perfect fits comfortably:

<SayIt text="I haven't seen Tom this morning. Have you?" />
&nbsp;
<SayIt text="Have you had a holiday this year?" />

<Callout type="warning" title="Quick test before you choose">
  Is there a finished time word (<em>ago, yesterday, last ...</em>)? → <strong>past simple</strong>. <br />
  Is the point the result you have <em>now</em>, or an unfinished period (<em>today, this week</em>)? → <strong>present perfect</strong>.
</Callout>
