In **Unit 53** you met verbs that are followed by **-ing** (*enjoy doing*, *stop doing*). This unit is the other half of the story: a large group of verbs is followed by **to + infinitive** instead — *decide **to do***, *promise **to do***, *forget **to do***. Choosing the right one isn't a matter of meaning; it's a matter of which verb comes first. So the real skill here is learning which verbs belong in which group.

## A — Verb + to ... (infinitive)

After these verbs you can use **to ...** (the infinitive):

<div className="not-prose my-6 grid grid-cols-2 gap-2 sm:grid-cols-3 lg:grid-cols-5">
  {["offer", "decide", "agree", "refuse", "plan", "arrange", "forget", "manage", "fail", "promise", "threaten", "deserve", "afford", "learn", "tend"].map((w) => (
    <span key={w} className="rounded-lg border-2 border-indigo-200 bg-indigo-50 px-3 py-2 text-center text-sm font-semibold text-indigo-800">{w} <span className="text-indigo-400">to …</span></span>
  ))}
</div>

<SayIt text="It was a long way to walk, so we decided to take a taxi home." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Simon was in a difficult situation, so I agreed to help him." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I waved to Karen, but I failed to attract her attention." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I like Dan, but I think he tends to talk too much." />
<br />
<SayIt text="How old were you when you learnt to drive?" />

<Callout type="info" title="learn to drive / learn how to drive">
  With <em>learn</em> (and a few others) you can drop a question word in between: <em>How old were you when you learnt <strong>to drive</strong>?</em> means the same as <em>… learnt <strong>how to drive</strong>?</em> More on question words in section D.
</Callout>

### The negative is *not to ...*

To make the infinitive negative, put **not** in front of **to**:

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-2xl border-2 border-rose-200 bg-gradient-to-br from-rose-50 to-pink-50 p-5 text-center shadow-sm">
  <p className="text-sm font-semibold text-slate-500">verb + <span className="text-rose-700 font-bold">not to</span> + infinitive</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-base font-semibold text-rose-800">We decided <strong>not to go</strong> out because of the weather.</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-base font-semibold text-rose-800">I promised <strong>not to be</strong> late.</p>
</div>

<Callout type="warning" title="One 'to', then the plain verb">
  After <em>to</em>, use the base form — never a past tense or an -ing form. <br />
  ✅ We decided <strong>to take</strong> a taxi. &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ We decided to <s>took</s> a taxi. <br />
  ✅ He promised <strong>to call</strong>. &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ He promised to <s>calling</s>.
</Callout>

## B — But some verbs take *-ing*, not *to*

This is exactly where learners slip. A different set of verbs — like **enjoy**, **suggest** and **think (of)** — is followed by **-ing**, *not* by *to* (see **Unit 53**):

<SayIt text="I enjoy reading." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(not <s>I enjoy to read</s>)</p>

<SayIt text="Andy suggested meeting for coffee." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(not <s>Andy suggested to meet</s>)</p>

<SayIt text="Are you thinking of buying a car?" />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(not <s>thinking to buy</s>)</p>

<Callout type="tip" title="There's no shortcut — you have to learn the verb">
  <em>Decided</em> takes <strong>to</strong>; <em>suggested</em> takes <strong>-ing</strong>. Both report a plan, but the grammar is fixed by the first verb, not the meaning. Treat each verb as carrying its own pattern, like a label.
</Callout>

## C — seem / appear / pretend / claim + to ...

We also use **to ...** after **seem**, **appear**, **pretend** and **claim**:

<SayIt text="They seem to have plenty of money." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Ann pretended not to see me when she passed me in the street." />

You can also use the **continuous infinitive** (*to be -ing*) and the **perfect infinitive** (*to have done*):

<div className="not-prose my-6 overflow-hidden rounded-2xl border-2 border-teal-200 shadow-sm">
  <div className="grid grid-cols-1 sm:grid-cols-2">
    <div className="bg-teal-50 p-4">
      <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-teal-700">to be + -ing</p>
      <p className="mt-2 font-semibold text-teal-900">I pretended <strong>to be reading</strong> the newspaper.</p>
      <p className="mt-1 text-sm text-teal-700">= I pretended that I was reading</p>
    </div>
    <div className="bg-cyan-50 p-4">
      <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-cyan-700">to have + done</p>
      <p className="mt-2 font-semibold text-cyan-900">You seem <strong>to have lost</strong> weight.</p>
      <p className="mt-1 text-sm text-cyan-700">= it seems that you have lost weight</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout type="info" title="A handy paraphrase">
  <em>seem / appear to</em> ≈ <em>it seems / appears that …</em> — so <em>She appears to be enjoying the party</em> = <em>It appears that she is enjoying the party</em>, and <em>They claim to have solved it</em> = <em>They claim that they have solved it</em>.
</Callout>

## D — Verb + question word + to ...

After these verbs you can use a **question word** (*what / how / where / whether* etc.) + **to ...**:

<div className="not-prose my-6 grid grid-cols-2 gap-2 sm:grid-cols-4">
  {["ask", "decide", "remember", "forget", "learn", "explain", "understand", "wonder"].map((w) => (
    <span key={w} className="rounded-lg border-2 border-amber-200 bg-amber-50 px-3 py-2 text-center text-sm font-semibold text-amber-800">{w}</span>
  ))}
</div>

<SayIt text="We asked how to get to the station." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Have you decided where to go for your holidays?" />
<br />
<SayIt text="I don't know whether to apply for the job or not." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Do you understand what to do?" />

You can also use the pattern **show / tell / ask / advise / teach + somebody + question word + to ...**:

<SayIt text="Can somebody show me how to use this camera?" />
<br />
<SayIt text="Ask Jack. He'll tell you what to do." />

<Callout type="tip" title="The shape">
  <strong>verb (+ somebody) + what / how / where / whether + to + verb</strong> — e.g. <em>tell <span className="text-amber-700">you</span> <span className="text-amber-700">what</span> to do</em>, <em>show <span className="text-amber-700">me</span> <span className="text-amber-700">how</span> to use it</em>. Note <em>whether</em>, not <em>if</em>, before <em>to</em>: ✅ <em>whether to go</em>, ❌ <s>if to go</s>.
</Callout>
