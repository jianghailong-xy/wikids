## A — all / some / most + a noun (no *of*)

These words all answer the question *how many?* or *how much?*:

<div className="not-prose my-6 flex flex-wrap justify-center gap-2">
  {["all", "some", "any", "most", "much", "many", "(a) little", "(a) few", "no"].map((w) => (
    <span key={w} className="rounded-lg border-2 border-brand-200 bg-brand-50 px-3 py-2 text-center text-sm font-semibold text-brand-800">{w}</span>
  ))}
</div>

When you put them **straight in front of a noun**, you do **not** use *of*:

<SayIt text="All cars have wheels." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Some cars can go faster than others." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Many people drive too fast." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I go away most weekends." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I feel really tired. I've got no energy." />

<Callout type="warning" title="No 'of' before a plain noun">
  We do <strong>not</strong> say <em>all of cars</em> or <em>some of people</em>. <br />
  ✅ <strong>Some people</strong> learn more easily than others. <br />
  ❌ <s>Some of people</s> learn more easily than others.
</Callout>

Here we are talking about cars, people, weekends **in general** — not a particular group. That is the clue: no *of*.

## B — all *of* / some *of* / most *of* + the / this / my ...

The moment a little word like **the, this, that, these, those, my, your, his** comes between the quantifier and the noun, you add **of**:

<div className="not-prose my-6 flex flex-wrap items-center justify-center gap-3 text-center">
  <div className="rounded-2xl border-2 border-amber-200 bg-amber-50 px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-amber-800">
    some of<br />most of<br />none of <span className="font-normal">etc.</span>
  </div>
  <span className="text-2xl font-bold text-slate-400">+</span>
  <div className="rounded-2xl border-2 border-sky-200 bg-sky-50 px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-sky-800">
    the … &nbsp; my …<br />this … &nbsp; these …<br />that … &nbsp; those …
  </div>
</div>

So you can say:

<SayIt text="Some of the people I work with are very strange." />
<br />
<SayIt text="None of this money is mine." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Have you read any of these books?" />
<br />
<SayIt text="I was ill yesterday. I spent most of the day in bed." />

<Callout type="warning" title="Still no shortcut">
  ✅ <strong>most of my time</strong>, <strong>most of the time</strong> &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ <s>most of time</s> <br />
  ✅ <strong>some of those people</strong> &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ <s>some of people</s>
</Callout>

### *of* is optional after **all** and **half**

After **all** and **half** (and only these), you can keep or drop *of* before *the / my / this* etc. — both are correct:

<SayIt text="All my friends live near here." />
&nbsp;=&nbsp;
<SayIt text="All of my friends live near here." />

<br />

<SayIt text="Half this money is mine." />
&nbsp;=&nbsp;
<SayIt text="Half of this money is mine." />

### General vs a specific group

This is the meaning behind the rule. *of the* points to **a particular group**; no *of* keeps it **general**.

## C — all *of* / some *of* / none *of* + it / us / you / them

Before the pronouns **it, us, you, them**, *of* is **always required** — you can never drop it:

<div className="not-prose my-6 flex flex-wrap items-center justify-center gap-3 text-center">
  <div className="rounded-2xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-emerald-50 px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-emerald-800">
    all of &nbsp; some of<br />any of &nbsp; most of<br />half of &nbsp; none of
  </div>
  <span className="text-2xl font-bold text-slate-400">+</span>
  <div className="rounded-2xl border-2 border-rose-200 bg-rose-50 px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-rose-800">
    it &nbsp; us<br />you &nbsp; them
  </div>
</div>

  ✅ <strong>All of us</strong> were late. &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ <s>All us</s> were late. <br />
  ✅ I've only read <strong>half of it</strong>. &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ I've only read <s>half it</s>.
</Callout>

## D — Used alone, without a noun

You can also use **some, most, none, a few, half** etc. on their own, when the noun is already clear:

<SayIt text="Some cars have four doors and some have two." />
<br />
<SayIt text="A few of the shops were open, but most were closed." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Half this money is mine, and half is yours." />

<Callout type="tip" title="Not 'the half'">
  Standing alone it is just <strong>half</strong>, never <s>the half</s>: <em>half is yours</em> ✅.
</Callout>

  Ask yourself: *is there a little word (the / this / my / these) or a pronoun (it / us / them) after the quantifier?* If **yes**, use **of**. If it's a plain noun spoken in general, **leave 'of' out** — and remember *all* and *half* don't mind either way.
