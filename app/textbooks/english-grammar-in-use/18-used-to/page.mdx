Some things were true once but aren't any more. You smoked, then you stopped. There was a cinema on the corner, then they pulled it down. English has a neat little tool for exactly this — for a **past habit** or a **past state** that has now finished: **used to**.

## A — Study this example situation

Nicola doesn't travel much these days. She prefers to stay at home. But a few years ago things were different.

<SayIt text="She used to travel a lot." />
<br />
<SayIt text="She used to go away two or three times a year." />

**She used to travel a lot** = she travelled a lot regularly in the past, but she doesn't now.

## B — used to (do) — past habits and past states

We use **used to + verb** for something that **happened regularly** or was **true** in the past, but no longer happens or is no longer true.

For a habit you've given up:

<SayIt text="I used to play tennis a lot, but I don't play very often now." />
<br />
<SayIt text="David used to spend a lot of money on clothes. These days he can't afford it." />

You can leave the verb out if it's already clear, exactly like other auxiliaries:

<Callout type="tip" title="“I used to” can stand alone">
  Just like <em>I do / I don't</em>, you can answer with <strong>used to</strong> on its own — <em>"Not now, but I used to."</em> The listener fills in the rest (<em>I used to go</em>).
</Callout>

It isn't only for actions. We also use **used to** for **states** — things that were *true* about the past:

<SayIt text="This building is now a furniture shop. It used to be a cinema." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I used to think Mark was unfriendly, but now I realise he's a very nice person." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Liz used to have very long hair when she was a child." />

<Callout type="info" title="Habits and states, both gone">
  <strong>Habit:</strong> <em>I used to smoke.</em> (a repeated action you've stopped) <br />
  <strong>State:</strong> <em>There used to be a cinema here.</em> (a situation that was true, now isn't)
</Callout>

## C — used to is always past — there is no present

**I used to do** something is always **past**. There is **no present form**. You cannot say *"I use to do"* to talk about now. For the present, use the **present simple** (*I do*).

| | the past | the present |
|---|---|---|
| habit | he **used to play** | he **plays** |
| place | we **used to live** | we **live** |
| existing | there **used to be** | there **is** |

<SayIt text="We used to live in a small village, but now we live in London." />
<br />
<SayIt text="He used to play tennis. These days he plays golf." />

<Callout type="warning" title="No present, and no -ing">
  ✅ <em>I <strong>used to</strong> drive to work.</em> &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ <em>I <strong>use to</strong> drive to work.</em> (there is no present "use to") <br />
  ✅ <em>There <strong>used to be</strong> a school here.</em> &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ <em>There <strong>uses to be</strong> a school here.</em>
</Callout>

## D — Questions and negatives: did you use to ...?

The normal **question** is **did (you) use to ...?** — and the normal **negative** is **didn't use to ...**. Notice: once **did / didn't** appears, **use** drops its **-d** (just like *did you play?*, not *did you played?*).

<SayIt text="Did you use to eat a lot of sweets when you were a child?" />
<br />
<SayIt text="I didn't use to like him, but now we're good friends." />

<Callout type="warning" title="did + use to (no -d on use)">
  ✅ <strong>Did</strong> you <strong>use to</strong> live here? &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ Did you <strong>used to</strong> live here? <br />
  ✅ I <strong>didn't use to</strong> like coffee. &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ I <strong>didn't used to</strong> like coffee. <br />
  <span className="text-sm">(The more formal negative <em>I used not to like coffee</em> is also possible.)</span>
</Callout>

## E — used to do or was doing?

Don't mix **used to do** (a habit, repeated over a period in the past) with the **past continuous** **was doing** (in the middle of one action at a particular past moment — Unit 6).

<Callout type="info" title="A habit vs the middle of an action">
  - <em>I <strong>used to watch</strong> a lot of TV.</em> → a regular habit in the past (I don't now) <br />
  - <em>I <strong>was watching</strong> TV when Rob arrived.</em> → I was in the middle of it at that moment
</Callout>

## F — used to do or am used to doing?

There's one more pair to keep apart. **I used to do** and **I am used to doing** look similar but mean completely different things (more on the second in Unit 61).

<Callout type="warning" title="Two very different structures">
  - <em>I <strong>used to live</strong> alone.</em> → I lived alone in the past, but I don't any more. (<strong>used to + base verb</strong>) <br />
  - <em>I <strong>am used to living</strong> alone.</em> → I live alone now, and it feels normal because I've done it for a while. (<strong>be used to + -ing</strong>) <br />
  So: <em>used to <strong>live</strong></em> (past habit) but <em>am used to <strong>living</strong></em> (it's familiar).
</Callout>
