You already know **in / on / at** for time (Unit 121) and place (Units 123–125). But these three little words do a lot more than point at clocks and maps. English glues them to dozens of **fixed expressions** — *in love*, *on holiday*, *at the age of* — where there's no logical rule, only the right combination to learn.

<Callout type="info" title="The honest truth about these">
  There is <strong>no rule</strong> that tells you it must be <em>in</em> love but <em>on</em> holiday. These are <strong>set phrases</strong> — collocations you memorise as whole chunks. The good news: there aren't many, and they come up constantly.
</Callout>

## A — in

We use **in** in these expressions:

**in the rain / in the sun / in the shade / in the dark / in bad weather** etc.

<SayIt text="We sat in the shade. It was too hot to sit in the sun." /> &nbsp; <SayIt text="Don't go out in the rain. Wait until it stops." />

**in a language / in a currency** etc.

<SayIt text="How do you say 'thank you' in Russian?" /> &nbsp; <SayIt text="How much is a hundred pounds in dollars?" />

**(be / fall) in love (with somebody)**

<SayIt text="They're very happy together. They're in love." />

**in a (good / bad) mood**

<SayIt text="You seem to be in a bad mood. What's the matter?" />

**in (my) opinion**

<SayIt text="In my opinion the movie wasn't very good." />

<Callout type="tip" title="One more 'in' to pocket">
  We also write things <strong>in</strong> a medium: <em>in pen</em>, <em>in ink</em>, <em>in pencil</em>. <br />
  ✅ Please fill in the form <strong>in pen</strong>, not <strong>in pencil</strong>.
</Callout>

## B — on

We use **on** in these expressions:

| Expression | Example |
|---|---|
| **on TV / on television** | I didn't see the news **on TV**. |
| **on the radio** | I heard the weather forecast **on the radio**. |
| **on the phone** | I've never met her, but I've spoken to her **on the phone**. |
| **on fire** | Look! That car is **on fire**. |
| **on purpose** (= intentionally) | I'm sorry. I didn't mean to hurt you. I didn't do it **on purpose**. |
| **on the whole** (= in general) | Sometimes I have problems at work, but **on the whole** I enjoy my job. |

<SayIt text="There's a good film on TV tonight." /> &nbsp; <SayIt text="I didn't do it on purpose — it was an accident." /> &nbsp; <SayIt text="On the whole, I enjoy my job." />

<Callout type="warning" title="It's ON, not IN, the screen">
  ✅ I saw it <strong>on</strong> TV / <strong>on</strong> television. &nbsp;&nbsp; ❌ I saw it <strong>in</strong> the television. <br />
  But news that you read is <strong>in</strong> the newspaper: ✅ I read about it <strong>in</strong> the paper.
</Callout>

## C — on holiday / on a trip etc.

We say that somebody is (or goes) **on** an activity or arrangement:

| Expression | Example |
|---|---|
| (be / go) **on holiday / on vacation** | I'm going **on holiday** next week. |
| (be / go) **on a trip / on a tour / on a cruise** etc. | One day I'd like to go **on a world tour**. |
| (be / go to a place) **on business** | Emma's away **on business** at the moment. |
| (be / go) **on strike** | There are no trains today. The drivers are **on strike**. |
| (be / go) **on a diet** | I've put on weight. I'll have to go **on a diet**. |

<Callout type="warning" title="on holiday vs for a holiday">
  You go somewhere <strong>for a holiday</strong>, but once you're away you are <strong>on holiday</strong>: <br />
  ✅ Steve has gone to France <strong>for a holiday</strong>. <br />
  ✅ Steve is <strong>on holiday</strong> in France this week. <br />
  ❌ Steve is <strong>in</strong> holiday.
</Callout>

## D — at (age, speed, temperature)

We use **at** for ages, speeds and temperatures:

**at the age of 16 / at 90 miles an hour / at 100 degrees** etc.

<SayIt text="Tracy left school at 16." /> &nbsp; <SayIt text="Tracy left school at the age of 16." />
<br />
<SayIt text="The train was travelling at 120 miles an hour." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius." />

<Callout type="info" title="One picture, two 'at's">
  A pilot's announcement packs both uses into one sentence: <br />
  <em>"We are now flying <strong>at a speed of</strong> 800 kilometres an hour and <strong>at an altitude of</strong> 9,000 metres."</em>
</Callout>
