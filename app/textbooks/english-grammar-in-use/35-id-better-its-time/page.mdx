This unit is about two ways of saying *do it — and do it now*. The first, **had better**, is strong advice with a warning hidden inside it. The second, **It's time ...**, says something is overdue. They look small, but each one has a twist of grammar that trips learners up: a *past* form (`had`, `went`) that points firmly at the **present and future**.

## A — had better (I'd better / you'd better)

**I'd better do something** = it is the sensible thing to do. And there's a warning attached: *if I don't do it, there will be a problem or a danger.*

<SayIt text="I have to meet Amy in ten minutes. I'd better go now or I'll be late." />
<br />
<SayIt text="'Shall I take an umbrella?' 'Yes, you'd better. It might rain.'" />
<br />
<SayIt text="We'd better stop for petrol soon. The tank is almost empty." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-2xl border-2 border-rose-200 bg-gradient-to-br from-rose-50 to-orange-50 p-5 text-center shadow-sm">
  <p className="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-rose-700">had better always carries a warning</p>
  <div className="my-3 flex flex-col items-center justify-center gap-2 text-sm font-semibold text-slate-600 sm:flex-row">
    <span className="rounded-lg bg-rose-200 px-3 py-1 text-rose-900">I'd better go now</span>
    <span className="text-rose-400">———&nbsp;or&nbsp;———</span>
    <span className="rounded-lg bg-orange-200 px-3 py-1 text-orange-900">⚠️ I'll be late</span>
  </div>
  <p className="text-base font-semibold text-rose-800"><em>advice</em> + <em>a problem if you don't follow it</em></p>
</div>

`I'd` is short for **I had** — so we usually say `I'd better`, `you'd better`, `we'd better`, `he'd better`.

### The negative: had better not

The negative is **I'd better not** (= I had better not):

<SayIt text="'Are you going out tonight?' 'I'd better not. I have a lot to do.'" />
<br />
<SayIt text="You don't look very well. You'd better not go to work today." />

<Callout type="warning" title="Two things to remember about the form">

- The form is **had** (usually `'d`), but the meaning is **present or future**, never past: *I'd better phone Chris, **hadn't** I?*
- We say **had better do**, not *had better to do*: ✅ We'd better **take** an umbrella. &nbsp; ❌ We'd better *to take* an umbrella.

</Callout>

## B — had better and should

**had better** is similar to **should**, but they are *not* the same. The difference is the warning.

| | should | had better |
|---|---|---|
| When? | any situation, general or specific | **only a specific situation** |
| What it means | it's a good idea | it's a good idea **— or there'll be a problem** |
| If you don't do it | no real consequence | something will go wrong |

Compare:

<SayIt text="It's a great film. You should go and see it." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">General recommendation — but nothing bad happens if you don't. (not You'd better go and see it)</p>

<SayIt text="The film starts at 8.30. You'd better go now or you'll miss the beginning." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">A specific moment with a clear danger — had better fits perfectly.</p>

<Callout type="tip" title="The test">
  Ask yourself: <strong>"...or what will happen?"</strong> If there's a real answer — you'll be late, you'll get wet, you'll miss it — use <strong>had better</strong>. If the honest answer is "nothing really", use <strong>should</strong>.
</Callout>

## C — It's time ...

The simplest form is **It's time (for somebody) to do something**:

<SayIt text="It's time to go home." /> &nbsp; <SayIt text="It's time for us to go home." />

But there's a second pattern that surprises people — **It's time + past**:

<SayIt text="It's late. It's time we went home." />

We use the **past** (`went`), but the meaning is **present or future**, not past:

<SayIt text="It's 10 o'clock and he's still in bed. It's time he got up." />
<p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-1">(not <em>It's time he gets up</em>)</p>

<Callout type="info" title="It's time you did something = it's overdue">
  This pattern says the action <strong>should have started already</strong>. We very often use it to <strong>criticise</strong> or <strong>complain</strong>:

- *It's time the children **were** in bed. It's long after their bedtime.*
- *You're very selfish. It's time you **realised** you're not the most important person in the world.*

</Callout>

### Make it stronger: It's about time ...

Adding **about** turns up the volume — it makes the criticism sharper:

<SayIt text="Jack is a great talker. But it's about time he did something instead of just talking." />

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-2xl border-2 border-violet-200 bg-gradient-to-r from-violet-50 to-fuchsia-50 p-5 shadow-sm">
  <p className="mb-3 text-center text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-violet-700">past form, present meaning</p>
  <div className="flex flex-wrap items-center justify-center gap-2 text-center">
    <span className="rounded-lg bg-violet-100 px-3 py-1 text-sm font-bold text-violet-800">It's time I <u>went</u></span>
    <span className="rounded-lg bg-violet-100 px-3 py-1 text-sm font-bold text-violet-800">It's time you <u>did</u></span>
    <span className="rounded-lg bg-violet-200 px-3 py-1 text-sm font-bold text-violet-900">It's about time he <u>got</u> up</span>
  </div>
  <p className="mt-3 text-center text-sm font-semibold text-violet-800">all mean <em>now</em> — the past tense just adds a feeling of "this is overdue"</p>
</div>
