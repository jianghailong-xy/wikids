## 97 -ing and -ed clauses (the woman talking to Tom, the boy injured in the accident)

### A. What are -ing and -ed clauses?

Look at these two sentences:

| -ing clause | -ed clause |
|:--|:--|
| Who is the woman **talking to Tom**? | The boy **injured in the accident** was taken to hospital. |

Both parts in bold are **clauses** that come after a noun. They work like relative clauses (Unit 92), but shorter — no *who* or *which* needed.

- An **-ing clause** tells you what somebody or something **is doing** (or **was doing**) at a particular time.
- An **-ed clause** has a **passive** meaning — it tells you what **happened to** somebody or something.

<Callout icon="🔑" title="The core idea">
  Think of -ing and -ed clauses as **shortened relative clauses**:

  | Full relative clause | Shortened with -ing / -ed |
  |:--|:--|
  | the woman **who is talking** to Tom | the woman **talking** to Tom |
  | the boy **who was injured** in the accident | the boy **injured** in the accident |
  | the bell **that was ringing** | a bell **ringing** |
  | the money **that was stolen** | the money **stolen** |

  **-ing** = active (the subject is doing it) &nbsp;&nbsp;|&nbsp;&nbsp; **-ed** = passive (the subject receives it)
</Callout>

<SayIt text="Who is the woman talking to Tom? The boy injured in the accident was taken to hospital." lang="en" />

---

### B. -ing clauses — what somebody is (or was) doing

We use **-ing clauses** to say what somebody or something is (or was) **doing at a particular time**:

- *Who is the woman **talking to Tom**?* (the woman is talking to Tom)
- *Police **investigating the crime** are looking for three men.* (police are investigating the crime)
- *Who were those people **waiting outside**?* (they were waiting)
- *I was woken up by a bell **ringing**.* (a bell was ringing)

<SayIt text="Who is the woman talking to Tom? Police investigating the crime are looking for three men. I was woken up by a bell ringing." lang="en" />

<Callout icon="💡" title="When to use an -ing clause">
  Use an -ing clause when:
  - The action is **happening now** or **was happening at that moment**.
  - You could rewrite it with *who/that is/was + -ing*.

  But do **NOT** use -ing for a single completed action:
  - ✓ *The man **who drove** me to the airport was very nice.* (a completed trip — NOT "the man driving me")
</Callout>

---

### C. -ing clauses — what happens all the time

You can also use an **-ing clause** to say what happens **all the time** — not just at a particular moment. It describes a permanent situation:

- *The road **connecting the two villages** is very narrow.* (the road connects the two villages)
- *I have a large room **overlooking the garden**.* (the room overlooks the garden)
- *Can you think of the name of a flower **beginning with T**?* (the name begins with T)

<SayIt text="The road connecting the two villages is very narrow. I have a large room overlooking the garden. Can you think of the name of a flower beginning with T?" lang="en" />

<Callout icon="📝" title="-ing clause = general fact vs. particular moment">
  | -ing clause | Meaning |
  |:--|:--|
  | The road **connecting the two villages** is narrow. | General fact — the road always connects them. |
  | The woman **talking to Tom** is my boss. | Particular moment — she is talking right now. |
</Callout>

---

### D. -ed clauses — passive meaning

We use **-ed clauses** with a **passive meaning**. The clause contains a **past participle** (*injured, painted, used, stolen, made, built*, etc.):

- *The boy **injured in the accident** was taken to hospital.* (= who was injured)
- *George showed me some pictures **painted by his father**.* (= which were painted)
- *The gun **used in the robbery** has been found.* (= which was used)

<SayIt text="The boy injured in the accident was taken to hospital. George showed me some pictures painted by his father. The gun used in the robbery has been found." lang="en" />

Most past participles end in **-ed**, but many are **irregular** (*stolen, made, built, written, broken*, etc.):

- *The police never found the money **stolen in the robbery**.*
- *Most of the goods **made in this factory** are exported.*

<SayIt text="The police never found the money stolen in the robbery. Most of the goods made in this factory are exported." lang="en" />

<Callout icon="⚠️" title="-ing vs. -ed — don't mix them up!">
  | Clause type | Meaning | Example |
  |:--|:--|:--|
  | **-ing** | active — somebody **is doing** it | the woman **talking** to Tom |
  | **-ed** | passive — something **is done** | the boy **injured** in the accident |

  A simple test: If you can add "who is/was + -ing" → use **-ing**. If you can add "who is/was + past participle" → use **-ed**.
</Callout>

---

### E. there is / there was + -ing and -ed clauses

You can use **there is / there was** (etc.) together with -ing and -ed clauses:

- *There were some children **swimming in the river**.*
- *Is there anybody **waiting**?*
- *There was a big red car **parked outside the house**.*

<SayIt text="There were some children swimming in the river. Is there anybody waiting? There was a big red car parked outside the house." lang="en" />

We also use **left** in this way, with the meaning **'not used, still there'**:

- *We've eaten nearly all the chocolates. There are only a few **left**.*

<SayIt text="We've eaten nearly all the chocolates. There are only a few left." lang="en" />

### Key points — Unit 97

- ✅ **-ing clauses** tell you what somebody or something **is/was doing** (active meaning):
  - *Who is the woman **talking to Tom**?* (= who is talking)
  - *Police **investigating the crime** are looking for three men.* (= who are investigating)
  - *I was woken up by a bell **ringing**.* (= which was ringing)

- ✅ **-ing clauses** can also describe **general facts** (what happens all the time):
  - *The road **connecting the two villages** is very narrow.* (= which connects)
  - *I have a large room **overlooking the garden**.* (= which overlooks)

- ✅ **-ed clauses** have a **passive meaning** (something is/was done):
  - *The boy **injured in the accident** was taken to hospital.* (= who was injured)
  - *The gun **used in the robbery** has been found.* (= which was used)
  - *Most of the goods **made in this factory** are exported.* (= which are made)

- ✅ **Past participles** in -ed clauses include irregular forms:
  - *stolen, made, built, written, broken, blown*

- ✅ Use **there is / there was** + -ing or -ed clauses:
  - *There were some children **swimming in the river**.*
  - *There was a big red car **parked outside**.*
  - *There are only a few **left**.* (= remaining)

- ✅ **Don't confuse -ing and -ed:**
  - **-ing** = active → the woman **talking** (she is talking)
  - **-ed** = passive → the boy **injured** (he was injured)
