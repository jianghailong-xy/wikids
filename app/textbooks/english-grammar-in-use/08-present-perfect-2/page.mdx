## A — Study this example situation

Dave and Jane are talking about travel. Jane is talking about the places she has visited **in her life** — a period that started when she was born and continues until **now**. Listen for the little verbs that bridge past experience to the present.

  title="Dave and Jane talk travel"
  participants={[
    { name: "Dave", emoji: "👨🏻", side: "left", tone: "sky" },
    { name: "Jane", emoji: "👩🏻", side: "right", tone: "rose" },
  ]}
  turns={[
    { speaker: "Dave", text: "Have you travelled a lot, Jane?" },
    { speaker: "Jane", text: "Yes, I've been to lots of places." },

    { speaker: "Jane", text: "Yes, I've been to China twice." },

    { speaker: "Jane", text: "No, I haven't been to India." },
  ]}

<Callout type="tip" title="The time bridge">
  The conversation is happening **now**, but Jane's travel experiences stretch back across her **whole life** — a period that continues from the past until now. When a time period is still open like this, we use the **present perfect** (have been / have travelled), not the past simple.
</Callout>

## When the time period continues until now

When we talk about a period of time that continues from the past until now, we use the present perfect. Here are more examples where the period is a person's whole life up to the present:

<SayIt text="Have you ever eaten caviar?" />
&nbsp;
<SayIt text="We've never had a car." />
<br />
<SayIt text="I don't know what the film is about. I haven't seen it." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Susan really loves that book. She's read it three times." />
<br />
<SayIt text="It's the most boring movie I've ever seen." />

<Callout type="info" title="been (to) = visited">
  <strong>I've been to</strong> a place = I have visited that place at some time in my life. <br />
  ✅ <em>I've never been to Canada. Have you been there?</em> <br />
  ✅ <em>My sister has been to Italy twice.</em> <br />
  ❌ <em>I've gone to Canada.</em> (gone = she's still there now; been = she went and came back)
</Callout>

<SayIt text="I've never been to Canada. Have you been there?" />

## B — Recently, in the last few days, so far, since ...

The same idea works for shorter periods — as long as the period is **still open** (not finished) at the time of speaking. Look at the time words:

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-2xl border-2 border-amber-200 bg-gradient-to-br from-amber-50 to-orange-50 p-5 shadow-sm">
  <p className="mb-3 text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-amber-700">Time periods that reach up to now</p>
  <div className="grid gap-2 sm:grid-cols-2">
    {["recently", "in the last few days", "so far", "since I arrived", "today", "this evening", "this year", "this morning"].map((w) => (
      <span key={w} className="rounded-lg border-2 border-amber-200 bg-white px-3 py-2 text-center text-sm font-semibold text-amber-800">{w}</span>
    ))}
  </div>
</div>

Listen — in every sentence, something has (or hasn't) happened inside a period that isn't over yet:

<SayIt text="Have you heard anything from Ben recently?" />
<br />
<SayIt text="I've met a lot of people in the last few days." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Everything is going well. There haven't been any problems so far." />
<br />
<SayIt text="The weather is bad here. It's rained every day since I arrived." />

<Callout type="tip" title="since = from a point in the past until now">
  <em>Since I arrived</em> = from the moment I arrived until now. The period starts at a specific point and reaches all the way to the present.
</Callout>

### today / this evening / this year (unfinished periods)

When the period is **not finished** at the time of speaking, we use the present perfect:

<SayIt text="I've drunk four cups of coffee today." />
<span className="ml-2 text-xs text-slate-500">(today is still going — I might drink more)</span>

<SayIt text="Have you had a holiday this year?" />
<br />
<SayIt text="I haven't seen Tom this morning. Have you?" />

<Callout type="warning" title="When the period closes, switch to past simple">
  ✅ <em>I haven't seen Tom <strong>this morning</strong>.</em> (it's still morning) <br />
  ✅ <em>I didn't see Tom <strong>this morning</strong>.</em> (it's now afternoon — the morning is over) <br />
  The same period word can take either tense — the choice depends on whether the period is still open or has closed.
</Callout>

## C — It's the first time something has happened

Here is a special pattern you'll hear all the time. Look at this situation:

<div className="not-prose my-6 rounded-2xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-gradient-to-br from-emerald-50 to-teal-50 p-5 text-center shadow-sm">
  <p className="text-lg">🚗</p>
  <p className="mt-2 text-sm text-slate-700">Don is having a driving lesson. It's his <strong>first</strong> lesson.</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-base font-semibold text-emerald-800">It's the first time he <strong>has driven</strong> a car.</p>
  <p className="mt-1 text-xs text-slate-500">(not: It's the first time he drives a car.)</p>
</div>

The pattern is always:

> **It's the + (first / second / third ...) time + subject + present perfect**

<SayIt text="It's the first time he has driven a car." />
<br />
<SayIt text="He hasn't driven a car before." />
<br />
<SayIt text="He has never driven a car before." />

More examples from the book:

<SayIt text="Sarah has lost her passport again. This is the second time this has happened." />
<br />
<SayIt text="Andy is phoning his girlfriend again. It's the third time he's phoned her this evening." />
<br />
<SayIt text="It's good to see you again. We haven't seen each other for a long time." />

<Callout type="info" title="Three ways to say the same thing">
  <em>It's the first time he has driven a car.</em> = <em>He hasn't driven a car before.</em> = <em>He has never driven a car before.</em> <br />
  All three point at the same idea — learn one, and you get the other two for free.
</Callout>

## Two tricky pairs: gone to vs been to

These two look similar but mean completely different things:

<div className="not-prose my-6 grid gap-3 sm:grid-cols-2">
  <div className="rounded-2xl border-2 border-rose-200 bg-gradient-to-br from-rose-50 to-pink-50 p-5 shadow-sm">
    <p className="mb-2 text-center text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-rose-700">has gone to ...</p>
    <p className="text-center text-base font-semibold text-rose-900">She <strong>has gone</strong> to Italy.</p>
    <p className="mt-2 text-center text-sm text-rose-800">= She is in Italy <strong>now</strong>. She went there and she hasn't come back.</p>
  </div>
  <div className="rounded-2xl border-2 border-emerald-200 bg-gradient-to-br from-emerald-50 to-teal-50 p-5 shadow-sm">
    <p className="mb-2 text-center text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-700">has been to ...</p>
    <p className="text-center text-base font-semibold text-emerald-900">She <strong>has been</strong> to Italy.</p>
    <p className="mt-2 text-center text-sm text-emerald-800">= At some time in her life she visited Italy. She went there <strong>and came back</strong>.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<SayIt text="She has gone to Italy. (= she's there now)" />
&nbsp;
<SayIt text="She has been to Italy twice. (= she visited twice in her life)" />

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't mix them up">
  ✅ <em>Have you ever <strong>been</strong> to Japan?</em> (you went and came back) <br />
  ✅ <em>Where's Tom? He's <strong>gone</strong> to the shops.</em> (he's there now) <br />
  ❌ <em>Have you ever <strong>gone</strong> to Japan?</em> (this makes it sound like you never came back!)
</Callout>
